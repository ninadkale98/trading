## The one-paragraph version

A company sells things (**revenue**). After paying to make them, what's left is
**gross profit**. After paying everything else, including salaries, research,
interest and taxes, what's left is **net income** (profit). Split that profit across
all shares and you get **EPS**. Every three months the company reports these
numbers (**earnings**) and says what it expects next (**guidance**). The stock price
moves mostly on whether the future looks **better or worse than people expected**,
not on whether the company is "good". **Valuation** asks how much you are paying
for each dollar of profit. **Technical indicators** help you choose *when* to buy.
**Risk management** keeps one bad surprise, like Intel's, from hurting you badly.
