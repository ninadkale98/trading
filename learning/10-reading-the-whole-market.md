# 10. Reading the Whole Market: Where Money Is Flowing

Individual stocks don't move alone. On most days a big part of any stock's move is just "the market" and "its
sector" moving. These are the lenses our system uses every day to understand the big picture.

## Breadth: is everyone rising, or just a few giants?
**Breadth** counts how many stocks are taking part in a move.
- **Healthy:** SPY is up *and* most stocks are above their 200-day average, with many new 52-week highs.
- **Narrow (fragile):** SPY is up only because a handful of giant companies are up, while most stocks fall.
- Quick check: **RSP** (the S&P 500 with every company weighted equally) vs **SPY** (big companies count more).
  If SPY beats RSP for months, the rally is narrow.

## Rotation: money moves between groups
Big investors keep shifting money between sectors and themes (chips → software, tech → energy, and so on).
**Relative strength** = how a group is doing *compared with SPY*. A rotation map puts each group in one of four boxes:

| Box | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Leading** | Beating SPY, and the lead is growing |
| **Weakening** | Still ahead, but the lead is shrinking (money starting to leave) |
| **Lagging** | Behind SPY and still falling behind |
| **Improving** | Behind, but catching up (money starting to arrive) |

Groups often travel around the boxes in that order, which is why "improving" is interesting to watch.

## Style: what kind of stock does the market want?
- **Growth vs value:** fast-growing companies (often tech) vs cheap, steady ones (banks, energy).
- **Small vs large:** IWM (small companies) vs SPY.
- **Defensive vs cyclical:** utilities and food (people buy them in any economy) vs tech, travel, cars (depend on good times).
  Money moving into defensives often means investors are getting nervous.

## Risk-on / risk-off and other markets
- **Risk-on:** investors are confident: stocks, small caps and bitcoin rise; "safe" assets lag.
- **Risk-off:** fear: bonds, gold and the dollar rise; stocks fall; **credit spreads** widen (risky company bonds, HYG,
  fall versus safe government bonds, IEF).
- **Interest rates** matter for everything: higher rates make future profits worth less today, which hurts fast-growing,
  expensive stocks the most.
- **Global:** if Europe, Japan and China all fall with the US, it's a world story; if only the US falls, look for a US cause.

## Stories vs prices
"AI needs chips" was true in 2026, but AMD rose +101% from Jan 2 to Nov 6 while NVDA was −1%. A story can be right
and still not tell you **which stock** or **when**, and stories everyone knows are often **already priced in**.
Our system learns the story, then waits for it to show up in the numbers (revenue speeding up, guidance raised,
the stock starting to beat its sector).

## Two traps when looking back
- **Hindsight bias:** after a stock goes up, the reason always looks obvious. Before, it rarely was.
- **Base rate:** before saying "this pattern predicts winners", check how often stocks *without* the pattern also
  went up. If 6% of all stocks rise 20%+ in three months and 8% of stocks with the pattern do, the pattern barely helps.
