# 6. Analysts: Ratings and Price Targets

## Who are analysts?

People at banks and research firms (Bank of America, UBS, Morgan Stanley and so on)
who study companies full-time. They publish **ratings** and **price targets** for
their clients, and these are widely reported.

## Ratings

| Rating | Other names | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy** | Outperform, Overweight | Expect it to do better than the market |
| **Hold** | Neutral, Market Perform, Equal Weight | Expect it to do about as well as the market |
| **Sell** | Underperform, Underweight | Expect it to do worse |

**Note:** "Sell" ratings are rare because banks also want companies as clients. In
practice, **"Hold" is often a polite "not now"**.

**Intel (Robinhood, Nov 2026):** 18 Buy, **30 Hold**, 1 Sell. Most analysts are
cautious.

## Price target

**What:** Where the analyst thinks the stock will trade in about **12 months**.

**Intel:**

| | Price target |
|---|---|
| Highest | $160 |
| **Average** | **$91.81** |
| Lowest | $63 |
| Current price | $56.81 |

## Upgrade and downgrade

- **Upgrade:** rating raised (Hold → Buy). Often pushes the stock up that day.
- **Downgrade:** rating cut (Buy → Hold). Often pushes it down.
- **Price target cut or raise:** the target number changes; the rating may stay the same.

**After Intel's earnings:** Bank of America, Roth and Seaport downgraded **Buy → Neutral**.
UBS cut its target from **$100 → $80**. That added selling pressure on top of the
earnings shock.

## Limits of analysts (important)

1. **Targets lag.** Analysts update *after* the price moves. Intel's average target of
   $91.81 still includes old, pre-crash targets. Expect it to keep falling.
2. **Herding.** Analysts tend to move together.
3. **Conflicts of interest.** Banks want business from the companies they cover.
4. **Track record.** Price targets are often wrong. Treat them as one opinion, not a forecast.

## How to use analyst data

- **Changes matter more than levels.** A wave of upgrades or downgrades tells you
  sentiment is shifting.
- **The spread tells you how uncertain things are.** Intel's targets range from $63 to
  $160, so even the experts disagree a lot.
- **Use it as context, never as the only reason to buy.**
