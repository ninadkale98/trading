### Quick terms
- **Excess vs SPY:** how much a trade beat (or trailed) just holding the S&P 500 ETF over the same days.
- **t-stat:** roughly, how many "noise units" the result sits away from zero. Below about 2 it is weak evidence. **The bar also goes up with every test we run:** after 21 backtests the lab needs t ≥ 2.47 to count a result as "beats SPY." With that many tries, some results will look good by luck alone.
- **Random twin:** the same entry and exit rules applied to randomly picked stocks on the same days. If we can't beat the twin, the stock selection adds nothing. The gains came from timing or from the market as a whole.
- **Signal study vs pool:** how stocks flagged by the idea did over the next 5/20/60 trading days, compared with all eligible stocks. **t_adj** is the same t-stat corrected for many signals landing on the same days, because those signals aren't independent.
- **Trailing stop:** sell if the price falls X% from its highest close since we bought.

## 1. sue_drift_no_momentum: earnings-beat drift without the price-strength filters (LIVE)

**What it tested:** Our live strategy `sue_drift_loose` buys stocks a few days after they beat earnings estimates and the price reacted well. It also requires the stock to already be strong: high relative-strength rank, near its 52-week high, and above its 50-day average. The question was whether the earnings surprise drives the edge, or whether those momentum filters do. This variant drops the momentum filters and keeps everything else.

**Results (2016–2022 backtest):**
- 252 trades, average **+1.52% vs SPY per trade (t 2.01)**, win rate 56%, average hold 48 days, worst drawdown −14%.
- Beats the random twin by +2.13%/trade (t 2.43). This is the strongest part of the evidence: picking these particular stocks mattered.
- Beat SPY in 6 of 7 years. Without the best year (2020, +4.6%) the edge is still +1.06%.
- Signal study: weak at 20 days (+0.58% vs pool, t_adj 0.69), strong at 60 days (+3.59% vs pool, t_adj 2.77; +3.23% vs SPY).
- It failed the formal "beats SPY" gate: t 2.01 against a required 2.40.

**Its own kill tests:** (a) excess ≥ +1.5%: **+1.52%, passed by a hair**. (b) twin t ≥ 1.0: passed. (c) positive without best year: passed. (d) 60-day study > 0: passed.

**Decision and why:** Admitted on "sign of life." I agree. It survived tests it set for itself before seeing the data, and the twin comparison is solid.

**What we learned:**
- Without the momentum filters the edge drops from +3.56% to +1.52%. **The momentum filters account for more than half of the parent's edge.** The surprise alone still carries some, but less.
- The drift is slow: almost nothing at 20 days, clear at 60 days. That matches the story of analysts and funds adjusting over weeks.
- **2022 warning:** it beat SPY (+0.9%), but the average trade *lost* 3.5% and only 34% of trades won. Beating the market isn't the same as making money. That matters for our goal of real profit.
- **Overlap risk:** this is a looser version of a strategy that's already live. Many of its trades will be the same stocks as `sue_drift_loose`, so in practice we may be making one bet twice.

## 2. rev_accel_late_drift: buy 15–35 days after a report showing faster sales growth (LIVE, but should not be)

**What it tested:** Whether companies whose latest quarter grew faster than their past year keep drifting up for 1–3 months, with entry delayed to skip the first-day spike.

**Results:**
- 523 trades, average **+0.01% vs SPY (t 0.02)**. That is zero.
- Random twin: +0.64% (t 0.86). Not distinguishable from random stock picking.
- Beat SPY in only 3 of 7 years. One year (2020, +6.9% on 63 trades) carries everything. **Without 2020 the average is about −0.93% per trade.**
- Signal study: negative at 5 and 20 days. Positive only at 60 days (+2.01% vs pool, t_adj 2.17; vs SPY +0.98%, t 1.08).
- Failed 3 of 4 gates.

**Its own kill tests:** (a) twin t ≥ 1.5: **failed (0.86)**. (b) excess > 0: technically passed (+0.01%). (c) positive without best year: **failed (≈ −0.93%)**. (d) 60-day study: passed.

**Decision and why:** The lab admitted it because the 60-day signal study showed "sign of life." **That's a process error.** The idea said in advance that it should be considered disproven if (a) *or* (c) happened, and both did. The admission rule doesn't read the pre-registered kill tests.

**What we learned (important):** The 60-day signal study measures a holding period this strategy can't actually hold. Entry is 15–35 days after earnings, and the next report comes about 63 trading days after the last one. The rule "sell when the next earnings is ≤ 3 days away" therefore cuts most trades short: 256 of 523 exits were that signal exit, and the average hold was 39 days. The 60-day window likely includes the *next* earnings jump, which the strategy deliberately avoids. So the one positive number came from a period the strategy never trades. **Recommendation: retire it, or at least flag it for review.**

## 3. insider_confirms_beat: insider buys right after a good earnings reaction (REJECTED)

**What it tested:** Does an insider buying with their own money *after* the stock already jumped strengthen post-earnings drift? This is the mirror image of our live `insider_buys_the_drop`.

**Results:**
- The signal study found only **11 signals on 3 days**, and the lab rejected it as "too rare."
- **But the backtest made 497 trades.** The two can't both be right. One of the two code paths is computing the rules differently. The likely culprit is the "new insider buyer vs 5 days earlier" lag, or insider data missing from the signal-study data. (The 60-day t_adj of 8.77 from 3 days of data shows how meaningless tiny samples are.)
- Taking the backtest at face value: +0.71% vs SPY (t 0.94) and +0.17% vs twin (t 0.19). That's no better than random picks. It's also below insider_buys_the_drop's +1.87% (t 1.43), so it fails its own test (d) as well.

**Decision:** Rejection is correct either way, because the backtest also fails. But the stated reason ("too rare") is probably a bug, not a fact.

**What we learned:** With the caveat that the bug muddies this, insider buying after a pop added nothing. Insider buying seems useful as a "someone believes in this dip" signal, not as confirmation of good news.

## 4. cheap_beat_drift: good earnings news at cheap, cash-generating companies (REJECTED)

**What it tested:** Whether value stocks (low price-to-earnings or high free-cash-flow yield) drift up for longer after a strong earnings reaction, because investors are slow to believe good news about companies they've written off.

**Results:**
- Signal study: **0 signals**. Backtest: **538 trades**. Same mismatch as above, even more extreme. Probably one of the valuation fields (pe, fcf_yield, ps, debt_to_equity with its "or blank" rule) is missing or blank in the signal-study data.
- Backtest: **−0.18% vs SPY (t −0.35)**, −0.12% vs twin (t −0.21). Beat SPY in 3 of 7 years. Only 2016 (21 trades, +6.9%) looked good.

**Decision:** Rejection is correct on the backtest evidence (fails kill tests a, b and c). The stated reason is a bug.

**What we learned:** Within our 2016–2022 data, "cheap + good earnings reaction" showed no edge at all. This is a clean negative result. Don't spend more rounds on small variations of it.

## Overall honesty check
- **Multiple testing:** we've now run 21 backtests, and **none has cleared the "beats SPY" bar (t ≥ 2.47)**. Both admissions this round were "sign of life" admissions, which are a weaker standard.
- **No out-of-sample check yet:** `validate` is empty for every idea. Everything here comes from 2016–2022. The admitted strategies need to show they hold up on 2023+ data or in live dry-run results before we trust them.
- **Crowding:** 14 strategies are live now, and 5–6 of them are earnings-drift variations (sue_drift_loose, sue_drift_no_momentum, surprise_near_high, rs_earnings_confirmed, earnings_gap_up, rev_accel_late_drift). They'll often pick the same stocks at the same time, so a bad earnings season would hit all of them together.

## Action items for the lab code (not trading decisions)
1. Make the admit rule check each idea's own "PROVEN WRONG" conditions. Under that rule `rev_accel_late_drift` would have been rejected.
2. Add a sanity check that stops a round when the signal-study signal count and the backtest trade count disagree wildly (11 vs 497, 0 vs 538).
3. Cap the signal-study horizon at the strategy's real maximum hold, including the exit before the next earnings.


## Lessons
- **The admit rule ignores each idea's pre-registered PROVEN WRONG conditions. A 60-day 'sign of life' can admit an idea that disproved itself.** Evidence: rev_accel_late_drift was admitted although kill test (a) twin t 0.86 < 1.5 and (c) excess without best year ≈ −0.93% (2020 carried all of it) both triggered. Backtest excess was +0.01% (t 0.02).. Test: Change lab.cycle to evaluate the idea's falsification clauses before admitting, then re-run the round-3 decisions. Expect rev_accel_late_drift → reject and sue_drift_no_momentum → still admit (it passes a–d, with a at +1.52% vs 1.5%).
- **The signal study and backtest are not applying the same rules for some features. Signal counts and trade counts can disagree by orders of magnitude.** Evidence: insider_confirms_beat: 11 signals on 3 days vs 497 backtest trades. cheap_beat_drift: 0 signals vs 538 trades. Both were rejected as 'too rare', but the backtests show plenty of trades.. Test: Add an assertion: backtest trades must be ≤ ~1.2× flagged signals and flagged signals ≥ 0.3× trades, otherwise mark the idea build_error. Check the 5-day lag of insider_buyers_90d and the pe/fcf_yield/ps/debt_to_equity coverage in the signal-study panel. Re-run both ideas after the fix (expect both still rejected on twin t < 1.5).
- **The 60-day signal-study horizon can extend past the next earnings report. Strategies that exit before earnings can't capture that return, so it gives false signs of life for late-entry ideas.** Evidence: rev_accel_late_drift enters 15–35 days after a report and sells ≤3 days before the next one (256/523 signal exits, avg hold 39 days). The 60d study shows +2.01% vs pool (t_adj 2.17) but the backtest shows +0.01% vs SPY.. Test: Recompute the signal study with forward returns truncated at min(horizon, days_to_earnings_est − 3) for every live earnings strategy. Expect rev_accel's 60d edge to fall to ≈0 and sue_drift_no_momentum's to shrink but stay positive.
- **The momentum filters (RS rank, near 52w high, above 50d SMA) carry more than half of sue_drift_loose's edge. The earnings surprise alone keeps roughly 40%.** Evidence: Removing the filters cut excess from +3.56% to +1.52%/trade (t 2.01). Twin t 2.43, 6/7 years positive, +1.06% without the best year.. Test: Run the complement: the momentum filters plus a positive earnings_reaction but no eps_surprise_px requirement. If it gets ≥ +2.5%/trade, the momentum filters are the main driver and the surprise is secondary.
- **Post-earnings drift from a surprise is slow: it barely shows at 20 days and is clear at 60 days. Shorter holds would likely give up most of the edge.** Evidence: sue_drift_no_momentum signal study: 20d +0.58% vs pool (t_adj 0.69), 60d +3.59% (t_adj 2.77). 168 of 252 exits were time exits at 55 days.. Test: Run a variant with max hold 30 days and everything else equal. Expect excess vs SPY to drop below +0.8%/trade.
- **Beating SPY in a bear year still lost money. Earnings-drift strategies have no protection against a market-wide fall.** Evidence: sue_drift_no_momentum in 2022: +0.9% vs SPY but −3.5% average absolute return, 34% win rate, 65 trades (its busiest year).. Test: Add a market filter (only enter when SPY is above its 200-day average) and compare absolute return and max drawdown. Expect 2022 trades to fall sharply and drawdown to drop below −14%, with little change in other years.
- **Cheap valuation + strong earnings reaction showed no edge in 2016–2022. Don't spend more rounds on value-flavoured earnings drift variants.** Evidence: cheap_beat_drift: 538 trades, −0.18% vs SPY (t −0.35), twin t −0.21, positive in 3/7 years (only 2016 meaningfully).. Test: Only revisit with a different mechanism (e.g. value plus an analyst upgrade). Otherwise log a lab rule: any proposal whose core filter is pe/fcf_yield + earnings_reaction must cite this result and explain what is different.
- **Insider buying after a price pop adds nothing over random picks. Insider buying seems useful as a dip marker, not as confirmation of good news.** Evidence: insider_confirms_beat backtest: +0.71% vs SPY (t 0.94), +0.17% vs twin (t 0.19), below insider_buys_the_drop's +1.87%. The result is tentative until the signal-count bug is fixed.. Test: After fixing the lag-feature bug, re-run it. If twin t stays < 1.0, close the 'insider confirms' direction for good.
- **Live earnings-drift strategies overlap heavily, so admitting more variants adds correlated bets, not new evidence.** Evidence: 5–6 of 14 live strategies are earnings-drift variants. sue_drift_no_momentum is a strict loosening of the live sue_drift_loose.. Test: Measure the share of sue_drift_no_momentum backtest trades (same symbol, entry within 5 days) that sue_drift_loose also takes, and the correlation of their daily sleeve returns. If overlap > 50% or correlation > 0.7, have the portfolio treat them as one sleeve for sizing.
- **After 21 backtests, none has cleared the multiple-testing-adjusted 'beats SPY' bar, and no admitted idea has an out-of-sample check.** Evidence: The required t rose from 2.40 to 2.47 this round. The best result was t 2.01. validate = null for all four ideas.. Test: Run every 'sign of life' admission on 2023–2026 data (or require 6 months of dry-run results). Retire any whose out-of-sample excess vs SPY is ≤ 0.
