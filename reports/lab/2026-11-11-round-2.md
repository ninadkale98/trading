# Idea lab round 2 (2026-11-11)

# Lab round 2: report

## Summary

Four ideas were tested on 2016–2022 data. **All four were admitted** to live simulated trading (dry run/paper, no real money). The lab now runs **12 live strategies**, and none were retired this round.

That headline is better than the evidence. Only **one idea (`sue_drift_loose`) looks promising**, and even it is not proven. **Two ideas failed the "proven wrong" tests we wrote down before running them**, but they went live anyway. The automatic admission rule only needs a "sign of life", and it ignores those pre-written tests. This is the main issue to fix before round 3.

### Terms used below
- **Excess return**: how much a trade beat a comparison, such as SPY (the S&P 500 index fund).
- **Random twin**: the same number of trades on randomly chosen stocks from the same eligible group, with the same timing and exits. If we can't beat the twin, our selection rule adds nothing.
- **Pool**: the average stock in the universe over the same window.
- **t-stat**: how many "noise widths" a result is away from zero. Below about 2 is weak evidence. Because we have now run 17 backtests, the bar for beating SPY has risen to **t ≥ 2.38**. With more tries, a lucky result becomes more likely, so the bar goes up.
- **t_adj (overlap-adjusted t)**: a corrected t-stat. When the same stocks are flagged day after day, the trades are not independent, and the raw t-stat overstates the evidence. t_adj corrects for that.
- **Signal study**: what happened N days after a flag, with no stops. **Backtest**: actual simulated trades with the stops and exits.
- **Max drawdown**: the worst peak-to-bottom fall of the strategy's account.

## 1. `big_crash_capitulation`: buy big, steady stocks after a 25%+ crash, hold 6 months
**What it tested:** whether forced selling in large, heavily traded stocks overshoots and then recovers over about 120 trading days.

**Results:**
- **Backtest:** 57 trades. **−3.59% per trade vs SPY (t −1.20)** and **−1.15% vs twin (t −0.30)**.
- **Signal study:** +1.40% vs pool at 20 days (t_adj 0.97), then **−3.17% vs pool at 60 days**.
- **By year:** 2020's +29% average raw return was mostly the market bounce; the excess was only +2.6%. 2022 had 20 trades at −9.2% excess each.

**Decision:** went live on the 20-day "sign of life". **That decision is not justified.** The idea broke three of its own four pre-written kill rules:
- (a) twin excess ≤ 0
- (b) it is already negative before removing the best year
- (c) only 3 years with positive excess, where 4 were needed

Rule (d), the 2023+ out-of-sample check, was never run. A t-stat of 0.97 at 20 days is noise.

**What we learned:** the earlier event study (+9.3% vs SPY over 120 days) did not hold up in the real backtest. That study tried 12 variants, so it was probably a lucky pick. Crash buying in this setup mostly took on market risk without adding an edge. **Recommendation: retire at the next cycle.**

## 2. `sue_drift_loose`: earnings beat + strong price reaction near highs (looser version of `surprise_near_high`)
**What it tested:** "post-earnings drift", the tendency of a stock to keep moving for weeks after a real earnings beat. The question was whether the parent's edge (+2.18% per trade on 50 trades) survives looser filters.

**Results:**
- **Backtest:** 88 trades. **+3.56% per trade vs SPY (t 2.44)**, the only idea above the multiple-testing bar (2.33 at the time).
- **Robustness:** +2.25% per trade without its best year. Positive in all 5 years with enough trades (2021, with 9 trades, was −2.3%).
- **Signal study:** **+5.96% vs pool at 60 days (t_adj 3.39)**.
- **Risk:** max drawdown only −6.2%.
- **But:** vs twin it made +2.99% at **t 1.62**, below 2. It also had 88 trades, just under its own 90-trade minimum.

**Decision:** live. This is reasonable, but by its own pre-written rules it is **not yet a pass**: it fails the twin t-stat and is 2 trades short. The "score top third vs bottom third" check was never reported.

**What we learned:** loosening the filters *kept* the edge instead of destroying it. That is evidence against the parent's result being just a product of tight filters. Two cautions:
- Much of the evidence overlaps with the parent: same idea, same years, and many of the same trades.
- The lab has looked at this data several times, so the effective bar is above 2.38.

The parent and this variant are now both live and will often buy the same stocks. Watch for doubled-up positions.

## 3. `rs_earnings_confirmed`: momentum leaders with improving earnings and sales (same exits as `relative_strength`)
**What it tested:** whether adding a "business is improving" filter makes momentum leaders better. The comparison is the parent's +1.14% per trade vs twin.

**Results:**
- **Backtest:** 786 trades. **−0.12% per trade vs SPY (t −0.21)** and **+0.37% vs twin (t 0.64)**, which is worse than the parent.
- **Exits:** win rate 35%. **515 of 786 trades hit the stop.** The average hold was 28 days, even though up to 120 were allowed.
- **Risk:** max drawdown −40%, and the sleeve lagged SPY by 44% overall.
- **Signal study:** at 60 days, +1.11% vs pool with a raw **t of 4.04**. But it fired 28,728 times on only 1,437 days, so after the overlap correction **t_adj is 0.91**. It was also only +0.13% vs SPY.

**Decision:** live. **Not justified.** It broke its pre-written kill rule (twin excess +0.37%, below the parent's +1.14%, and t well below 2). The "excluded parent trades" comparison group was never reported.

**What we learned:**
1. The earnings filter did not improve momentum here.
2. The tight 2.5×ATR stop dominates the outcome. (ATR, average true range, is a measure of a stock's normal daily price swing.) Most trades are shaken out long before any 60-day edge could show up.
3. A raw t of 4 can shrink to below 1 once you account for the same stocks being flagged every day.
4. "Beats the pool" is not "beats SPY": in 2016–2022 the average stock lagged the big-cap index.

**Recommendation:** retire, or re-test the same entries with a looser exit.

## 4. `insider_buys_the_drop`: an insider buys within weeks after a bad earnings reaction
**What it tested:** whether insider buying right after the market punishes a report means the drop overshot.

**Results:**
- **Backtest:** 421 trades. **+1.87% per trade vs SPY (t 1.43)** and **+1.38% vs twin (t 0.98)**. It stays positive (+0.56%) without its best year.
- **Signal study:** the best reading is **+0.92% vs pool at 20 days (t_adj 2.18)**. At 60 days it is +1.06% (t_adj 1.69).
- **By year:** positive in 4 of 7 years (57%, below the 60% gate). The best years, 2016 and 2020, were both market-rebound years.
- **Risk:** max drawdown −35%.

**Decision:** live. Against its own rules it passes two (it beats `insider_cluster`'s −0.43% and survives removing the best year) and **fails the main one (twin t < 2)**. The pre-written split by reaction size was not reported.

**What we learned:** combining two signals that each failed alone gives a small positive result with weak evidence. It may mostly be "bought a beaten-down stock before a rebound" rather than insider information. It's worth watching, but it's a long way from proven.

## The process problem behind this round
- **Admission ignores the pre-written tests.** Every idea came with written "proven wrong if…" rules. Two ideas clearly broke theirs and still went live, because one 20-day t-stat near 1 counted as a "sign of life".
- **Several pre-registered diagnostics were never computed:** the 2023+ out-of-sample check, score terciles, the excluded-trades group, and the reaction-size split. `validate` was empty for all four ideas. Ideas are being written with tests the pipeline doesn't run.
- **The live list keeps growing** (12 strategies), and three pairs overlap: parent and variant both live. The simulated book can end up holding the same stocks twice, which makes the live results harder to read.
- **Scoreboard:** across all four ideas, only `sue_drift_loose` clears the multiple-testing bar vs SPY, and none clears t ≥ 2 vs its random twin.


## Lessons
- **Pre-registered kill rules must be enforced at admission. A 20-day signal-study t_adj near 1 should not override a written 'PROVEN WRONG' rule that has been met.** Evidence: big_crash_capitulation met its kill rules (a), (b) and (c): twin excess -1.15% (t -0.30), SPY excess -3.59%, only 3 positive years. rs_earnings_confirmed met its rule: twin excess +0.37% vs the parent's +1.14%, t 0.64. Both were admitted on 'sign of life' (20d t_adj 0.97 and 0.66).. Test: Add a gate in lab.cycle: admission requires none of the machine-checkable pre-registered kill rules to be met (twin t, leave-best-year-out sign, minimum trades). Re-run round 2 admission under it; expected result is big_crash_capitulation and rs_earnings_confirmed rejected.
- **Crash-buying big steady stocks did not reproduce the earlier event study. The raw gains were market beta, not an edge.** Evidence: The pre-test event study showed +9.3% vs SPY at 120d over 12 variants. The backtest showed -3.59%/trade vs SPY on 57 trades. 2020 avg raw return was +29% but excess only +2.6%. 2022 had 20 trades at -9.2% excess. The 60d signal study was -3.17% vs pool.. Test: Run the signal study at 120d (the actual hold) and on 2023+ data. If excess vs SPY is <= 0 in either, retire big_crash_capitulation and record that the 12-variant event study was overfit.
- **Loosening the earnings-drift filters kept the edge, so surprise_near_high's result is less likely to be filter-fitting. It still lacks a t >= 2 win over its random twin.** Evidence: sue_drift_loose: 88 trades, +3.56%/trade vs SPY (t 2.44, above the 2.33 multiple-testing bar), +2.25% without its best year, 60d signal +5.96% vs pool (t_adj 3.39), positive in 5/5 qualifying years, max drawdown -6.2%. But vs twin: +2.99%, t 1.62.. Test: Report the pre-registered score top-third vs bottom-third, run 2023+ out-of-sample, and measure what share of its trades overlap with surprise_near_high. Keep it only if 2023+ excess vs SPY > 0 and the top third beats the bottom third.
- **For relative_strength-style entries, the tight 2.5×ATR stop decides the outcome. Most trades are stopped out long before the 60-day horizon where the signal shows up.** Evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed: 515 of 786 exits were stops (+49 stop_gap), win rate 35%, avg hold 28 days of 120 allowed, max drawdown -40%. Its 60d signal study (no stops) showed +1.11% vs pool.. Test: Re-run the same entries with only a 20% closing stop (no ATR stop or trend-break exit) and a 60-day time exit. Compare twin excess and t with the current exits; if the looser exit is better, test the same change on the parent relative_strength.
- **Raw t-stats are badly inflated for signals that fire on many stocks the same day. Only the overlap-adjusted t should be quoted.** Evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed 60d vs pool: raw t 4.04 but t_adj 0.91 (28,728 signals on only 1,437 days). The 20d raw t was 2.09 vs t_adj 0.66.. Test: In every future signal study, flag any idea where signals/signal_days > 5 and require t_adj (not raw t) >= 2 for any claim. Check that round-3 reports never cite raw t for such ideas.
- **Beating the pool is not beating SPY. In 2016–2022 the average universe stock lagged the large-cap index, so a 'vs pool' edge can disappear against SPY.** Evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed 60d: +1.11% vs pool but only +0.13% vs SPY. big_crash_capitulation 20d: +1.40% vs pool but -0.10% vs SPY.. Test: Make admission's 'sign of life' require positive excess vs both pool and SPY at the same horizon. Count how many round 1–2 admissions would have been blocked.
- **Insider buying after a bad earnings reaction shows a small positive effect, but it rests mostly on rebound years and may just be generic dip-buying.** Evidence: insider_buys_the_drop: 421 trades, +1.87%/trade vs SPY (t 1.43), +1.38% vs twin (t 0.98), +0.56% without best year, positive in 4/7 years with best years 2016 and 2020 (rebounds). 20d signal t_adj 2.18. Max drawdown -35%.. Test: Report the pre-registered split by reaction size (<= -10% vs -5% to -10%), and run a control with the same earnings-drop trigger but no insider condition. Keep the insider condition only if it beats the control vs twin with t >= 2.
- **The pipeline does not compute idea-specific pre-registered diagnostics, so ideas are written with kill rules that never get checked.** Evidence: None of these were reported: the 2023+ out-of-sample check (big_crash rule d), score terciles (sue_drift_loose), the excluded-trades comparison (rs_earnings_confirmed), the reaction-size split (insider_buys_the_drop). validate was null for all four ideas.. Test: Either extend the backtest output with a standard set (2023+ out-of-sample, score terciles, leave-best-year-out vs twin), or have the idea writer only use kill rules built from fields the pipeline reports. Round 3 check: every kill rule has a reported value.
- **Running a parent and its variant live together doubles exposure to the same trades and blurs the live comparison.** Evidence: Live now: surprise_near_high + sue_drift_loose, relative_strength + rs_earnings_confirmed, insider_cluster + insider_buys_the_drop. 12 live strategies, 0 retired this round.. Test: Measure the share of same-stock, same-week entries between each parent/variant pair in the dry-run book after 4 weeks. If overlap > 30%, keep only the pair member with the higher twin t.
