# Lab Round 4 Report

## The short version

We tested four ideas. Three of them are now **live**, which here means simulated trading in dry run or paper, not real money. One was rejected. **None of the four cleared the main bar of beating SPY with a strong enough t-stat.** The three were admitted on weaker "sign of life" rules.

Two of the three admitted ideas also failed the "PROVEN WRONG" tests they set for themselves before the backtest ran. We should be honest about that. They are live because the lab's automatic admission rule is looser than their own pre-registered tests, not because they proved themselves. Only **sue_drift_market_gate** looks like a real improvement, and even its evidence is still weak.

The bar keeps rising as we test more ideas. We have now run 25 backtests, so a result needs a t-stat of about **2.54** before we treat it as more than luck. Each extra test raises it a little.

### Terms used below
- **Excess vs SPY**: how much a trade beat (or lagged) simply holding the S&P 500 index fund over the same days.
- **t-stat**: a rough measure of how confident we can be that a result isn't luck. Below about 2 is weak. With many tests, we need more (2.5+ here).
- **Twin**: for each real trade, the backtester picks a random comparison stock that looks similar on basic traits. Beating the twin means the rules pick better than chance *within that kind of stock*.
- **Signal study**: before the full backtest, a quick check of how flagged stocks did 5, 20 and 60 trading days later vs SPY and vs the "pool" (all similar candidates). `t_adj` is the t-stat corrected for many signals landing on the same days.
- **Max drawdown**: the worst peak-to-bottom drop of the simulated sleeve.
- **Trailing stop**: a stop-loss that moves up as the price rises, here 20% below the highest close.
- **Post-earnings drift**: stocks that report good news tend to keep drifting up for weeks, because investors adjust slowly.

## 1. react_drift_momentum: does a good earnings reaction plus momentum work without the EPS-surprise data?

**What it tested.** The parent idea, sue_drift_loose, buys after a positive earnings surprise. The surprise data comes from Alpha Vantage and is sparse. An earlier finding was that the momentum filters (near the 52-week high, strong relative strength) seemed to carry most of the edge. If that were true, we could drop the surprise data and get far more history and trades. Entry and exits match the parent: entry 2–10 days after the report, wide 20% stops and a 20% trailing stop, a 55-day max hold, and a sale before the next report.

**Result.**
- 576 trades, **+0.67% per trade vs SPY (t 1.15)**, win rate 53%.
- Without its best year (2020, +4.1%), the excess is **+0.01%**, which is effectively zero.
- By year: 2017 +2.1%, 2018 +2.3%, 2019 −0.4%, 2020 +4.1%, 2021 **−3.3%**, 2022 +0.1%.
- It beat its random twin by +1.60% (t 2.40). That is the only gate it passed.
- The signal study was *negative* vs SPY at every horizon (−0.19% at 60 days). It was slightly positive vs the pool at 60 days (+1.04%, t_adj 1.62).

**Decision: admitted by the lab's rule, but by its own pre-registered test it is PROVEN WRONG.** The hypothesis said it was wrong if excess was below +1.0% (it was +0.67%) or if excess without the best year was at or below +0.3% (it was +0.01%). Two of its three kill conditions were met.

**What we learned.**
- **The surprise data seems to matter.** The pre-registered conclusion applies: excess is below +1.0% while sue_drift_loose sits around +3.5%, so momentum plus a good reaction is *not* enough by itself. One caveat: sue_drift_loose is tested on a smaller, sparser sample, so part of its +3.5% may be luck. The clean test is to run both on the same stocks and dates.
- **"Beats the twin but not SPY" is a warning sign.** The twin beat lagged SPY by about 0.9% per trade (+1.60% vs the twin but only +0.67% vs SPY). In 2017–2022 the typical stock in this pool trailed the index, which was carried by a few giant companies. Picking well within a lagging group still left us roughly level with buying SPY.
- **On the side question about earnings_gap_up:** that idea used tight exits and lost −0.47%. This one used wide exits and made +0.67%. That hints the wide exits help, but the entry filters also differ, so this does not cleanly answer the question.
- About 30% of trades were stopped out (174 of 576) even with 20% stops. These stocks are volatile.

## 2. sue_drift_market_gate: the earnings-surprise drift, only when the market itself is trending up

**What it tested.** The parent, sue_drift_no_momentum, beat SPY in 2022 but still *lost money* in absolute terms: −3.5% per trade over 65 trades, its busiest year. The idea is that in bear markets, broad forced selling drowns out the slow buying that drives post-earnings drift. So this version only opens new positions when SPY's 6-month return is positive and its 3-month return is above −5%.

**Result.**
- 191 trades, **+1.43% per trade vs SPY (t 1.64)**. Without its best year it is still **+1.16%**, so the result does not depend on one lucky year.
- Average absolute return per trade was +3.27%, the win rate 59%, and **max drawdown only −12.1%**, the smallest of the round.
- The gate cut 2022 entries from 65 to **10**, about 85% fewer. That passes the hypothesis's "is the proxy too slow?" check, which needed at least 40%.
- The signal study had the strongest number of the round: **at 60 days, +3.24% vs pool (t_adj 2.30) and +2.55% vs SPY (t 2.38)**. At 5 and 20 days it was near zero. The edge builds slowly over weeks 4–12, which fits the drift story.
- Twin: +1.61% (t 1.53), short of the t ≥ 2 gate.

**Decision: admitted as a sign of life.** Excess is above the +1.0% floor. We can't fully check the other two kill conditions (absolute return per trade up at least 0.5 points and drawdown at least 3 points better than the parent) because this round's data doesn't include the parent's overall numbers. That comparison needs to be pulled.

**What we learned, and the caveats.**
- The gate does what it was built to do: it avoids most bear-market entries, and the edge holds up outside the best year.
- **Hindsight warning.** The idea came from looking at 2022, so "it fixed 2022" is partly built in. The 10 trades it still took in 2022 averaged −3.4%, about the same as the parent's −3.5%. The gate worked by *taking fewer trades* in that year, not by picking better ones.
- **It did not catch 2018.** That year shows −1.9% absolute over 35 trades. A 6-month trend filter is slow, and the late-2018 selloff came after a strong first nine months, so the gate stayed on. Sudden drops will get through.

## 3. insider_drop_intact: insider buying after a bad earnings reaction, only at healthy companies

**What it tested.** The parent, insider_buys_the_drop, buys when an insider buys shortly after the stock fell at least 5% on its report. It earned +1.87% per trade. The new idea added a health filter: revenue still growing, an operating profit, and gross margin of at least 20%. The theory was that an insider buying a healthy business that the market over-punished is the strongest version of the signal.

**Result.**
- 189 trades, **+0.45% per trade vs SPY (t 0.32)**, far *below* the parent's +1.87%.
- Twin: +1.31% (t 0.74).
- 2022 was its busiest *and* worst year: 42 trades, −7.0% absolute, −3.1% vs SPY. Without 2022 the excess would be about +1.5% per trade, which is still below the parent.
- The signal study showed something at 20 days (+1.37% vs pool, t_adj 1.84). This is what got it admitted.

**Decision: admitted by the lab's rule, but by its own pre-registered test it is PROVEN WRONG.** It failed two kill conditions: excess at or below the parent's +1.87%, and t vs twin below 1.5. The requested split comparing the trades the filter *removed* with the ones it *kept* was not produced.

**What we learned.**
- The "healthy business" filter made things worse, not better. One possibility is that the bigger mispricing, and the more informative insider buys, come from companies that *look* troubled, where fear is highest. The difference could also be noise. Only the removed-trades split can tell these apart.
- Insider-buying strategies took a heavy hit in 2022. A market-trend gate like idea #2's could matter here too.

## 4. pre_earnings_runup: ride the attention run-up into the next report, then sell before it (REJECTED)

**What it tested.** Academic papers find that stocks, especially recent winners, tend to beat the market in the weeks *before* a scheduled earnings report. The rules were: buy strong stocks 12–20 days before the expected report, sell at least 3 days before it, and avoid the report itself.

**Result.**
- 1207 trades, **−0.15% per trade vs SPY (t −0.75)**. It beat SPY in only 2 of 6 years (2020 and 2021).
- Against the twin it was +0.05% (t 0.20), so it was no better than random similar stocks.
- Average hold was 8.4 days. 87% of trades ended by the planned sell-before-report exit, so the rules ran as designed.
- Max drawdown was −32%.

**Decision: rejected.** It lost to SPY over a large sample.

**What we learned.**
- In $2B+ stocks from 2017 to 2022, the published pre-earnings run-up for winners did not show up once we sell before the report. Effects that are well known from papers often fade after publication.
- **There is a trap in the signal study.** It showed +1.09% vs SPY at 20 days (t 2.34). But 20 trading days from an entry 12–20 calendar days before the report runs *past* the report. Most of that gain likely came from the report and the drift after it, which is the same effect our post-earnings sleeves already try to capture. Read this way, the "premium" sits in the report itself and the drift after it, not in the run-up. The hypothesis also asked to report trades that accidentally held through a report, and that wasn't produced.

## Process concerns
1. **Admission is looser than our own pre-registered kill tests.** react_drift_momentum and insider_drop_intact each met at least two of their own "PROVEN WRONG" conditions and still went live. If we write kill conditions in advance and then ignore them, we lose the main protection against fooling ourselves.
2. **Crowding.** About nine of the 16 live strategies are variants of "buy after earnings," and two are near-copies of the insider-drop idea. They probably pick many of the same stocks on the same days. Sixteen strategies may really be a handful of bets, so the diversification is smaller than the count suggests.
3. **Requested checks went missing.** The removed-vs-kept split (insider_drop_intact), the held-through-report split (pre_earnings_runup) and the parent comparison (market_gate) were all asked for and none were produced. Next round should make them standard outputs.

## Lessons
- **Dropping the EPS-surprise requirement from post-earnings drift cuts the edge from about +3.5% to +0.67% per trade. The surprise data seems to matter, and momentum plus a positive reaction is not enough on its own.** Evidence: react_drift_momentum: 576 trades, +0.67%/trade vs SPY (t 1.15), +0.01% without its best year (2020). This meets 2 of its 3 pre-registered PROVEN WRONG conditions. Parent sue_drift_loose is about +3.5%/trade but on a smaller, sparser sample.. Test: Limit react_drift_momentum to the stock-dates where Alpha Vantage surprise data exists, then split by eps_surprise_px >= 0.001 vs < 0.001 on that same sample. If the surprise group beats the no-surprise group by >= 2 points/trade, surprise is the driver. If the gap is under 1 point, sue_drift_loose's +3.5% is probably partly luck from its small sample.
- **Beating the random twin while barely beating SPY means the comparison pool itself lagged the index (2017-2022 mega-cap dominance). Picking well inside a lagging pool doesn't make money vs just holding SPY.** Evidence: react_drift_momentum: +1.60% vs twin (t 2.40) but only +0.67% vs SPY (t 1.15). The signal study was negative vs SPY at 5/20/60 days but positive vs pool at 60 days (+1.04%).. Test: For every live strategy, compute twin-minus-SPY per trade, i.e. how much the comparison pool lagged SPY. Flag any strategy where twin excess is > 2x SPY excess, and require excess vs SPY with t >= 2 before treating a twin win as evidence.
- **A slow SPY trend gate (6-month return > 0, 3-month return > -5%) removes most bear-market entries while keeping the drift edge. It does not catch sudden selloffs after strong runs, such as late 2018.** Evidence: sue_drift_market_gate: 2022 entries fell from 65 to 10 (about 85% fewer), +1.43%/trade vs SPY (t 1.64), +1.16% without its best year, max drawdown -12.1%. But 2018 was still -1.9% absolute over 35 trades, and the 10 remaining 2022 trades averaged -3.4%, about the same as the parent.. Test: Pull the parent sue_drift_no_momentum's full backtest and check the pre-registered conditions: absolute return per trade +0.5 pts or more and drawdown 3 pts or more better. Then add a faster second condition (e.g. SPY 20-day return > -5%) and see whether 2018 Q4 entries drop without losing over 20% of trades in other years.
- **Insider-buy-the-drop strategies are badly hurt in bear markets. A market-trend gate is the most likely fix to try next, not more company-quality filters.** Evidence: insider_drop_intact: 2022 was its busiest and worst year (42 of 189 trades, -7.0% absolute, -3.1% vs SPY). Excluding 2022, excess rises from +0.45% to about +1.5%/trade.. Test: Apply the sue_drift_market_gate SPY gate (spy_ret120 > 0 and spy_ret60 > -0.05 at entry) to the parent insider_buys_the_drop. Pass if excess/trade does not fall, max drawdown improves by 3 pts or more, and 2022 trades fall by 50% or more.
- **Requiring a healthy business (revenue growth >= 0, operating profit, gross margin >= 20%) made insider-buy-the-drop worse, not better. The overshoot plus insider signal may be stronger at companies that look troubled.** Evidence: insider_drop_intact: +0.45%/trade vs SPY (t 0.32), twin t 0.74, vs parent insider_buys_the_drop +1.87%/trade. It met 2 PROVEN WRONG conditions. The removed-vs-kept split was requested but not produced.. Test: Run the parent's rules on only the trades the health filter removes (rev_growth_q < 0 OR op_margin <= 0 OR gross_margin < 0.2). If that group's excess is above +1.87%/trade with t >= 1.5, test an 'insider buys at a troubled company' variant. If it is about the same as the kept group, treat the parent's edge as unrelated to fundamentals.
- **The pre-earnings run-up for recent winners does not show up in $2B+ stocks over 2017-2022 when the position is sold before the report. Positive 20-day signal-study numbers that cross the report date are measuring the report and post-report drift, not the run-up.** Evidence: pre_earnings_runup: 1207 trades, -0.15%/trade vs SPY (t -0.75), twin +0.05% (t 0.20), beat SPY in 2 of 6 years. 87% of trades exited by the planned sell-before-report rule. Yet the signal study showed +1.09% vs SPY at 20 days (t 2.34), a horizon that spans the report.. Test: For the same entries, split forward return into (a) entry to 3 days before the report, (b) the report window from day -3 to day +2, and (c) day +2 to day +40. If (b)+(c) holds over 80% of the 20-day excess, stop testing pre-report sleeves. Also make the signal study cap horizons at the next estimated report for any idea that exits before earnings.
- **The lab's admission rule ('sign of life') is looser than the ideas' own pre-registered kill conditions. Ideas we declared wrong in advance are going live.** Evidence: react_drift_momentum (excess +0.67% < 1.0% floor; ex-best-year +0.01% <= 0.3%) and insider_drop_intact (excess +0.45% <= parent's +1.87%; twin t 0.74 < 1.5) were both admitted.. Test: Add a 'pre-registered kill' check to admission: parse each hypothesis's PROVEN WRONG thresholds into gates and block admission if any is met. Re-run it on all 16 live strategies, list which would be removed, and retire them at the next cycle unless their dry-run results so far disagree.
- **The live roster is crowded with near-duplicate earnings-drift and insider-drop variants, so 16 strategies may really be only a few independent bets.** Evidence: About 9 of the 16 live strategies are 'buy after earnings' variants (sue_drift_loose/no_momentum/market_gate, react_drift_momentum, earnings_gap_up, surprise_near_high, rs_earnings_confirmed, rev_accel_late_drift, earnings_overreaction). insider_buys_the_drop and insider_drop_intact share rules except for the health filter.. Test: For every pair of live strategies, compute the share of backtest trades with the same symbol entered within 10 trading days of each other, plus the correlation of monthly sleeve returns. For pairs with over 50% overlap or correlation above 0.7, keep only the one with the higher excess t-stat, so freed cap slots go to unrelated ideas.
- **Whether wide exits rescue earnings_gap_up is still unanswered. react_drift_momentum changed both the exits and the entry filters at the same time.** Evidence: earnings_gap_up (tight exits) -0.47%/trade vs react_drift_momentum (20% stop/trailing, 55-day hold) +0.67%/trade, but the entry filters differ (rs_rank, rev_growth, dist_52w_high, scoring).. Test: Re-run earnings_gap_up with its exact entry rules and only the exits swapped to 20% stop, 20% trailing stop, sell at days_to_earnings_est <= 3, and 55-day max hold. If excess rises by 1 point/trade or more, the exits were the problem. If not, the entry is.
