# Lab round 5 report

**In short:** we tested four ideas. Two were rejected and two went live in dry run (simulated trading only, no real money). Only one idea, `quiet_beat_drift`, looks interesting, and it failed its check on unseen data. Three of the lab's automatic decisions don't match the numbers behind them, and I flag each one below.

### A few terms first
- **Excess return:** what the trade made minus what SPY (an S&P 500 fund) made over the same days. A trade can make money and still have negative excess if the market rose more.
- **t-stat:** how sure we can be that a result isn't luck. Below about 2 is weak. Below about 1 is basically noise.
- **Random twin:** a comparison of our picks against randomly chosen stocks with similar timing and exits. Beating the twin means the stock *selection* adds something, not just being in the market.
- **The rising bar:** this round took the lab to 30 backtests. Each extra test is another chance for luck to look like skill, so the t-stat needed to beat SPY keeps rising. It went from 2.55 at test 26 to 2.61 at test 30.
- **Pool:** roughly, the other eligible stocks on the same day.
- **t_adj:** a t-stat corrected because overlapping signals aren't independent. It is often much smaller than the raw one.
- **Kill test:** the "PROVEN WRONG if…" rules each idea wrote down before it was tested.

## 1. `gap_up_wide_exits`: rejected (correctly)

**What it tested:** the same "buy the day after a big earnings gap-up" entry as `earnings_gap_up`. Only the exits changed: wide 20% stops instead of tight ones. The question was whether the tight stops were the problem or the entry was.

**Result:**
- **Per trade:** 654 trades, excess −0.09% per trade (t −0.13). The parent made −0.47%, per the idea text.
- **Raw return:** +1.86% per trade on average. Excess is still negative because SPY rose a little more over the same ~43-day holds.
- **Vs the random twin:** +0.78% (t 1.04), which is weak.
- **Risk:** max drawdown −39%, and the sleeve trailed SPY by 18% overall.

**Kill test:**

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Excess at least +0.53% | **Fail** (−0.09%) |
| t_excess at least 1.0 | **Fail** (−0.13) |
| t_vs_twin at least 1.0 | Pass, barely (1.04) |
| Excess without the best year above +0.2% | Fail: excess is below zero even with the best year included |
| dist_sma50 split | **Not produced** |

**Decision and why:** reject. The 0.4-point gain over the parent is within noise (t about 0), so I would not say the wide exits "helped." There is also a catch: the idea expected about 900 trades and got 654. Longer holds block re-entry, so this wasn't a clean A/B on the same trades.

**What we learned:** the idea's own rule says that if it fails, the entry is the problem and gap-up chasing should be retired. The lab retired `earnings_gap_up` this round to make room in the roster, which fits.

## 2. `insider_drop_troubled`: went live, but it failed its own kill test

**What it tested:** insider buying after a bad earnings reaction, limited to companies that look troubled (shrinking sales, losses or thin margins). The theory was that the parent's edge sits where the fear is.

**Result:**
- **Per trade:** 334 trades, excess +1.26% per trade (t 0.86).
- **Vs the random twin:** +1.45% (t 0.90).
- **Years:** positive in 5 of 7.
- **Signal study:** at 60 days, +1.54% vs pool (t_adj 1.92, still under 2).

**Kill test:**

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Excess at least +1.87% (the parent) | **Fail** (+1.26%) |
| t_excess at least 1.5 | **Fail** (0.86) |
| At least 120 trades | Pass (334) |
| Excess without the best year at least +1.0% | **Fail** |
| No single year supplies more than half the excess | **Fail** |

The last two fail because of 2020. That year supplied about 68 × 9.0% ≈ 6.1 points of excess, while the whole backtest totalled about 334 × 1.26% ≈ 4.2 points. So 2020 alone is more than 100% of the edge. **Without 2020, the strategy loses about 0.7% per trade.**

**Decision and why:** the lab admitted it on a "sign of life" rule, which looks only at the signal study. That conflicts with the idea's pre-declared kill test, which it clearly failed. It's in dry run, so no money is at risk. Still, **this should be treated as a failed idea, not a promising one.**

**What we learned:** neither the "healthy" half (+0.45%, from the idea text) nor the "troubled" half beats the parent. As the idea said in advance, we should stop slicing this edge by company health. The 2020 dependence also raises a question about the parent itself (see lessons).

## 3. `sue_carry_through_report`: rejected, but the stated reason is a pipeline error

**What it tested:** the same "confirmed earnings beat" entry as `sue_drift_no_momentum`, but holding *through* the next report instead of selling 3 days before it. The idea is that companies that beat once tend to beat again.

**Result:**
- **Vs SPY:** 290 trades, excess +3.64% per trade (t 3.04, above the 2.58 bar). Positive in 7 of 7 years, max drawdown −18%.
- **Vs the random twin:** only +0.28% (t 0.20).

**Why the vs-twin number matters:** random stocks held the same way (about 95 days) did almost as well. So nearly all of the "edge" came from being in stocks for long periods in 2016–2022, not from picking these stocks.

**Kill test:**

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Excess at least +2.0% | Pass (+3.64%) |
| t_vs_twin at least 2.0 | **Fail** (0.20) |
| Next-report window split | **Not produced** |
| Drawdown vs the parent | **Not produced** |

**Decision and why:** the system rejected it for "0 signals in 2016–2022." That can't be right: the backtest has 290 trades, and the entry is copied from a live strategy. The signal-study step is broken for this idea, and I can't tell why from the results. Even so, **it would have failed on its own twin criterion**, so rejecting it is the right outcome for the wrong reason.

**What we learned:** with long holds, "beats SPY" can be big and still mean little. The twin comparison is the honest test.

## 4. `quiet_beat_drift`: the best result this round, but it failed its out-of-sample check

**What it tested:** companies that beat earnings estimates but whose stock barely moved (between −3% and +2%). The theory is that nobody noticed, so the price catches up later. All our other drift strategies require a jump of at least 2%, so this is genuinely new ground.

**Training result (2016–2022):**
- **Per trade:** 289 trades, excess +2.65% per trade (t 3.44, above the 2.60 bar).
- **Vs the random twin:** +2.96% (t 3.33).
- **Years and risk:** positive in 5 of 7, win rate 63%, max drawdown only −10%.
- **Without the best year (2021):** still about +2.1% per trade.
- **In 2022:** it lost 1.5% on average, but that was 3.6 points better than SPY.
- **Signal study:** at 60 days, +5.0% vs pool. The raw t of 4.98 shrinks to 2.27 once overlapping signals are accounted for.

**Unseen test (2023–2024):**
- **Per trade:** 59 trades, excess +0.18% per trade (t 0.10).
- **Vs the random twin:** +3.13% (t 1.58).
- The lab marked it `passed_validate: false`.

**Kill test:**

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Excess above +0.5% | Pass |
| t_excess at least 1.0 | Pass |
| t_vs_twin at least 1.0 | Pass |
| At least 120 trades | Pass |
| Top third by surprise vs bottom third | **Not produced** |
| Guidance/tone veto | Can't be tested on history |

**Decision and why:** it is live even though it failed validation. I'd keep it in dry run as a watch item, not a trusted strategy.

On unseen data it barely beat SPY but still beat random twins. With only 59 trades, both numbers are weak evidence in either direction. One *possible* explanation is that 2023–24 was a market where SPY was hard to beat. That is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

**What we learned:** a "quiet beat" may carry information that our jump-based strategies miss. It needs forward trades to prove it.

The most useful findings are the three engineer fixes in the lessons below:
1. Apply the kill tests automatically.
2. Fix the signal-study mismatch.
3. Produce the report splits the ideas ask for.


## Lessons
- **The gap-up entry has no edge. Wide exits didn't rescue it, so gap-up chasing should be retired, and any future gap-up idea must change the entry, not the exits.** Evidence: gap_up_wide_exits: 654 trades, excess -0.09%/trade (t -0.13) vs the parent's -0.47% (per the idea text). That gap is within noise. Fails its own kill tests (needed at least +0.53% and t at least 1.0). earnings_gap_up was retired this round.. Test: Reject any new idea whose entry is days_since_earnings==0 + earnings_reaction>=0.05 + rvol>=2 unless it adds a new entry filter. That filter needs a pre-declared split showing one bucket at excess >= +1% with t >= 2.
- **[Engineer] The lab's admission rules ignore each idea's own pre-declared PROVEN WRONG criteria. A 'sign of life' in the signal study can put a strategy live even when it clearly failed its own kill test.** Evidence: insider_drop_troubled went live on a 60d t_adj of 1.92 while failing 4 of its 5 kill conditions: excess +1.26% < +1.87%; t 0.86 < 1.5; 2020 alone supplied about 6.1 of about 4.2 total excess points; ex-2020 about -0.7%/trade. quiet_beat_drift is also live with passed_validate=false.. Test: Have the lab parse each idea's PROVEN WRONG list into checks and block admission if any check fails. Then re-run round 5: insider_drop_troubled should come out rejected and quiet_beat_drift flagged as watch-only.
- **[Engineer] The signal study can report 0 signals for an idea whose backtest has hundreds of trades. That produces false rejections.** Evidence: sue_carry_through_report: signal study 0 signals at 5/20/60d, but the backtest has 290 trades. Its entry is identical to the live sue_drift_no_momentum.. Test: Run the signal study on sue_drift_no_momentum and sue_carry_through_report. Signal counts must match within 1%, because the entries are identical. Add an assertion that fails the run if signals == 0 while backtest trades > 0.
- **With long holds, excess vs SPY can look strong while the random twin shows the stock selection adds nothing. Judge long-hold strategies mainly on t_vs_twin.** Evidence: sue_carry_through_report (about 95-day holds): +3.64%/trade vs SPY (t 3.04, 7/7 years) but only +0.28% vs the twin (t 0.20).. Test: For every live strategy with avg_hold_days > 60, list excess_vs_twin and t_vs_twin. Flag for retirement any with t_vs_twin < 1.0. Start with the parent sue_drift_no_momentum: if its t_vs_twin < 1.5, its +1.52% edge is mostly market exposure.
- **Slicing insider_buys_the_drop by company health doesn't help: neither half beats the parent. The troubled slice's edge is a 2020 artifact, which raises doubt about the parent itself.** Evidence: Healthy slice (insider_drop_intact) +0.45%/trade (from the idea text); troubled slice +1.26% (t 0.86), and ex-2020 about -0.7%/trade. Parent +1.87%.. Test: Recompute insider_buys_the_drop excess with 2020 trades removed. If ex-2020 excess < +0.75%/trade or t < 1.5, downgrade the parent to watch-only. Stop proposing further health-based slices.
- **Three nested insider-drop variants are live together and will double-count the same trades.** Evidence: insider_buys_the_drop, insider_drop_intact (healthy filter) and insider_drop_troubled (complement filter) are all live. The two children are subsets of the parent's entry rule.. Test: Count dry-run positions where 2 or more of these three strategies hold the same symbol at the same time. If overlap > 30% of positions, keep only one (the parent) and retire the children.
- **'Quiet' EPS beats (a real beat, but the stock moved between -3% and +2%) were the round's strongest training result but did not hold up vs SPY on unseen 2023-24. It is a watch item, not a proven edge.** Evidence: Train: 289 trades, +2.65%/trade (t 3.44), vs twin +2.96% (t 3.33), max drawdown -10%. Unseen 2023-24: 59 trades, +0.18% vs SPY (t 0.10), +3.13% vs twin (t 1.58).. Test: After at least 40 forward dry-run trades, require excess vs twin > 0 with t >= 1.0, else retire. Also run the pre-declared top-third vs bottom-third eps_surprise_px split on 2016-2024: the top third must beat the bottom third by at least 1 point. And check the hypothesis that 2023-24's narrow market explains the SPY shortfall: compare the strategy against an equal-weight benchmark (e.g. RSP) on 2023-24.

Test: Add a generic 'split_by' feature-bucket report to the backtest output. Re-run gap_up_wide_exits and confirm the results JSON contains 3 dist_sma50 buckets with trades and avg_excess for each.
- **Raw signal-study t-stats are heavily inflated by overlapping signals. Only the adjusted t should be used.** Evidence: quiet_beat_drift 60d vs pool: raw t 4.98 vs t_adj 2.27. gap_up_wide_exits 60d: raw t 1.98 vs adjusted 1.48..
