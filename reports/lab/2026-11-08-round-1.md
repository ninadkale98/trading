# Strategy lab: round 1 report

## Summary

We tested four ideas. Three were rejected and one, `surprise_near_high`, went live in simulation. **None of the four showed reliable evidence of an edge.** The one admitted is a long shot on trial, not a proven strategy. It passed only 1 of the 4 quality gates and failed a test it set for itself.

The round also exposed two likely problems in the lab's measurements (see "Problems with the lab itself").

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rs_wide_leash` | 507 | -1.36% (-1.02) | +0.46% (0.31) | 1 of 4 | Rejected |
| `insider_dip_quality` | 264 | +2.85% (0.92) | +2.92% (0.90) | 1 of 4 | Rejected |

### Terms used
- **Excess:** return above a comparison, here SPY (an S&P 500 fund).
- **Random twin:** a comparison that takes similar trades chosen by chance. Beating it suggests the stock-picking rule added something.
- **Pool:** the comparison group of stocks in the signal study.
- **Signal study:** measures what stocks did 5, 20 and 60 trading days after a signal, without simulating a portfolio.
- **Backtest:** simulates actual trades under the full rules.
- **t-stat:** how far a result stands out from luck. Below about 2 is weak evidence.
- **Rising bar:** each extra test makes a lucky result more likely, so the required t rose from 2.23 to 2.33 this round as the count went from 12 to 15 backtests.
- **Max drawdown:** the largest peak-to-trough fall.
- **Sleeve:** a slice of a portfolio running only this strategy. I read the sleeve figures below as cumulative over the whole test.

## The ideas

### 1. `rs_wide_leash`: strong stocks with looser exits
**What it tested:** The parent strategy, `relative_strength`, held for only about 19 days on average. This variant asked whether wider exits (25% stops, and selling only below the 200-day average) would let winners run.

**Result:**
- Average hold rose to 58 days, so the exits did change behaviour.
- Performance did not improve. The strategy trailed SPY by 1.36% per trade (t -1.02), and the win rate was 36%.
- Max drawdown was -37.5%, and the sleeve finished about 76 points behind SPY.
- Its edge over the twin was +0.46% (t 0.31), below the parent's +0.93%.
- Excess was positive in 3 of 6 years, with the worst in 2021 at -9.87%.

**Decision: rejected, and correctly, but the stated reason means nothing.** The verdict says "+0.00% vs pool (t 0.00)", which came from a comparison that appears not to have worked. The backtest alone justifies the rejection, because the idea failed the twin tests it set for itself.

**What we learned:** Even with stops this wide, 332 of 507 trades (65%) still ended at a stop. Holding longer did not turn these entries into better trades. The +0.84% vs SPY at 60 days (t 3.95) looks strong, but that t is not corrected for overlapping windows, and only 47% of signals beat SPY.

### 2. `insider_dip_quality`: insiders buying fallen, cash-generating smaller companies
**What it tested:** Whether several insiders buying a stock that is at least 20% below its high, at a company with positive free cash flow, signals a bargain.

**Result:**
- The signal study was negative at every horizon, including -0.67% vs pool at 60 days (adjusted t -0.15).
- The backtest showed +2.85% per trade vs SPY (t 0.92), and the win rate was 50%.
- Max drawdown was -41.6%, and the sleeve finished about 9 points ahead of SPY.

**Decision: rejected.** It failed all three of its own tests: adjusted t below 1.5, twin t below 2, and no improvement on the parent's +1.06%.

**What we learned:** The positive backtest is mostly one year. 2020 contributed 71 trades at +15.44% each. Without 2020, the other 193 trades averaged about -1.8%. Buying beaten-down stocks just before a historic rebound flatters this kind of rule.

### 3. `surprise_near_high`: confirmed earnings beats near the highs (now live)
**What it tested:** Whether to buy 2 to 10 days after a report when the company beat estimates, the stock reacted well, sales were growing and the price was near its high. It sells before the next report.

**Result:**
- 50 trades, with a 60% win rate and a 47-day average hold.
- +2.18% per trade vs SPY (t 1.25), and only +0.93% vs the twin (t 0.41).
- At 60 days the signal study showed +4.85% vs pool (t 2.03 unadjusted, 0.26 adjusted).
- Excess was positive in 5 of 6 calendar years, but 2021 was -8.22% and some years had only 3 to 8 trades.
- Max drawdown of -5.9% looks good, but probably reflects how rarely it trades.

**Decision: admitted under the "sign of life" rule.** It runs only in dry run or paper trading, so no real money is involved.

**Reasons for caution:**
- **It failed its own test.** The hypothesis says it is proven wrong if t vs twin is below 2.0, and the value is 0.41.
- **It is short on trades:** 50 against the 100 required.
- **The t-stat is well short of the bar:** 1.25 against 2.30.
- **Its one passed gate is thin.** "Works most years" shows "100% of 2 years", apparently because only 2 years had enough trades to count.
- **Luck is a plausible explanation.** A zero-edge strategy reaches t 1.25 about 10% of the time. Across 15 tests, the chance of at least one doing so is roughly 80% (a rough figure that treats the tests as independent).
- **Two checks are missing.** The planned test with the earnings surprise capped at 0.50 is not in these results, and no out-of-sample validation ran (`validate` is empty).

**What we learned:** The idea is promising enough to watch, but this data cannot confirm it. At about 8 trades a year, it would take roughly 6 more years to double the sample, so live results alone will not settle it soon.

### 4. `gp_yield_trend`: cheap gross profit in uptrending stocks
**What it tested:** Whether stocks offering a lot of gross profit per dollar of company value, and already in an uptrend, beat the market.

**Result:**
- Underperformance vs pool grew with time: -0.18% at 5 days, -0.72% at 20 days and -1.34% at 60 days (adjusted t -1.03).
- The backtest showed -1.90% per trade vs SPY (t -1.98), and -0.95% vs the twin (t -0.86).
- Only 2017 had positive excess, and the win rate was 42%.
- Max drawdown was -31.6%, and the sleeve finished about 49 points behind SPY.

**Decision: rejected.** It failed its twin test, and the gates agree.

**What we learned:** This was the most consistent result of the round, and it was consistently negative. Stops were not the cause, since only 8 of 321 trades exited that way. The stocks themselves lagged. Flipping the rule to bet the opposite way would count as another test and would need its own economic reason.

## Problems with the lab itself

1. **The "vs pool" comparison failed for `rs_wide_leash`.** Exactly 0.00% with t 0.00 at all three horizons suggests the signals and the pool were the same stocks, leaving nothing to compare. That should read "could not measure", not "no edge".
2. **The overlap adjustment may be too harsh on rare signals.** In this data, adjusted t is exactly the raw t divided by the square root of the horizon, whatever the signal frequency. That fits strategies signalling almost every day, like `gp_yield_trend` (1,422 signal days). It likely over-penalises `surprise_near_high` (47 signal days), whose true 60-day t lies somewhere between 0.26 and 2.03. Even the top of that range is below the current bar.
3. **Admission ignores each idea's own test for being wrong.** `surprise_near_high` went live despite failing one.

## Where things stand

- **Live strategies (8):** `accumulation`, `earnings_gap_up`, `earnings_overreaction`, `insider_cluster`, `quality_pullback`, `relative_strength`, `return_to_average`, `surprise_near_high`.
- **Retired this round:** none.
- **Test period:** backtest results stop at 2022. These results don't show whether later years are being held back for validation.


## Lessons
- **An exact 0.00% vs pool with t 0.00 means the comparison failed, so it must be reported as 'could not measure' and must not be used as a 'no edge' verdict.** Evidence: rs_wide_leash showed vs_pool 0.0 and t 0.0 at 5, 20 and 60 days across about 100,000 signals, and its verdict cited that figure. Its filter-only rules probably made signals and pool identical.. Test: Add a build check that fails when the signal set equals the pool or when vs_pool is exactly 0 at all horizons. Re-run rs_wide_leash's signal study against the broader eligible universe.
- **The overlap adjustment divides t by sqrt(horizon) regardless of signal frequency, which probably over-penalises rare signals and biases the lab against low-frequency strategies.** Evidence: t_vs_pool_adj equals t_vs_pool / sqrt(horizon) in every checked case, such as surprise_near_high at 60 days (2.027 to 0.262, 47 signal days) and gp_yield_trend at 60 days (-7.94 to -1.03, 1,422 signal days).. Test: Recompute all four signal studies with an overlap-aware method, such as date-clustered errors or a calendar-time portfolio. Confirm that sparse strategies change materially while dense ones change little, then re-check past rejections of sparse ideas.
- **Admission should check each idea's pre-registered falsification criteria, and any idea admitted despite failing one should be labelled probationary with a written retirement rule.** Evidence: surprise_near_high said it was proven wrong if t_vs_twin was below 2.0. It scored 0.41, passed 1 of 4 gates, and still went live under the sign-of-life rule.. Test: Add a pass/fail/not-run field for each falsifier to the round output. Backtest the lab's admission rule over past rounds with and without a falsifier requirement, and compare later live or out-of-sample excess.
- **Require positive average excess after removing the single best year, because one exceptional year can create an apparently positive backtest.** Evidence: insider_dip_quality averaged +2.85% per trade vs SPY, but 2020 contributed 71 trades at +15.44%. The other 193 trades averaged about -1.8%.. Test: Add a leave-best-year-out statistic to the backtest output and apply it to all 8 live strategies. Flag any whose average excess turns negative.
- **Widening exits alone did not improve relative_strength; longer holding did not add edge to these entries.** Evidence: rs_wide_leash lifted average hold from about 19 to 58 days, but excess vs twin fell from the parent's +0.93% to +0.46% (t 0.31), average excess vs SPY was -1.36%, and 65% of trades still ended at a stop.. Test: Before proposing another exit-only variant, run the parent's entry across a preset stop grid of 15%, 25% and 35%, counting each as a test. Continue only if excess vs twin improves steadily with stop width.
- **gp_yield_trend's top-ranked stocks lagged at every horizon, so the score itself needs diagnosing before any further gross-profit-yield variant is built.** Evidence: Excess vs pool was -0.18%, -0.72% and -1.34% at 5, 20 and 60 days. The backtest was -1.90% vs SPY (t -1.98), positive only in 2017, and only 8 of 321 trades exited on a stop.. Test: Within the eligible stocks, compare top-third vs bottom-third returns separately for gp_yield, rs_rank and op_margin_chg, as the hypothesis specified. Report sector and year breakdowns. Treat any inverted rule as a new test requiring its own mechanism.
- **Estimate trade count before backtesting; heavily filtered rules can be unable to reach the 100-trade gate within the data available.** Evidence: surprise_near_high stacked nine eligibility conditions and produced 50 trades over 2017-2022, about 8 per year. Reaching 100 at that pace would take roughly 6 more years.. Test: Add a pre-backtest count requiring an expected 100 or more trades over the test window. For surprise_near_high, test one preset looser version (for example dist_52w_high >= -0.15) and count it against the multiple-testing bar.
- **The 'works most years' gate should require a minimum number of qualifying years, since a pass based on two years is weak evidence.** Evidence: surprise_near_high passed with '100% of 2 years'. Only 2017 and 2020 appear to have had enough trades to count, while the other four years had 3 to 8 trades.. Test: Require at least 4 qualifying years to pass the gate, otherwise report 'insufficient data'. Re-score all live strategies under this rule.
- **surprise_near_high still needs its planned robustness and out-of-sample checks; live simulation is too slow to substitute for them.** Evidence: The validate field is null, the backtest years end at 2022, and the promised run with eps_surprise capped at 0.50 does not appear in the results.. Test: Run the 0.50-cap version, and run the unchanged rules on 2023 to the present if that data has not been used for development. Pass only if excess vs SPY and vs twin remain positive, and report the t-stats against the current bar.
