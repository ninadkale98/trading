# Idea lab round 6 (2026-11-25)

I# Strategy Lab: Round 6 Report

**Summary:** The lab tested four ideas, admitted one and rejected three.

- **The admission is weak.** `sue_drift_midcap` went live in dry run on one strong number, but it fails two of the four "proven wrong" tests it set for itself.
- **One rejection isn't a real result.** `sue_repeat_beat` was rejected, but its two test engines disagree (0 signals in one, 389 trades in the other), so we don't actually know anything about it yet.
- **Two rejections are solid.** `margin_inflection_drift` and `misread_report_rebound` showed no edge.
- **No backtest cleared the bar this round.** The best backtest t-stat was 1.45. After 33 backtests the bar is about 2.6.

### Quick terms
- **Excess return:** the trade's return minus what SPY (the S&P 500 index fund) did over the same days.
- **Random twin:** random stocks bought and sold with the same timing and exit rules. Beating the twin means the *stock picks* added value, not just the exits or the market.
- **Pool:** all stocks that were eligible to be picked that day. "vs pool" means the flagged stocks compared with those alternatives.
- **t-stat:** how many "noise-widths" a result is from zero.
  - Below about 2, it could easily be luck.
  - Every extra test we run makes a lucky result more likely, so the lab raises the bar. It is now about 2.6 after 33 backtests.
  - "t_adj" is the lab's adjusted t-stat. It is the safer number to read.
- **Signal study vs backtest:**
  - The signal study asks: "5, 20 or 60 trading days after the signal, did the flagged stocks beat the pool?"
  - The backtest actually trades the rules, with stops and exits.
- **PEAD (post-earnings-announcement drift):** the well-documented habit of stocks to keep drifting in the direction of an earnings surprise for weeks afterward.
- **Drawdown:** the worst peak-to-trough fall of the strategy's account value.

## 1. `sue_drift_midcap`: earnings-beat drift, mid-caps only ($1B–$15B). **Admitted (on probation)**

**What it tested.** The idea: drift should be stronger in companies with fewer analysts and funds watching them.

- **Change from its parent:** the rules are the same as `sue_drift_no_momentum` except for one thing, a ceiling of $15B on company size.
- **Entry:** 2–10 days after a confirmed earnings beat with a positive price reaction.
- **Exits:** a 20% stop-loss, a 20% trailing stop, a sale before the next earnings report, or 55 trading days at most.

**Results**

| Measure | Value | Bar | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trades | 157 | ≥ 100 | ✅ |
| Average excess vs SPY per trade | +1.58% (t 1.34) | t ≥ 2.61 | ❌ |
| Excess vs random twin | +1.95% (t 1.45) | t ≥ 2 | ❌ |
| Without its best year (2020) | +0.63%/trade | — | — |
| Win rate | 54.8% | — | — |
| Max drawdown | −9.6% | — | — |
| Signal study, 5 days | +0.32% vs pool (t_adj 0.54) | — | flat |
| Signal study, 20 days | +0.19% vs pool (t_adj 0.26) | — | flat |
| **Signal study, 60 days** | **+4.24% vs pool (t_adj 2.98)** | — | strong |

**Scoring it against its own "proven wrong" list (written before the test):**
- Fewer than 80 trades? No (157). ✅
- Excess vs twin ≤ the parent's +2.13%? **Yes, it is +1.95%. ❌ Tripped.**
- t vs twin below 1.5? **Yes, it is 1.45. ❌ Tripped.**
- Without the best year below +0.5%/trade? No (+0.63%). ✅

**Decision and why.** The lab's "sign of life" rule admitted it to dry run (simulated trading) because the 60-day signal study, at t_adj 2.98, is above the multiple-testing bar. To be honest:
- **Its own test failed.** It failed two of its own four pre-registered kill tests and both backtest gates. So it **does not replace the parent** (that was the deal only if it passed), and the "less attention" idea is **not confirmed**.
- **The strong number is a single cell.** It is one of nine filled-in signal-study cells this round (the other three ideas' cells are mostly near zero or negative). The 5-day and 20-day results for this same idea are essentially zero. Real PEAD usually shows up early, so an edge that only appears between day 20 and day 60 is unusual.
- **The 60-day window is longer than the strategy actually holds.** Its maximum hold is 55 days, the average is 45, and it exits before the next report. A 60-day window starting 2–10 days after a report probably includes the *next* report. My hypothesis, not yet tested: part of the +4.24% may come from the next earnings reaction, which the strategy never holds through.
- **One year carries most of the result.** 2020 supplied about 65% of all its excess return (20 trades averaging +8.1%). 2016, 2021 and 2022 were negative.
- **There is a small mismatch in the year count.** The "works most years" gate reports 67% of 6 years. The yearly table has 7 rows with 4 positive (57%). Possibly 2021 (only 9 trades) is left out by a minimum-trades rule; this needs confirming.
- **It doubles a bet we already have.** It is a strict subset of its parent (every midcap trade is also a parent trade), and both are now live. That means we are holding the same bet twice, not two independent strategies.

**What we learned.** There is a hint that mid-cap earnings beats keep paying off over about 3 months, but it rests on one measurement window that the strategy doesn't really trade, and on one strong year.

## 2. `sue_repeat_beat`: second earnings beat in a row. **Rejected, but really "not tested"**

**What it tested.** Earnings surprises tend to repeat (Bernard & Thomas): analysts are slow to update. So a beat that follows a previous beat should drift more. The one change from the parent was that the previous report also had to be a beat.

**Results**
- The signal study found **0 signals**.
- The backtest made **389 trades**:
  - +0.74% vs SPY per trade (t 1.36)
  - **−0.03% vs the random twin (t −0.05)**
  - 6 of 7 years positive vs SPY
  - −19.6% drawdown

**Decision and why.** The lab rejected it as "too rare". That label is wrong: the two engines disagree about the same rule.
- Zero signals and 389 trades can't both be right.
- 389 trades is also 2.5–4 times the expected 100–150 for a rule that is *stricter* than its parent.
- The likely culprit is the "look back to day p" step, which reads the previous report's surprise from the day before this report's reaction.
- Until the engines are reconciled, **treat the backtest numbers as untrusted.** Read this as "the idea has not been tested yet", not "repeat beats don't work".

**What we learned.** Rules that look up a value on an earlier day (p) need a check that both engines pick the same trades.

## 3. `margin_inflection_drift`: operating margin turns up, and the market liked it. **Rejected (correctly)**

**What it tested.** The idea: when year-over-year operating margin flips from shrinking to growing, analysts are slow to catch up. Entry was 15–40 days after the report, once the 10-Q filing had updated the numbers.

**Results**
- **Trades:** 246.
- **Vs SPY:** +0.82%/trade (t 0.87).
- **Vs twin:** +1.10% (t 1.03).
- **Without the best year:** only **+0.04%/trade**. 2020 supplied about 95% of all its excess.
- **Years positive:** 4 of 7 (57%).
- **Signal study:** −0.85% at 20 days, +0.56% at 60 days (t_adj 0.46).
- **Exits:** 121 of 246 trades (49%) ended on the pre-earnings exit.

**Decision and why.** Rejected. It tripped two of its own kill lines (t vs twin below 1.5, and without its best year below +0.4%). Take 2020 away and nothing is left. It also didn't clearly beat `rev_accel_late_drift`, so margins added nothing beyond revenue acceleration.

**What we learned.** Fundamentals from the filing, bought 3–8 weeks after the report, showed no drift in our data. And because entry is so late, about half the trades get cut short by the next earnings date.

## 4. `misread_report_rebound`: stock fell on the report, but the filed numbers improved. **Rejected (correctly)**

**What it tested.** A deliberately bold contrarian bet. Rules:
- The stock dropped 3–12% on earnings.
- The 10-Q then showed sales growth speeding up and margins growing.
- The selling had stopped.

The bet was that the price recovers as analysts update their models.

**Results**
- **Trades:** 328.
- **Vs SPY:** **−0.56%/trade** (t −0.84).
- **Vs twin:** −0.02% (t −0.03).
- **Years positive:** 3 of 7 (43%).
- **Max drawdown:** −24.4%.
- **Exits:** 165 of 328 (50%) ended on the pre-earnings exit.
- **Signal study:** +0.79% vs pool at 20 days. That looks significant on the raw t (2.10) but falls to 1.39 on the lab's adjusted t, and it is gone by 60 days (t_adj 0.19).

**Decision and why.** Rejected. The picks were no better than random stocks with the same exits.

The 20-day number is a good teaching example: a raw t of 2.1 looks like evidence, but once the lab adjusts for flagged stocks clustering on the same days, it isn't.

**What we learned.** In this data, "the price fell but the fundamentals are fine" did not produce a rebound. That fits the research finding that drift follows the direction of the reaction.

## Lessons
- **Admission should check each idea's own 'PROVEN WRONG' criteria mechanically. An idea that trips any of them should be marked 'probation' and must not replace its parent, even when the sign-of-life rule admits it.** Evidence: sue_drift_midcap tripped 2 of its 4 pre-registered kill lines but went live on the 60d signal study (t_adj 2.98). Its excess vs twin was +1.95%, at or below the parent's +2.13%, and its t vs twin was 1.45, below 1.5. Both backtest gates failed (t 1.34 vs a 2.61 bar; t 1.45 vs 2).. Test: Add a gate that parses the hypothesis kill criteria and evaluates them against the results. Re-run round 6 through it: midcap should come out as 'probation, failed own test'. Pass if every admitted idea's status records which kill lines it tripped.
- **The signal study and the backtest must select the same entries for the same rule, especially rules that look up a value on an earlier day (p = t - days_since_earnings - 1). A mismatch makes the verdict meaningless.** Evidence: sue_repeat_beat had 0 signals in the signal study but 389 backtest trades, 2.5-4x the expected 100-150 for a rule stricter than its parent. It was rejected as 'too rare', which is not supported.. Test: Add an assertion that the (symbol, entry date) sets from both engines match to within 5%; otherwise the run gets build_error. Also report the share of reaction days where eps_surprise_px is non-NaN at day p. Re-run sue_repeat_beat after the fix and judge it then, with the same pre-registered kill lines.
- **sue_drift_midcap's only strong number (60d signal study) covers a window longer than the strategy holds, and it may include the next earnings reaction. Its edge is unconfirmed until the signal study is measured over the horizon the strategy actually trades.** Evidence: 5d +0.32% (t_adj 0.54), 20d +0.19% (t_adj 0.26), 60d +4.24% (t_adj 2.98). The strategy's max hold is 55 days, its average hold is 45, and it exits when days_to_earnings_est <= 3. 2020 supplied about 65% of its backtest excess; without 2020 it makes +0.63%/trade.. Test: Re-run the signal study with each horizon cut off at min(55 days, 3 days before the next earnings), with and without 2020. Kill it if the truncated t_adj is below 2.0 or the result without 2020 is below +1%. Check this before the next round's decay review.
- **Entering 15-40 days after a report with a 50-day max hold means about half of trades are cut short by the pre-earnings exit. Late-entry earnings ideas really test holds of a few weeks, not months.** Evidence: margin_inflection_drift: 121 of 246 exits (49%) were the pre-earnings 'signal' exit. misread_report_rebound: 165 of 328 (50%). Both had about zero edge vs twin (t 1.03 and t -0.03).. Test: For any idea with days_since_earnings >= 15 at entry, report the median hold by exit reason. Require a pre-registered expected median hold. Flag it when more than 40% of exits are pre-earnings exits.
- **Buying after a negative earnings reaction because the filed numbers improved showed no edge. The reaction's direction dominated the fundamentals in our data.** Evidence: misread_report_rebound, 328 trades: -0.56%/trade vs SPY, -0.02% vs twin (t -0.03), 43% of years positive, -24% drawdown. The 20d signal study had raw t 2.10, but its adjusted t was only 1.39, and the effect was gone by 60d (t_adj 0.19).. Test: Before proposing another contra-reaction idea, require a pre-test on the signal study at 20d and 60d with adjusted t >= 2 across at least 300 signals. Otherwise skip it.
- **SEC-fundamental changes (margin inflection), entered after the 10-Q, added nothing beyond the 2020 rally and nothing beyond revenue acceleration.** Evidence: margin_inflection_drift: +0.82%/trade vs SPY (t 0.87), but only +0.04%/trade without 2020 (2020 was about 95% of the excess). The 60d signal study was +0.56% (t_adj 0.46). Its hypothesis put rev_accel_late_drift at +0.01%/trade.. Test: Pull the backtest without its best year for rev_accel_late_drift and any other live fundamentals-only strategy. Flag any with less than +0.4%/trade without the best year for decay review.
- **Variants that are strict subsets of a live parent create overlapping bets. Measure the overlap before counting them as separate strategies for sizing or diversification.** Evidence: sue_drift_midcap equals sue_drift_no_momentum plus a $15B size ceiling. Both are now live, alongside several other earnings-drift variants (sue_drift_loose, sue_drift_market_gate, quiet_beat_drift, surprise_near_high, react_drift_momentum, rs_earnings_confirmed). return_to_average, from a different family, was retired for the cap in the same round.. Test: Compute the share of midcap entries that are also parent entries within 3 days, and the correlation of daily returns between them. If overlap is above 50% or correlation above 0.7, treat them as one bet in sizing and in the live-count cap.
