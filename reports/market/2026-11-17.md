# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-17

> Stocks fell for a second day before Nvidia's earnings (SPY −1.0%, Dow ETF DIA −1.6%), and the selling was broad: decliners outnumbered advancers almost 4 to 1. Software was hit hardest (IGV −3.3%, Salesforce reportedly about −9%), while chips held steady (SOXX +0.2%). Unlike Monday, nothing was safe. Even staples and utilities fell, and only short-term Treasuries and the fear gauge rose. After the close, Nvidia beat estimates, guided next-quarter revenue to about $130B, and rose roughly 2–5% in after-hours trading. Wednesday will show whether that relief spreads beyond chips or fades, as Nvidia's post-earnings rally did a year ago.

## 1. What happened

**Facts from our pack:** SPY (S&P 500 ETF) fell **1.0%**, QQQ (Nasdaq 100) fell **0.7%** and DIA (Dow Jones) fell **1.6%**. The average stock did worse than the big indexes: the equal-weight S&P (RSP, every stock weighted the same) fell **1.5%**, the median stock fell **1.4%**, and **790 stocks rose while 2,970 fell**. Only **29** stocks were near 52-week highs, against **126** near lows. VIXY, which tracks fear-gauge futures, rose **7.4%** and is up **15.7%** in a week. Press reports say the S&P closed at about a five-month low.

**Why (facts from news, with my interpretation marked):**
- **Software sold off hard.** IGV (software ETF) fell **3.3%** and SKYY (cloud) fell **2.9%**. Market wraps say Salesforce dropped about 9%, with Intuit, ServiceNow and Oracle also lower, on worries that AI competition will squeeze software companies. Salesforce and Home Depot are both in the Dow, which explains most of why the Dow did worst of the three big indexes.
- **Retail earnings didn't help.** Home Depot beat estimates but still fell about 2.5%. Disney beat on earnings and announced a $3B cost-cutting plan, yet reportedly closed down about 1.5%. October retail sales reportedly rose only 0.2% for the month, after 0.8% in September.
- **Interest rates are still a headwind.** The 10-year Treasury yield is **5.12%**. The real 10-year yield (what's left after expected inflation) is **2.92%**, up from 2.74% a month ago. Fed official Hammack said businesses are passing on more price increases, which keeps a December rate hike on the table. A higher real yield makes future profits worth less today. That hurts expensive growth stocks most.
- **Chips were steady (SOXX +0.2%, SMH −0.4%).** My interpretation: after Monday's drop of more than 5%, traders weren't willing to keep selling right before Nvidia reported.

**After the close:** Nvidia, reporting for the first time under interim CEO Colette Kress after Jensen Huang resigned, posted about **$115.8B** in revenue (up roughly 103% from a year ago) and guided next quarter to about **$130B**, both above estimates. The stock closed at $155.46 during regular hours and traded about **2–5% higher after hours**; reports differ depending on the time they were taken. None of that is in today's numbers yet.

## 2. Where money moved

Today was different from Monday. On Monday, staples and utilities still rose. Today **every sector fell**: utilities −1.6%, staples −1.2%, healthcare −1.4%, financials −1.9%. The only gains were in short-term and long Treasuries (SHY +0.1%, TLT +0.2%), natural gas, silver, India (+0.7%) and volatility. When even defensive stocks fall, it usually means investors are cutting stock exposure overall, not just swapping one sector for another. (Interpretation.)

The **month-long rotation** (money moving from one group of stocks to another) is still clear in our numbers:
- Lagging over the month: semiconductors **−15.8%** (SMH), tech **−12.0%** (XLK), momentum **−11.7%** (MTUM), speculative growth **−15.1%** (ARKK), bitcoin **−22.6%**.
- Leading over the month: staples **+4.2%**, utilities **+3.7%**, healthcare **+3.0%**, financials **+2.4%**. The equal-weight S&P beat SPY by **4.7 points**.
- One headline in the pack says Wall Street sold chips and bought Coca-Cola at the strongest pace since September 2001.

Rate-sensitive and commodity themes are still breaking down: solar (TAN) **−5.2% today and −12.4% this week**, uranium −9.4% this week, copper miners −10.4% this week. The dollar (UUP) is up 2.2% in a month, which usually weighs on commodities.

**Credit is calm.** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) is **3.34%**, barely changed from 3.31% a month ago, and HYG slipped only 0.1%. So far this is a stock-valuation scare, not a credit or recession scare.

## 3. The story so far

For a month the market has been moving out of AI, chips and expensive growth stocks and into defensive and value stocks (cheaper companies with steadier profits). Hot inflation is part of the background: CPI is **4.08%** a year, core PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge) is **3.12%**, and a December rate hike is expected. Huang's resignation and weekend warnings about AI from Fed Chair Warsh and IMF chief Georgieva added to the worry. Breadth (how many stocks are taking part in a move) has weakened fast: only **38%** of stocks are above their 50-day average, down from 50% a week ago, and **42%** are above their 200-day average. Our exposure dial is at **50%**.

Nvidia's beat is good news for the AI story, but keep it in perspective. A year ago Nvidia also beat and jumped after hours, then the stock and the market sold off within a day. The real question is not whether Nvidia's business is fine. It is whether investors will pay high valuations again while the 10-year yield sits above 5%. A one-day chip rally would not undo a month of rotation.

**Both sides:** Bulls can point to strong AI demand, low unemployment (**4.1%**), calm credit spreads and very negative sentiment, which often comes before bounces. Bears can point to rising real yields, possible Fed hikes, weak breadth and software being hit by AI disruption, which no chip earnings report can fix.

## 5. Lesson of the day

**"Priced in"**: a stock's reaction depends on results *compared with what investors already expected*, not on whether the results are good. Nvidia reportedly went into the report about 26% below its peak, so expectations had already fallen. That made a beat more likely to bring relief. But after-hours trading is thin (few buyers and sellers), so early moves can grow or reverse once the regular session opens.
