# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-18

> Wednesday was a split day with no clear direction. SPY slipped 0.3%, but under the surface money moved hard: shoppers' stocks rallied after retailer earnings (XRT +2.3%, XLY +1.8%), cybersecurity jumped (CIBR +4.7%), and commodities fell across the board (copper −5.2%, silver −4.5%, gold −3.2%, oil −2.9%). Healthcare fell 2.2% after a proposed Medicare Advantage audit plan sank Humana 13%. Nvidia's earnings beat did not lift chips (SMH −0.1%), and our news feed flagged an unconfirmed report of an SEC investigation and a delayed 10-Q filing. The bigger picture is unchanged: a 10-year yield of 5.10%, a strong dollar, an expected December rate hike and weak breadth (42% of stocks above their 200-day average) keep the market defensive and choosy.

## 1. What happened

**Facts from the pack:** SPY fell 0.3%. The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) rose 0.4% and the Dow (DIA) fell 1.1%. Advancers and decliners were close to even (1,764 vs 1,994), and the median stock moved −0.1%. After two days of broad selling (Monday SPY −1.9%, Tuesday −1.0%), the market stopped falling, but it did not bounce. The fear gauge (VIXY, a fund tracking expected volatility) fell 2.9% but is still up 20.5% on the week.

Three things drove the day:

- **Commodities fell across the board.** Copper −5.2%, copper miners (COPX) −6.3%, silver −4.5%, gold −3.2%, crude oil −2.9%, and oil & gas producers (XOP) −3.4%. Energy (XLE, −3.3%) was the worst sector. The dollar rose another 0.3%, and 10-year expected inflation fell to 2.19% from 2.35% a month ago. *Interpretation:* a strong dollar plus high "real" yields (yields after inflation, now 2.89%) make assets that pay no income, like gold, or that are priced in dollars, like metals, less attractive. I could not find a single news trigger for the drop, so treat the cause as unclear.
- **Retail earnings lifted shopping stocks.** TJX, Lowe's and Target reported before the open, and the pack lists Walmart raising its full-year outlook (our earnings calendar shows Walmart on Nov 19, so that date may be off). Retail (XRT) rose 2.3%, consumer discretionary (XLY) 1.8% and homebuilders (XHB) 1.7%. Homebuilders also got a strong housing-starts report: 1.309 million homes per year vs 1.191 million in the prior release.
- **Healthcare sank 2.2%.** Humana fell 13% on a proposed Medicare Advantage audit framework (Medicare Advantage is the private insurance version of Medicare, and the audits check whether insurers were overpaid). Agilon (−21%), Alignment (−12%) and Clover (−12%) fell with it. Ardent Health fell 19% on a share sale. *Interpretation:* UnitedHealth is part of the Dow, so the insurer sell-off, together with energy, probably explains why DIA lagged.

**Cybersecurity was the surprise.** CIBR jumped 4.7%, with Tenable +15.8%, Qualys +12.9%, Netskope +12.7%, Rapid7 +12.5% and Okta +12.1%. Software overall (IGV) rose 1.2%. The pack does not show a clear catalyst. Palo Alto (PANW) reported after the close, so some of this may be positioning before its results. I could not confirm a specific cause.

**Nvidia:** last night's beat (next-quarter guidance around $130B) did not carry over to the chip group: SMH −0.1%, SOXX +0.3%. Our news labeler also logged a headline saying Nvidia faces an SEC investigation and has delayed its quarterly 10-Q filing (the detailed quarterly report it must file with regulators). **I could not verify this with a web search, so treat it as unconfirmed.** If it is true, it matters a lot: a late filing coming just days after the CEO resigned would cast doubt over the company's governance.

The equal-weight S&P (RSP, the average stock) is down only 0.6% for the month, while SPY is down 5.5%. Most of the damage is in the big tech names that dominate the index.

Bonds were steady: long Treasuries (TLT) rose 0.8%. Credit is softening but not breaking. The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) rose to 3.43% from 3.31% a month ago. That is a mild warning, not a crisis level.

## 3. The story so far

Our recent summaries trace one thread. A hot October CPI (4.08% yearly, core 2.87%) made a **December Fed rate hike** look fully priced in. The 10-year yield is at **5.10%** and the 2-year at 4.80%. Then Jensen Huang's resignation and the Fed and IMF warnings about the AI boom pushed money out of AI and chips. Our exposure dial fell from 95% to 50% in a week. Nvidia's earnings were supposed to settle that. So far they have not: chips are flat and the possible SEC headline adds a new question.

The economy itself looks fine. Unemployment is 4.1%, jobless claims are 200k, retail sales are up 6.8% a year and payrolls rose 165k. That strength is the problem: a strong economy plus 4% inflation keeps the Fed hawkish (leaning toward higher rates). Stocks that do well when rates are high, such as staples, utilities, big retailers and insurers, keep leading. Expensive growth stocks, which need low rates to justify their prices, keep lagging.

**Both sides:** Bulls can point to a still-rising long-term S&P trend, easy financial conditions (−0.48), strong retail earnings, and one-day jumps like cyber's that show buyers are waiting. Bears can point to only 42% of stocks above their 200-day average, 119 stocks near 52-week lows vs 40 near highs, a falling commodity complex that may signal slowing global demand, and a 5%+ 10-year yield.

## 5. Lesson of the day: real yields and gold

A **real yield** is a bond's interest rate minus expected inflation. Gold pays no interest, so holding it costs you the real yield you could have earned in a Treasury instead. A year ago the 10-year real yield was 1.85%. Today it is 2.89%, so the cost of holding gold has risen a lot. Add a dollar up 6% in three months (gold is priced in dollars), and gold's 20% drop in three months makes more sense. Rising real yields don't sink gold every time, because fear and central-bank buying also matter. But when real yields climb this fast, gold usually struggles.
