# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-10

> The October CPI (consumer price index, the main inflation report) came in hotter than expected. Prices rose 0.4% for the month and core prices rose 0.3% against a 0.2% forecast, and press reports say the 2-year Treasury yield jumped about 17 basis points (0.17 percentage points). A December Fed rate hike now looks fully priced in. Stocks fell modestly (SPY −0.4%, QQQ −0.7%), but the asset types most sensitive to interest rates took the hit: long Treasuries (TLT −1.3%), homebuilders (XHB −2.6%), gold (−2.7%), silver and clean-energy and metals themes. The dollar rose 0.6%. The equal-weight S&P was flat, so the month-long move out of big tech into defensives, financials and the average stock is still in place. Monday's rally was not a turning point, and Nvidia's earnings on Nov 17 are now the next big test.

## 1. What happened

**Fact:** The October inflation report came in hot. Consumer prices rose **0.4%** for the month. Core prices (which leave out food and energy because they jump around) rose **0.3% against a 0.2% forecast**. Our FRED feed shows yearly CPI at **4.08%**, up from 3.72%, and core CPI at 2.87%, up from 2.57%. Press reports say the **2-year Treasury yield jumped about 17 basis points to ~4.73%** (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point). The 2-year yield moves on what traders expect the Fed to do. After the report, futures markets treat a **December Fed rate *hike*** as fully priced in, with a small chance of a half-point move.

Fed officials added to the pressure:
- Goolsbee said services inflation "is not coming down."
- Paulson called further gradual hikes "appropriate."
- Bowman said she is "shifting my focus to inflation."
- Bank of America said the Fed may be falling behind and opened the door to a half-point hike.

**The result:** SPY −0.4%, QQQ −0.7%, DIA −0.3%. Losers beat gainers 2,322 to 1,430, and the median stock fell 0.5%. Reports also blame a second straight drop in Nvidia (around −5.5%) ahead of its Nov 17 earnings.

**Interpretation:** this was not a panic day. It was a **rates day**. The biggest losers were the assets most sensitive to interest rates, not the whole market:
- **Treasuries fell across the curve:** SHY −0.3%, IEF −0.9%, TLT −1.3%. Bond prices fall when yields rise.
- **Homebuilders (XHB) fell 2.6%.** Higher rates mean pricier mortgages.
- **Precious metals fell:** gold (GLD) −2.7% and silver (SLV) −3.5%. A stronger dollar (UUP +0.6%) and higher yields make metals, which pay no interest, less attractive.
- **Long-dated, speculative themes fell:** solar −3.7%, uranium −3.5%, copper miners −3.2%, lithium −2.2%, ARKK −2.4%.

## 2. Where money moved

Defensive and value areas held up. Low volatility (USMV) rose 0.2%, financials (XLF) +0.5%, healthcare (XLV) +0.3%, defense (ITA) +0.9% and energy (XLE) +0.4%. Large value (IWD) was flat while large growth (IWF) fell 0.9%. Tech (XLK) and software (IGV) both fell 1.1%. Software had its own extra problem: a headline said ServiceNow dropped on new Anthropic AI tools, which adds to the worry that AI could disrupt software companies.

The **equal-weight S&P (RSP) finished flat while SPY fell 0.4%**. Equal-weight means every stock counts the same, so RSP shows how the average stock did. The gap means the damage was concentrated in the biggest companies again.

Company stories, all fact from the pack:
- **Hims & Hers −16.6%:** five states joined an FTC privacy lawsuit, on top of a weak reaction to its Q3 results.
- **AAON +19%** and **Ameresco +23%:** Ameresco raised its profit outlook sharply. Both are tied to data-center and energy-efficiency demand.
- **Charter +10.6%:** a rebound after its Q3 results.
- **CoreWeave:** reported a "breakout" quarter and raised guidance, so the companies building AI capacity still report strong business even while chip stocks fall.
- **Bad news among small companies:** GoPro and Microvast warned they may not be able to keep operating (a "going concern" warning), and The Children's Place filed for bankruptcy. iHeartMedia's CEO said advertisers are delaying spending. Interpretation: weaker, debt-heavy companies are struggling as rates stay high.

## 3. The story so far

Over the past month the rotation has been clear and steady. Rotation means money moving from one part of the market to another.
- **Losers:** semiconductors (SMH −17.8%, SOXX −19.1%), tech (XLK −13.9%) and momentum stocks (MTUM −14.8%). Momentum stocks are recent winners, which explains why they fell hardest.
- **Winners:** healthcare (XLV +11.3%), staples (+8.1%), consumer discretionary (+7.7%), financials (+6.3%), airlines (+12.7%) and the equal-weight S&P (+3.5% against SPY −3.6%).

Monday's +2.4% relief rally looked like a bounce, and today confirmed it was only a pause: growth beat value on Monday, then lagged again today.

The bigger backdrop is unusual: **the Fed is moving toward raising rates, not cutting.**
- The fed funds rate is 3.88%.
- Yearly inflation is about 4%, and the Fed's preferred gauge, core PCE, is 3.12%.
- Jobs are solid: 165k added last month, unemployment at 4.1% and jobless claims at only 189k.
- The yield curve (10-year minus 2-year yield) has flattened to 0.25% from 0.44% a month ago, because short-term rates are being pushed up by hike expectations.
- The real 10-year yield (what bonds pay after inflation) is high at 2.66%. That makes expensive growth stocks less attractive, which helps explain why tech and momentum are leading the decline.

**Credit, both sides.** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) is 3.34%, up from 3.11% a month ago, though below the 3.48% we noted on Nov 6. The going-concern warnings fit a picture of tighter money squeezing weaker borrowers. On the other side, the overall financial conditions index is still −0.46 (looser than average), and HYG fell less than Treasuries today. Interpretation: stress is building slowly, but it is not a credit crisis.

**Breadth is quietly improving.** Breadth means how many stocks join a move. 50% of stocks are above their 50-day average, up from 45% a week ago and 29% on Oct 13. This is a weak market for the biggest companies, not for every stock.

## 4. What to watch

- **Thursday after the close: Cisco and Applied Materials.** Applied Materials sells chipmaking equipment, so it will show whether chip spending is holding up after a ~19% monthly drop in chip stocks.
- **Nov 17: Nvidia earnings** (plus Home Depot and Disney). This is the biggest single event for QQQ and SPY.
- **Nov 18: Lowe's, TJX, Target, Ross.** These test the consumer, after strong retail sales (+6.3% yearly) but very weak consumer sentiment (48.1).
- **The 2-year yield and Fed speakers before the Dec 8–9 meeting.** Talk of a 50-basis-point hike would hurt rate-sensitive areas (homebuilders, long bonds, small caps).
- **The junk-bond spread.** A move back above ~3.5% would signal that credit stress is spreading.
- **Open predictions to track:** #5 (SMH lags SPY) looks good so far. #11 (VIXY falls 5%) is under pressure on days like this.

## 5. Lesson of the day: the 2-year yield is the market's Fed forecast

The 2-year Treasury yield mostly reflects where traders think the Fed's rate will be over the next two years. When inflation surprises higher, it jumps first. It rose about 0.17 percentage points today, which is a big one-day move. Assets whose value depends on cash far in the future, or on borrowing, then reprice: long bonds, homebuilders, unprofitable growth companies and gold. That is why today's losses were concentrated rather than spread evenly.
