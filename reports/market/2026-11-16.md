# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-16

> Monday was a broad risk-off day. SPY fell 1.9% and QQQ fell 2.7%, while the chip ETFs dropped more than 5% (SMH −5.1%, SOXX −5.4%) on the day before Nvidia reports earnings under an interim CEO. Over the weekend, Fed Chair Warsh and IMF chief Georgieva both warned about the risks if the AI boom disappoints, and that added to the worry left by Jensen Huang's resignation. Only staples (+0.3%) and utilities (+0.2%) rose, and decliners beat advancers about 3.4 to 1. Our exposure dial fell from 95% a week ago to 50%. The month-long move out of AI and tech into defensive and value stocks got stronger. Nvidia's results Tuesday night are the main event of the week.

## 1. What happened

**Facts from the pack:** SPY (the S&P 500) fell **1.9%** and QQQ (the Nasdaq 100) fell **2.7%**. The chip ETFs did much worse: SMH −5.1% and SOXX −5.4%. Taiwan (EWT −6.8%) and South Korea (EWY −4.4%) also fell hard, because their stock markets are mostly chipmakers. The selling was broad: **849 stocks rose and 2,916 fell**, and the median (middle) stock lost 1.5%. Only consumer staples (XLP +0.3%) and utilities (XLU +0.2%) finished green. Fear rose: VIXY, which tracks volatility futures (a bet on bigger market swings), jumped **9%**.

**Why (the evidence):** There was no single trigger. Several weekend stories pointed the same way:
- **Fed Chair Warsh** described a range of AI outcomes. In the bad case, AI disappoints, stock prices fall and unemployment rises. Coming from the head of the central bank, that hit hard. ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/11/16/fed-warsh-ai-boom-stocks))
- **IMF chief Georgieva** said the global economy is "standing on one leg" and pointed to about $1 trillion of financial-stability risk tied to the AI boom. BlackRock's research arm also warned about what happens if the AI buildout slows.
- **Nvidia** reports Tuesday after the close, a few days after CEO Jensen Huang resigned. A news item in the pack argues that "fallout from last week's news may be more severe than market action suggests." Press reports say the Philadelphia chip index fell about 5.3%, with Intel and Micron each down more than 6%. They also mention nerves ahead of the Anthropic IPO. ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/world/2026/11/17/ai-selloff-deepens-after-huang-exit-chip-index-sinks-53), [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261117/nasdaq-sp-500-fall-over-1-in-run-up-to-nvidia-results-anthropic-debut))
- **Nebius** (an AI cloud company) updated its 2026 revenue guidance, its forecast of future sales, and our news labeler marked it as negative.

**My interpretation:** This looks like investors cutting AI exposure before a big binary event (one with a sharp yes/no outcome, here Nvidia's earnings), on top of fresh doubts voiced by officials. It does not look like a new economic shock. Bonds barely moved and junk bonds slipped only 0.3%.

## 2. Where money moved

- **Out of:** chips, speculative growth (ARKK −4.6%), bitcoin (IBIT −5.5%), metals (copper miners −5.4%, silver −3.9%), uranium (−4.4%) and oil (USO −3.8%). The momentum factor (MTUM −3.3%) took a heavy hit, because it is loaded with last year's AI winners.
- **Into or holding up:** staples, utilities, healthcare (XLV −0.5%), real estate (−0.3%), low-volatility stocks (USMV −0.8%) and airlines (JETS +0.6%, helped by cheaper oil). The equal-weight S&P (RSP), which gives every stock the same weight, fell only 1.0% against SPY's 1.9%. That gap means the biggest companies did most of the damage.
- One headline in the pack sums up the month: *"Wall Street sold chips, bought Coca-Cola at the strongest pace since September 2001."*
- **Single stocks:** Corning (GLW) announced a large optical-glass order from Nvidia, and Dollar General raised its outlook. In healthcare, CG Oncology rose 14% on a successful Phase 3 trial (the large final-stage test before approval), while Summit Therapeutics fell 16.5% after the FDA rejected its application for ivonescimab in its current form.

## 3. The story so far

The big rotation (money moving from one group of stocks to another) is a month old and got stronger today. Over 20 trading days:
- Tech (XLK) is down 11.4% and chips (SMH) are down 15.8%.
- Healthcare (+7.0%), utilities (+6.7%), staples (+6.2%) and financials (+5.7%) are all up.
- Growth trails value by **11 percentage points**, and cyclicals (stocks tied to the economic cycle) trail defensives by 10.5 points.

Last week's rallies (Nov 9, Nov 11) were **bounces inside a downtrend**, as we said at the time. In one week, the share of stocks above their 50-day average (the average closing price of the last 50 days) fell from 52% to 43%. Our exposure dial, the system's rule-based suggestion for how much to keep invested, fell from 95% to 50%.

**The rates backdrop still matters.** The 10-year Treasury yield is **5.15%**, and the real yield (after inflation) is **2.91%**, up a full point from a year ago. Headline CPI inflation is 4.08%, pushed up by oil around $90, and markets expect a December Fed hike. High real yields make expensive growth stocks less attractive, because their value depends on profits far in the future.

**The calm side:** credit is not panicking. The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) is 3.23%, lower than the 3.48% we noted on Nov 6. Financial conditions (−0.46) are still loose, jobless claims are low (200k) and unemployment is 4.1%. So far this is a valuation and AI-confidence problem, not a credit or recession problem. If spreads start widening, that would change.

**The bull case:** big tech is now much cheaper than a month ago, and sentiment is very negative. A solid Nvidia report could set off a sharp short-covering rally, like the one on Nov 11 after CoreWeave's results.

## 4. What to watch

- **Nvidia (Tue after close):** watch data-center revenue, guidance, and any comments on leadership or customer demand. The stock moves the whole market: the move on Wednesday will decide whether chips stabilize or break lower.
- **Retailers:** HD and DIS (Tue), TJX, LOW and TGT (Wed), and WMT (Thu). These are a check on the US consumer while sentiment sits at a weak 48.
- **Junk-bond spread and HYG:** a move above roughly 3.5% would mean the stress is spreading from stocks into credit.
- **Breadth:** can the share of stocks above their 50-day average hold around 40%? The October low was 28%.
- **Fed speakers:** more talk of a December hike (Hammack spoke today about price pass-through, meaning businesses raising prices to cover their own higher costs).

## 5. Lesson of the day

**Pre-earnings de-risking.** Before a very important company reports, many funds trim positions in that stock and in related stocks so they aren't caught by a big surprise. That can push prices down *before* the news, as it did with chips today. The flip side: if the report is merely "fine", the selling pressure is gone, and stocks can jump even on ordinary numbers. A drop before earnings tells you about positioning (how investors were holding the stock), not about the results themselves.

*Fact vs interpretation:* the price moves, breadth and macro numbers are facts from our pack. The weekend comments and the chip-index move come from press reports. The claim that today's selling was mainly de-risking before Nvidia is my interpretation.
