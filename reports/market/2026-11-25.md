# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-25

> Wednesday before Thanksgiving looked like a crash at dawn and ended as a small loss. Nasdaq futures were down about 3% before the open as chip selling continued, and October core PCE inflation came in hot at 3.3%. SPY closed only 0.3% lower, the Dow rose 0.3%, and the average stock gained 0.8% (RSP), with about 2.2 stocks rising for every one that fell. Nvidia fell another ~3.9% to about $149, and Ambarella (−19.8%) and Nutanix (−15.2%) sank on weak outlooks, while homebuilders, materials and mid caps led. The rotation is unchanged: money keeps leaving crowded AI and growth trades for the rest of the market. Meanwhile, a near-certain December Fed rate hike, a junk-bond spread of 3.72% and distress pricing in CoreWeave's credit keep the regime Defensive at a 25% exposure dial.

## 1. What happened

**Fact:** Early trading looked ugly. Before the open, Nasdaq 100 futures were down about 2.9% and S&P 500 futures about 2.0%. (Futures are contracts that trade before the market opens and show where traders expect prices to start.) Nvidia was down 5–6% pre-market after Tuesday's 5.9% drop. News reports cite worries about AI debt and financing costs and a broker cutting Micron's price target, and one source mentions a new tariff proposal. The chip selling had begun before the 8:30 am inflation report, so that report did not start it.

At 8:30 the October **PCE** report came out. PCE is the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. **Core PCE**, which leaves out food and energy, rose 3.3% from a year earlier. Forecasters expected 3.2%, and it is the highest reading since 2023. Weekly jobless claims fell to 189,000, a very low number. A strong job market plus sticky inflation is why markets now treat a December 8–9 **rate hike** (the Fed raising its benchmark interest rate) as close to certain.

Then buyers stepped in. SPY finished **−0.3%**, QQQ −0.7%, and the Dow ended **+0.3%** after being down early. Nvidia still closed about **−3.9% near $149.30**. That is below Monday's $165 and below the roughly $155 level from mid-November. Under the surface the day was healthy: **2,570 stocks rose and 1,194 fell**, and the median stock gained 0.8%.

Company news:
- **Ambarella (AMBA) −19.8%** on 8.3× normal volume. Its revenue guidance fell far short, and it blamed a memory-chip shortage.
- **Nutanix (NTNX) −15.2%** on revenue guidance below forecasts plus a CEO succession plan.
- **Deere (DE) rose about 4.4%** even though earnings missed and its 2027 outlook was soft. When a stock rises on bad news, it often means investors had already expected the worst.
- **HP Inc** rose about 11% after its results. This is a different company from Hewlett Packard Enterprise.

## 2. Where money moved

- **Out of AI:** semiconductors fell again (SMH −1.0%), as did software (IGV −0.4%) and Taiwan and South Korea (EWT −1.3%, EWY −1.2%). Over the past month SMH is down 25.6% and XLK 18.4%.
- **Into the rest of the market:** homebuilders XHB +2.4%, materials XLB +2.1%, mid caps MDY +1.2%, equal-weight RSP +0.8% and small caps IWM +0.7%. Every major sector except tech, healthcare and discretionary rose.
- **Bonds rallied despite hot inflation:** long Treasuries TLT +0.9% and 10-year Treasuries IEF +0.5%. **Interpretation:** the market may believe a Fed hike will slow the economy and cool inflation later. One clue is that the 10-year expected-inflation rate has fallen to 2.12% from 2.34% a month ago. An equally plausible explanation is safe-haven buying, or thin trading before the holiday. One day is not enough to tell.
- **Oil bounced:** USO rose 3.3%, but it is still down 16% for the month. Gold and silver edged up after heavy losses.
- **Fear stayed high:** VIXY, which tracks expected stock swings, rose 4.4%. Traders still want protection.

## 3. The story so far

A rough timeline:
- Nov 19–20: Nvidia's accounting scandal triggered a correction, meaning a fall of at least 10% from the high.
- Nov 23: SPY jumped 4.7% when Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO.
- Nov 24–25: the market split. Chips gave back much of Monday's gain while the average stock kept rising.

The table's "Week" column (SPY −1.8%) covers the last five trading days, so it includes last Thursday and Friday's drops. Measured from Friday's close, SPY is up about 4%.

Three things matter more than any single day:

1. **Leadership has flipped.** Over the past month RSP is 6.4 points ahead of SPY, value (IWD) is 5.7 points ahead, and staples, utilities and real estate lead. Momentum (MTUM, −18.9%) is the worst factor. The stocks that led on the way up are leading on the way down.
2. **Rates are a headwind.** As of Tuesday the 10-year Treasury yield was 4.91%, and the **real yield** (the yield after expected inflation) was 2.79%, up from 1.81% a year ago. The dollar is strong too.
3. **Credit is getting nervous at the edges.** As of Tuesday the junk-bond spread, the extra interest risky companies pay over the government, was 3.72%. That is up from 3.38% a month ago, but still far from crisis levels (above 5–6%). Our news feed flagged that CoreWeave's **credit default swaps** (insurance against a company not repaying its debt) are priced at distressed levels, even as banks arrange a $4.5B loan backed by its GPUs. AI's problems are moving from stock prices into debt markets. That is worth watching, though it has not spread across credit markets.

Breadth is still weak: only 41% of stocks are above their 200-day average (the average closing price over roughly 10 months) and 39% above their 50-day. The regime stays **Defensive** with the exposure dial at 25%. **Bull case:** the rally spreading beyond AI is what a healthy market looks like, and chips have already fallen about 25%. **Bear case:** the index's biggest weights are still falling, and a rate hike into a correction rarely helps.

## 5. Lesson of the day: real yield

The **real yield** is a bond's interest rate minus expected inflation. It is the return you earn in actual buying power. Today the 10-year pays 4.91% and markets expect about 2.12% inflation, which leaves about 2.79% real. A year ago it was 1.81%.

This matters most for growth stocks. Their value depends on profits expected many years from now. When a safe Treasury pays nearly 3% above inflation, investors want more return before they pay up for those far-off profits, so the price they will pay falls. That is part of why rate-sensitive, high-multiple stocks (QQQ, ARKK, software) are hurting the most. A high multiple means a high price compared with current earnings.
