## 1. What happened

**Fact (pack):** SPY, the S&P 500 fund, fell **0.3%**, and the Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) fell **0.7%**. The average stock had a good day, though. The equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP, which gives every company the same weight) rose **0.8%**, **2,569 stocks rose and 1,195 fell**, and the median stock gained **0.8%**. When the index falls but most stocks rise, a few giant companies are pulling the index down. Here those were mostly chip and AI names.

**Fact (news reports, not in our pack):** The closing numbers hide a wild day. Reports say the S&P 500 was down as much as **3.2%** during the session and recovered almost all of it, closing at 7,182.78. Two triggers are cited:
- **Inflation:** October core PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation measure, excluding food and energy) rose **3.3%** from a year earlier, above the 3.2% economists expected. Our pack shows **3.26%**, up from 3.14%.
- **Tariffs:** The White House proposed new import taxes on major trading partners. Reports differ on whether 14 or 16 countries are covered.

The VIX (the fear index) reportedly came close to 35 during the day before closing near 28. Our volatility fund VIXY rose **4.4%** (+14.1% this week), so fear eased from its peak but did not disappear.

**Nvidia** fell again. One report has it down 3.9% to $149.30, while Reuters described a drop of roughly 6%. Several reports also say S&P Global put Nvidia's credit rating under review for a possible downgrade, citing management and board issues. I could not confirm the exact wording. The chip ETFs fell again (SMH −1.0%, SOXX −0.5%), and one report says the main chip index is now **40% below its June peak**.

**Interpretation:** I can't say for sure *why* stocks recovered. "Bargain hunters" is a headline, not evidence. One odd detail: Treasuries rallied (TLT +0.9%, IEF +0.5%) on a *hot* inflation report. Reports put the 10-year yield near 4.85%, its eighth straight daily decline. Our FRED data shows 4.91% for Tuesday and 5.00% a month ago. Normally hot inflation pushes yields up. My reading is that bond buyers now care more about slower growth and seeking safety than about inflation. Expected 10-year inflation in our pack fell from 2.34% to 2.12% in a month, which fits that reading.

Breadth (how many stocks take part) is still weak: **41% of stocks are above their 200-day average** (the long-term trend line) and 39% above their 50-day. The dial reads **25%**. That is the system's rule-based setting, not advice.

**Bull case:** Credit is still far from crisis levels, the average stock is holding up, and bad news was bought today. **Bear case:** Spreads keep widening, the Nasdaq 100 reportedly touched the −20% "bear market" line during the day, and a Fed hike plus new tariffs is a tough mix for expensive growth stocks.

## 5. Lesson of the day: intraday reversal

An **intraday reversal** is when a market falls (or rises) sharply during a session and then closes near where it opened. Today, a 3.2% drop turned into a 0.25% loss. Traders often read this as a sign that sellers ran out of steam, because buyers absorbed the selling. It can be a first step toward a bottom. But one reversal proves nothing. After big reversals, markets often retest the lows within days, especially when the underlying problem (here, AI credit worries and inflation) hasn't been solved. A useful habit: look for a second sign, such as breadth improving for several days or credit spreads narrowing, before trusting it.
