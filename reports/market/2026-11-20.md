# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-20

> Friday, November 20 was the worst day of the AI sell-off so far. SPY fell 3.6% (−8.6% for the week), QQQ fell 5.7%, and the chip ETFs fell more than 10% (SMH −10.6%). News reports say Nvidia dropped about 14% to roughly $125.5 after Reuters reported an SEC investigation into its accounting. Reuters says the S&P 500 and the Dow both closed in correction territory, meaning at least 10% below their highs. The selling was broad: 565 stocks rose and 3,220 fell, 26% of stocks are above their 50-day average, and our exposure dial stays at 25%. Only consumer staples rose among the sectors, while Treasuries rallied and junk-bond spreads widened to 3.52%. That widening is a warning but not a crisis yet. This is now a full correction led by the AI trade, with defensive stocks and Treasuries holding up while gold, silver and bitcoin were sold along with stocks.

## 1. What happened

**Facts from the pack:** SPY fell **3.6%** today and **8.6%** this week. QQQ (Nasdaq 100) fell **5.7%**, technology (XLK) fell **7.3%**, and both chip ETFs fell more than 10% (SMH **−10.6%**, SOXX **−10.5%**). Taiwan's ETF (EWT, which is mostly TSMC and other chip suppliers) fell **10.9%**, and South Korea's (EWY) fell **8.4%**. Breadth (how many stocks join a move) was terrible again: **565 up, 3,220 down**, and the median stock fell 2.4%. Only **16** stocks were near 52-week highs, against **373** near 52-week lows. The fear gauge (VIXY, which tracks VIX futures) rose **13.4%** today and **44.6%** this week.

**Why (with evidence):** News reports say Nvidia fell about **14%** and closed near **$125.5**. It was already down about 6% earlier in the day, and the drop sped up after **Reuters reported that the SEC is investigating Nvidia's accounting**. The SEC declined to comment. ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/20/nvidia-stock-plunge-wipes-out-nearly-usd500-billion-in-one-day-as-sec-reportedly-probes-its-accounting/), [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9213528/nvidia-nvda-stock-drops-14-hits-lowest-level-since-may-2025)) On Wednesday our news feed had flagged an *unconfirmed* report of an SEC probe and a late 10-Q filing (the required quarterly report to regulators). That story now looks much more real. It also came on top of Jensen Huang's resignation and a California subpoena reported Thursday. According to [Reuters via the Star-Advertiser](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/11/20/breaking-news/wall-street-rout-pushes-sp-500-dow-into-correction/) and [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/11/20/stocks-dow-jones-nasdaq-correction-territory/1061795214998/), the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in a **correction** (at least 10% below their highs), and the Nasdaq 100 briefly fell into bear-market territory (20% below its high) during the day. Newly listed Anthropic reportedly gave back much of its first-day gain.

**My interpretation:** When the company at the center of the market's biggest theme faces both a leadership crisis and an accounting question, investors stop debating its valuation and start asking whether they can trust its numbers. That would explain why Nvidia's strong earnings on Tuesday gave no lasting support.

## 2. Where money moved

- **Into safety:** Consumer staples (XLP **+0.7%**) was the only sector that rose. Healthcare (XLV −0.1%) and utilities (XLU −0.7%) barely fell. Long-term Treasuries rose (TLT **+1.4%**, IEF +0.9%), and the 10-year yield has slipped from 5.12% a month ago to 5.04%.
- **Out of everything "hot":** Momentum stocks (MTUM −5.7%), large growth (IWF −6.0%), speculative growth (ARKK −6.8%), uranium (−6.6%), solar (−7.3%) and bitcoin (IBIT −5.9%; bitcoin miners MARA −18.6% and IREN −18.0%).
- **Gold (−3.7%) and silver (−6.7%) also fell.** This is my interpretation: when stocks drop this fast, some investors sell what they can (often their winners) to raise cash or meet margin calls (demands to add money to borrowed positions). This is the second day in a row that gold failed to act as a safe place to hide.
- **The equal-weight S&P (RSP −2.0%) beat SPY by 1.6 points.** The biggest companies are now doing the most damage, which is the reverse of the past two years.
- **A few bright spots:** Retailers with good earnings rose (GAP +11.4%, Buckle +5.7%; Target, TJX and Ross raised their outlooks). Global Payments rose 9.9% on takeover talk.

## 3. The story so far

A week ago (Nov 13) SPY was close to its highs, 47% of stocks were above their 50-day average, and our exposure dial read 95%. Since then the market has had **Huang's resignation → Fed Chair Warsh warning about AI-bubble risk → Nvidia's earnings beat that didn't hold → Walmart's beat that didn't help → today's SEC report.** Now only **26%** of stocks are above their 50-day average and **34%** are above their 200-day. The dial reads 25%.

The month-long numbers show how big the rotation has been. Over 20 days, growth trails value by **11.1 points** and cyclicals trail defensives by **11.0 points**. Staples are **+2.5%** for the month while SPY is **−9.2%**.

The macro backdrop leaves the Fed little room to help. CPI inflation is **4.08%**, core PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge) is **3.12%**, unemployment is a low **4.1%**, and a December rate *hike* is expected. That makes this unlike sell-offs where the Fed steps in to cut rates. One piece of good news: oil has fallen from about $108 to **$89.5**, and 10-year expected inflation has dropped from 2.37% to **2.15%**. That could ease the Fed's worry later on.

**Credit check:** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) widened from 3.33% to **3.52%**, but HYG fell only 0.3% today. Credit is starting to worry but is not panicking. If that changes, this becomes a bigger problem than a stock correction.

**Scorekeeping, honestly:** Prediction #26 (Nvidia at least 2% above $155.46 after 5 days) will almost certainly fail, since the stock is about 19% below that level. Most of our defensive-tilt predictions (XLP, XLV, XLU, USMV and RSP beating SPY) are working so far.

## 4. What to watch

- **Nvidia's response:** whether it confirms or denies the SEC probe, gives news on the late 10-Q, or names a permanent CEO. Clarity, even bad news, often stops forced selling.
- **Credit:** whether the junk spread rises above about 4% or HYG falls more than 1% in a day. Either would mean the stress is spreading beyond stocks.
- **Earnings next week:** ADI (an analog chipmaker, less tied to AI) on Tuesday morning, Workday, Autodesk and HP after the close Tuesday, and Deere on Wednesday. Next week is also Thanksgiving week, which usually means thin trading and larger price swings.
- **Signs of a washout:** a day where 80–90% of stocks rise after this streak (a "breadth thrust") would be the first real sign of a bottom. One bounce day alone is not.

## 5. Lesson of the day: correction vs. bear market

A **correction** is a drop of at least 10% from a recent high, and a **bear market** is a drop of at least 20%. These are labels, not forecasts. Corrections happen about once every year or two and usually don't become bear markets. The ones that do usually come with a recession or a credit squeeze. That is why the junk-bond spread and jobless claims (still low, at **203,000**) matter as much as the size of today's drop. For multi-week holds, a correction mostly changes **position size and patience**: our rules cut exposure as breadth breaks down and wait for breadth to recover before adding back. Both sides deserve a hearing. Corrections have often been good long-run entry points, and they can also run much further than feels reasonable while they're happening.
