# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-09

> Stocks had a broad relief rally: SPY +2.4%, QQQ +3.5%, SOXX +7.5%, with five stocks rising for every one that fell. Press reports credit a rebound after chips' worst week since April 2025, plus oil falling as Iran tensions eased, though Nvidia fell on its own news. The rally mostly undid last week's damage: SPY is flat over five days and still down 3.3% in a month, while QQQ is down 8.9%. The month-long shift from big tech into healthcare, consumer stocks, financials and bonds is still intact after one day. Tuesday's CPI report, which will have no official yearly figure, is the first test, followed by Cisco and Applied Materials on Thursday and Nvidia on Nov 17.

## 1. What happened

**Facts.** It was a strong day almost everywhere. SPY (the S&P 500 fund) rose 2.4%, QQQ (Nasdaq 100) rose 3.5%, and small-cap IWM rose 3.2%. Breadth, meaning how many stocks took part, was excellent: 3,142 up against 627 down, with the median stock up 2.4%. VIXY, a fund that rises when investors pay up for crash protection, fell 13.4%. One market recap called it the S&P's and Nasdaq's best day since April.

**Why, according to press reports:**
- **Chips bounced from a rout.** The Philadelphia chip index rose more than 6% after falling more than 12% last week, its worst week since April 2025. AMD gained about 13% and Broadcom about 9%. Morgan Stanley and UBS both called the AI selloff overdone.
- **Oil fell on Iran.** Trump said he sees "positive momentum" toward ending the conflict, and reports said he signaled new strikes were unlikely. USO (crude oil fund) fell 3.9% and is down 9.1% on the week. Cheaper oil eases inflation fears, which is the link Reuters drew. Our own headlines also show the threat isn't gone: he said the US is "ready for strikes if necessary."
- **Nvidia went the other way.** Reports tied its drop to a US investigation connected to its CEO. I only saw this secondhand, so treat the details as unconfirmed.

**My interpretation.** This looks like a relief rally, a sharp bounce when selling pressure runs out, rather than a change in the trend. Bonds barely moved (TLT +0.1%, IEF −0.1%), so falling interest rates did not power today's gain. It was mostly investors becoming willing to own risk again.

## 2. Where money moved

**Today, the most beaten-down groups bounced hardest.**
- Uranium (URA) +6.2%, copper miners (COPX) +5.6%, Taiwan (EWT) +7.2%
- Momentum (MTUM, a fund of recent winners, which lately means AI names) +4.9%
- Airlines (JETS) +5.1%, helped by cheaper fuel

**The laggards were the safe havens and oil.** Energy (XLE) rose just 0.5%, and oil producers (XOP) only 0.2%, leaving XOP down 10% on the week. Staples (XLP) rose 0.9%.

**The monthly picture barely changed:**

RSP, which holds every S&P stock in equal size, is up 3.6% while SPY is down 3.3%. The average stock is doing fine, and the giants are what's hurting.

**A warning on healthcare.** XLV's +9.5% hides a split. The median healthcare stock is up only 1.9% and just 48% are above their 50-day average, so large companies are doing the lifting. Healthcare also fills 9 of our 15 leader slots and most of today's worst drops: Zura −69.7% on a failed trial, Janux −27.6%, Acadia −22.3%, Ionis −21.4%. Single-trial biotech risk stays huge even when the sector is in favor.

## 3. The story so far

After Friday's small bounce we wrote that one green day doesn't prove a turn. That still applies to today, with one upgrade: this bounce was far broader. The share of stocks above their 50-day average is 52%, up from 28% on Oct 12. Still, only 89 stocks are near 52-week highs, and SMH (chips) is down 4.1% on the week even after today's jump.

The AI selloff has a political layer too. Reports link part of it to Democratic midterm wins raising fears of AI regulation, and our headlines show Sen. Warren promising scrutiny as Senate Banking chair. That is a slower-moving worry than a one-week panic, and a bounce doesn't remove it.

The macro picture is mixed:
- **Rates:** The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.86% from 5.28% a month ago, which helps homebuilders (XHB +12.7% on the month). But the 2-year yield, at 4.51%, sits well above the Fed's 3.88% rate, which I read as markets leaning toward rate hikes. The Fed's Barkin said the risk "lies on the inflation side."
- **Economy:** Jobs are solid (165k added, 4.1% unemployment, claims at 189,000). Yearly inflation is 3.72% and core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, is 3.12%. Consumer sentiment is very weak at 48.1, even though retail sales are up 6.29% on the year.
- **Credit:** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) eased to 3.42% from 3.48% but is still above 3.09% a month ago. This is improving but not resolved.

Our exposure dial reads 95%, up from 50% a week ago. It's a fast-moving rule, so the jump says conditions improved. It doesn't say the risk is over.

## 4. What to watch

- **Tuesday 8:30 am ET, CPI.** Economists expect +0.3% for the month and +0.2% core, down from 0.6% and 0.3%. The government **won't publish the official yearly figures**, because October 2025 prices were never collected during the 43-day shutdown. Estimates of the yearly rate run near 4%. Our pack's yearly CPI line may be blank or misleading next month.
- **Earnings:** Cisco and Applied Materials on Thursday, then Nvidia and Home Depot on Nov 17. Nvidia is the real test of the AI story.
- **Iran and oil:** Headlines have flipped repeatedly. A reversal would hurt today's winners, especially airlines.
- **Follow-through:** Does the share of stocks above their 50-day stay over 50%, and does junk-bond stress keep easing?
- **Bull case vs bear case:** Bulls see an overdone panic, solid jobs and falling yields. Bears see a downtrend in tech, sticky inflation and a Fed that may hike.
- **Data note:** Some market caps in our earnings table look wrong (AKTX at $1,193B, EC at $642B), probably a data or currency error. Our WTI oil figure ($99.64) is also from Nov 3. One report put WTI near $86 today, but that doesn't fully match USO's move, so I'd treat it loosely.

## 5. Lesson of the day

Two chip funds, same day: SOXX +7.5%, SMH +5.1%. Both own semiconductors, but they hold them in different amounts. SMH carries a much larger slice of Nvidia, which fell today, so it rose less (my inference from the weights). Over the past month SOXX fell further (−19.2% vs −17.5%), so the gap cuts both ways. Before trusting a fund's label, check its top holdings.
