## 1. What happened

**Fact:** The S&P 500 (SPY) edged up **+0.3%**. The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) rose **+1.4%** and the beaten-down chip ETFs jumped (SMH **+3.6%**, SOXX **+3.5%**). Chip-heavy foreign markets bounced too: South Korea (EWY) **+4.1%** and Taiwan (EWT) **+3.9%**. Speculative names rose with them: ARKK **+3.2%**, uranium (URA) **+4.4%**, and quantum-computing stocks IonQ **+23%** and Quantinuum **+26%**, both after beating Q3 expectations.

Meanwhile the "safe" groups that had been winning slipped: consumer staples (XLP) **−1.3%**, the equal-weight S&P (RSP) **−0.4%**, and the Dow (DIA) **−0.4%**. Under the surface the day was barely positive: 2,040 stocks rose and 1,729 fell, and the median stock gained just **+0.2%**.

**Interpretation:** This looks like a *relief rally*, a short bounce in whatever fell hardest. That makes sense after SMH lost **11.2%** this week and **23%** this month. My web search found no single headline behind the chip bounce, so treat the cause as unconfirmed. Two pieces of news worked against AI stocks today. Bloomberg reported that **Microsoft is winding down its deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle**. A research firm also warned that AI stocks could fall further even with strong GPU demand. Bouncing despite that news suggests the move was mostly positioning (short-sellers buying back, bargain hunters stepping in), not a new bullish story.

**Macro:** Today brought October's jobs report. According to our FRED data, employers added **165k** jobs (up from 60k), unemployment fell to **4.1%**, and weekly jobless claims are very low at **189k**. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly called it good news. She also said inflation is "more stubborn than we'd like" and that the Fed "will have to tighten policy further," meaning raise rates. Headline inflation is **3.72%**, pushed up by oil near **$100** compared with $62 a year ago. Core inflation (without food and energy) is a calmer **2.57%**. Daly also described the recent painful volatility as "the kind of rebalancing you would hope for." My reading is that the Fed isn't about to rescue falling tech stocks.

## 3. The story so far

This is our first journal entry, so here is the setup.

**The index is hiding the real picture.** SPY is down **5.6%** this month, but the average stock (RSP) is **up 1.4%**. That gap of about **7 points** shows that a handful of giant tech and AI companies are dragging the cap-weighted index down while most of the market holds up. The median stock is only **−0.2%** over 20 days.

**But participation is mediocre, not healthy.** Only **45%** of stocks are above their 200-day average (the long-term trend line) and **43%** above their 50-day average (the medium-term trend line). We count **44** stocks near 52-week highs and **44** near lows. Our rule-based exposure dial (how much of a portfolio our rules say to keep invested) fell from **95% on Oct 9 to 50%** now.

**Risk gauges are flashing amber.** The junk-bond spread (the extra interest risky companies pay over the government) widened from **3.03% to 3.48%** in a month. When that spread widens, lenders are getting nervous. VIXY, a fear gauge, is up **29%** this month. Consumer sentiment sits at a gloomy **48.1**. In contrast, the job market is strong and financial conditions are still easy (**−0.46**, where below zero means easy money).

**My overall interpretation:** This looks more like a punishing correction in a crowded AI trade than an economy-wide recession scare, but credit spreads deserve close attention. The bull case is that strong jobs, falling yields and a broad market holding up leave room for a healthy rebalancing. The bear case is that a hawkish Fed, wider credit spreads and cracks in AI spending (the Microsoft–Oracle report) could turn a tech correction into something broader.

## 4. What to watch

- **Does the chip bounce hold?** One +3.6% day after a −23% month proves little. Watch whether SMH can string together several up days on rising volume.
- **The junk-bond spread:** a move above ~3.75% would be a warning that stress is spreading beyond tech.
- **Fed speakers and the next CPI report:** more "tighten further" talk would pressure growth stocks again.
- **Oil and Iran headlines:** a lasting drop in oil would ease headline inflation, which helps bonds, consumers and airlines (JETS **+6.7%** this week).
- **AI capex news:** any more hyperscalers (the giant cloud companies) pulling back on rented compute, like Microsoft with Oracle.

## 5. Lesson of the day

**Cap-weighted vs. equal-weighted indexes.** SPY weights each company by its total market value, so a few trillion-dollar tech firms drive much of its move. RSP holds the same 500 companies at equal weight, so it shows how the *typical* company is doing. This month SPY is down **5.6%** while RSP is up **1.4%**. "The market is down" really means "the biggest stocks are down." Comparing the two is one of the easiest ways to see whether a selloff is broad or concentrated.
