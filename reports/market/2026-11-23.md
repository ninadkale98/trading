# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-23

> Monday was one of the biggest up days in years. SPY rose 4.7%, QQQ 7.0% and the chip ETF SMH 15.0% after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, a move news reports tie partly to pressure from the White House. Nvidia rose 31.5% to $165.05, its biggest one-day gain since 2003. The rally was wide, with 3,274 stocks up and 524 down, but it was led by giant stocks: the average stock (RSP +2.6%) did about half as well as the index, and junk bonds barely moved (HYG +0.2%). The market is mostly back to where it was a week ago, not out of the correction. Our regime is still Defensive, with the dial at 25% and only 38% of stocks above their 200-day average.

## 1. What happened

**Fact:** Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as President and CEO, effective immediately (Nvidia 8-K filing dated Nov 23). Colette Kress had been interim CEO since he resigned earlier this month. CNN and Business Recorder report the reversal came after pressure from President Trump. That is their reporting, not something our data can confirm. Nvidia closed up **31.5% at $165.05**, adding nearly $950B in market value. It was the stock's best day since 2003.

That one event lifted the whole market:
- **SPY** (S&P 500 ETF) **+4.7%**
- **QQQ** (Nasdaq 100) **+7.0%**
- **SMH** (semiconductors) **+15.0%**
- **XLK** (tech sector) **+9.0%**
- Chip-heavy Asian funds caught up during US hours: **Taiwan EWT +18.0%** and **South Korea EWY +10.1%**.
- The fear gauge **VIXY fell 13%**.

The rally reached most stocks: **3,274 rose and 524 fell**, and the median stock gained 2.8%.

**What did not change:** the SEC's civil probe into Nvidia's disclosures is still open. Oppenheimer also downgraded Nvidia to "Perform" (roughly neutral). One economist quoted by CNN called the day a possible "dead cat bounce", meaning a short rebound inside a falling trend.

Other news:
- **CrowdStrike** said it will buy **Zscaler** for $43.9B in stock (1.135 CRWD shares per ZS share). CRWD fell 8.9% on 4.6× normal volume. Buyers often drop when they pay with their own shares, because arbitrage traders (who bet on deals closing) short the buyer.
- **Capricor (CAPR) fell 74.7%** and **Oatly (OTLY) rose 109%**. Our feed has no clear cause for either, so treat them as unexplained.

## 2. Where money moved

**Interpretation:** this was a classic "short-covering" rebound. That is when traders who bet on falling prices rush to buy back. It is visible in the pattern: the groups hit hardest last week bounced the most.
- Momentum stocks: **MTUM +6.1%**
- Speculative growth: **ARKK +8.0%**
- Cloud: **SKYY +6.4%**
- Copper miners: **COPX +6.8%**

Defensive groups trailed:
- Consumer staples: **XLP −0.1%**
- Utilities: **XLU +1.5%**
- Healthcare: **XLV +1.2%**

Energy fell: **XLE −1.1%**, oil (**USO**) **−4.5%**. We don't have a confirmed reason for today's oil drop. Last week's declines were reported as being driven by a coming supply surplus.

**Three warning signs inside the rally:**
1. **It was top-heavy.** RSP (equal-weight S&P, the "average stock") rose only 2.6%, against SPY's 4.7%. A few giant tech names did most of the lifting.
2. **Credit barely reacted.** HYG (junk bonds) rose only 0.2%. When stock buyers truly believe the worst is over, junk bonds usually rally too. The last reading of the junk-bond spread was **3.76%** (Nov 20, before today), up from 3.27% a month earlier. The spread is the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries.
3. **Treasuries didn't sell off.** TLT (long-term Treasuries) still rose 0.3%. Bond investors did not rotate out of safety.

- **Follow-through:** does the rally hold for 2–3 days, and does RSP start keeping up with SPY? A rebound that holds and lifts the average stock is far stronger than one that fades.
- **Credit:** HYG and the junk spread need to improve to confirm the rally.
- **Nvidia news:** any update on the SEC probe, or more analyst moves after today's Oppenheimer downgrade.

## 5. Lesson of the day

**Bear-market rallies.** Some of the biggest one-day gains ever came during falling markets: October 2008, and March 2020 before the low. Sellers are exhausted, short sellers rush to buy back, and any good news gets an outsized reaction. One huge up day tells you fear has eased. It does not tell you the trend has changed. More useful checks:
- Do gains keep coming over the next several days?
- Does the *average* stock join in (equal-weight versus cap-weight)?
- Do credit markets confirm?

Today passed the "most stocks up" test but failed the other two so far.
