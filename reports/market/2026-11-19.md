# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-19

> Thursday was the worst day for breadth in this sell-off. SPY fell 2.0% for its fifth straight loss, and about 6.5 stocks fell for every one that rose (504 up, 3,263 down). Strong results from Nvidia (Tuesday night) and Walmart (this morning) did not stop the selling. Both stocks reportedly closed lower, and Taiwan's ETF fell 5.4% as the AI sell-off spread through Asia. Almost nothing was safe: staples, utilities, gold and silver all fell, and only Treasuries, oil, India and cybersecurity edged up. Our exposure dial dropped from 95% a week ago to 25%. Credit markets are still calm, though (junk ETF −0.2%), so this looks like a stock-market correction rather than a financial crisis so far.

## 1. What happened

**Facts from our pack:** SPY (S&P 500) fell **2.0%**, QQQ (Nasdaq 100) **2.2%**, and small caps (IWM) **2.2%**. SPY is now down **5.5% this week and 6.3% over the month**, and this was its fifth losing day in a row. Breadth, meaning how many stocks join a move, was the worst of the decline: **504 stocks rose and 3,263 fell**, about 6.5 losers per winner. The median stock fell 1.9% today and is down 5.2% over 20 days. Only **38%** of stocks are above their 200-day average (their long-term trend line), and only **32%** are above their 50-day average. A week ago both figures were 47%. Our rule-based exposure dial fell from **95% on Nov 12 to 25%** today. The fear gauge ETF (VIXY) rose **12.2%** today and 30.7% this week.

**Why (with evidence):**
- **Good news didn't help.** Nvidia beat estimates Tuesday night. News recaps (not our pack) say it erased an early gain of about 4.7% and closed down about **3.3%**, its sixth straight loss. Our news feed flagged analyst downgrades after Jensen Huang's resignation and a reported **SEC investigation** with a delayed quarterly filing. Press reports confirm the SEC probe, but I could not confirm the filing delay from a primary source. Walmart beat estimates and raised its full-year guidance, yet reportedly slipped about 1%. Williams-Sonoma fell **15.2%** after its results.
- **The AI sell-off went global.** Taiwan's ETF (EWT) fell **5.4%**. Reports say Taiwan's market fell about 4.5% overnight, with chip and circuit-board stocks hitting their daily limits, and that the local regulator restricted short selling starting Nov 20. Semiconductor ETFs fell again (SMH −2.6%, SOXX −2.8%) and are down about 11% this week.
- **A new place for AI money.** Our headlines show Anthropic listed on Nasdaq today. Reports say its first-day jump faded. *My interpretation:* a big new AI stock gives investors something to sell other AI names to buy, but I can't measure that effect.

## 2. Where money moved

Unlike Monday, nothing was a real shelter. Staples fell 1.5%, utilities 1.2%, and low-volatility stocks (USMV) 1.5%. Yesterday's retail rally fully reversed: retail ETF XRT went from +2.3% to **−2.8%**, and consumer discretionary (XLY) fell 2.8%. Gold (−1.8%) and silver (−2.6%) fell with stocks. That often happens in sharp sell-offs when investors sell what they can to raise cash, and a strong dollar plus a high real yield (yield after inflation) of 2.88% doesn't help gold either.

Only a few things rose: short and long Treasuries (+0.1% to +0.2%), oil (USO +1.5%), India (INDA +1.1%) and cybersecurity (CIBR +0.3%). Biotech held up better (IBB −0.8%, XBI −0.6%), helped by **Insmed +20.6%** on a report that Sanofi is considering buying it.

**Over the month the rotation still holds.** Rotation means money moving from one group of stocks to another. Equal-weight S&P (RSP) is ahead of SPY by 3.3 points, value (IWD) beats growth (IWF) by 6.5 points, and real estate, utilities and staples are all up or flat while tech (XLK) is down **11.9%** and chips (SMH) **14.5%**. Commodity-linked themes are being crushed: copper miners are down 16.5% this week, uranium 11.4% and silver 12.2%.

## 3. The story so far

Since late October the market has gone through three phases:
1. **Money left AI and tech for defensive and value stocks** while the overall index held up.
2. **Huang resigned (Nov 12), and Fed Chair Warsh and the IMF warned about an AI bust**, so the selling spread.
3. **This week the earnings catalysts came out good** (Nvidia, Walmart, TJX), **and stocks fell anyway.**

When good news can't lift prices, it usually means positioning and fear matter more than fundamentals (the actual business results) for now.

The economy itself isn't weak. 165k jobs were added in October, jobless claims are low at 203k, and retail sales are up 6.8% from a year ago. That is part of the problem. With CPI inflation at **4.08%** and the 10-year Treasury yield at **5.07%**, the Fed has no reason to rescue markets, and recent summaries noted that a December rate hike is still expected. One small change: 10-year expected inflation fell from 2.35% to **2.16%** in a month as oil dropped from about $108 to $89.5. If that continues, it could eventually ease pressure on the Fed.

**Credit is the important calm spot.** The junk-bond spread (extra yield that risky companies pay over Treasuries) widened from 3.29% to **3.47%** over the month. That is a real move, but far from stress levels, which usually start above about 5%. HYG fell only 0.2% today. Financial conditions are still slightly easy (−0.48). *Interpretation:* bond investors don't yet see a funding crisis, which is why I lean toward "sharp correction" rather than "start of a bear market." I could be wrong. Wider spreads alongside falling stocks would change that view.

Both sides: a 6.5-to-1 down day with 182 stocks near 52-week lows and fear jumping 30% in a week looks like the kind of washout that often comes before a bounce. On the other hand, our regime is "Defensive," trend-following rules say to cut exposure, and bounces in down-trending markets often fail.

- **Friday:** Does Taiwan's short-selling restriction steady Asian chip stocks? Do advancers start to outnumber decliners?
- **Credit:** a junk spread above about 3.75% or HYG falling sharply would be a warning.
- **Next week:** Analog Devices (Nov 24) and Marvell (Nov 26) report, both key reads on chips. Deere reports Nov 25.
- **Nvidia:** any confirmed news on the SEC probe or a filing delay.

## 5. Lesson of the day

**When good news gets sold.** Nvidia beat estimates, and Walmart beat and raised guidance, but both stocks fell. Prices move on results *compared with what investors already expected and owned*, not on the results alone. When a sell-off is driven by fear and crowded positions, even good reports become a chance to sell. Watching how a stock reacts to news tells you more about the market's mood than the news itself.
