# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-27

> Black Friday was a half-day session on light holiday volume, and stocks rose across the board. SPY gained 1.6%, QQQ 2.7%, and the chip ETFs rebounded 4–5% (SMH +4.2%, SOXX +4.8%). Memory stocks led, while Nvidia lagged after reports that it told partners to stop server shipments to Southeast Asian countries. Breadth improved enough to move our rule-based regime from Defensive to Mixed, with the exposure dial up from 25% to 50% and 43% of stocks above their 50-day average, compared with 26% a week ago. The caution flags are still there: the junk-bond spread reached a new high for this episode at 3.77%, Treasuries fell, and thin holiday volume makes the rally harder to trust. The month-long rotation out of AI and commodity trades into defensive stocks, healthcare and consumer stocks is still the main story.

## 1. What happened

Friday was the day after Thanksgiving. Markets closed early at 1 pm ET, and many traders were away. Stocks still rose almost everywhere:

- **SPY rose 1.6%, QQQ 2.7%, IWM (small caps) 1.8% and DIA (Dow) 1.3%.**
- **Breadth** (how many stocks join a move) was strong: 3,153 stocks rose and 616 fell, about 5 to 1. The median stock gained 1.4%. 106 stocks were near 52-week highs and only 1 was near a low.
- Chips led. **SMH rose 4.2% and SOXX 4.8%.** News reports say the Philadelphia Semiconductor index gained almost 5%, led by memory makers: Micron rose about 7%, and our data show SanDisk (SNDK) up 16% on 2.8× its normal volume.
- **Nvidia lagged.** That is a fact in our news feed ("NVIDIA Orders Partners to Stop Server Shipments to ASEAN States"), and news reports say it gave back a premarket gain of almost 4%. Nvidia remains far below its October high.
- **Brazil (EWZ) jumped 9.8%.** Silver rose 6.2%, uranium (URA) 5.3% and copper miners (COPX) 4.6%. I could not find a clear news cause for the Brazil move, so treat it as unexplained. The metals moves look like short-covering bounces (bears buying back their bets) after very deep falls: SLV is still down 33% over 3 months.

**Why did it rise?** My interpretation is that this was mainly a relief bounce on thin volume after the worst November in years. There was no single catalyst. Supporting points: chips were the most oversold group and bounced hardest, and the fear gauge fell (VIXY −7.8%). Upbeat early holiday-shopping data helped consumer stocks: Adobe counted more than $7 billion of online spending on Thanksgiving Day, and XLY rose 2.1%.

## 2. Where money moved

- **Today:** money went to the beaten-down groups. Chips, solar (TAN +5.4%), lithium (LIT +4.6%), speculative growth (ARKK +3.2%) and momentum (MTUM +3.0%) led. Defensive sectors barely moved: healthcare +0.3%, utilities +0.3%, staples +0.4%.
- **Bonds fell.** Long Treasuries (TLT) dropped 1.2%, so yields rose. This often happens on "risk-on" days, when investors sell safe assets to buy stocks. Crude oil fell 1.5%.
- **Over the month, the order has not changed.** Communication services (+6.2%), staples (+5.1%), discretionary (+3.2%) and homebuilders (+3.2%) are up. Semiconductors (−21.6%), uranium (−18.3%), copper miners (−19.2%) and bitcoin (−25.9%) are deep in the red. The average stock (RSP) is down only 0.8% for the month, while SPY is down 5.3%. That 4.6-point gap shows how much of the damage was in the giant tech stocks.

One quirk in the numbers: our "week" column shows SPY +1.7%, measured from Monday's close. If you count the whole holiday week, including Monday's 4.7% jump, news reports put the S&P 500 up about 5.5%.

## 3. The story so far

Here is the arc of the last eight sessions:

1. A broad AI-led sell-off culminated on Nov 20 (SPY −3.6%, SMH −10.6%), after reports of an SEC investigation into Nvidia's accounting.
2. A huge rebound followed on Nov 23, when Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia's CEO.
3. Chips then gave back about half of that gain.
4. A hot core PCE inflation reading (3.26%) and tariff news hit on Nov 25.
5. Today's holiday bounce.

**What improved (fact):** our rule-based regime moved from *Defensive* to *Mixed*, and the exposure dial rose from 25% to 50%. The share of stocks above their 50-day average (their trend over roughly the last 10 weeks) rose to 43%, from 26% on Nov 20. The share above the 200-day average (the long-term trend) is 43%, up from 38%. Jobless claims are very low (189,000), and October payrolls were solid (+165k).

**What did not improve (fact):**
- The **junk-bond spread**, the extra yield risky companies pay over the government, widened again to 3.77%, the widest of this episode, from 3.18% a month ago. Credit investors are not joining the stock rally, and HYG rose only 0.1% today.
- Inflation is moving the wrong way: CPI is 4.08% and core PCE 3.26%.
- The 10-year yield is still high at 4.85%, and the dollar is strong (UUP +5.5% over 3 months).

**My interpretation:** this is a market that has stopped falling, not yet one that has turned. Two things would make the rebound more convincing: rising volume on up days, and junk spreads starting to narrow.

## 5. Lesson of the day: thin-volume sessions

A **half-day or holiday session** has far fewer trades than normal, and many professional traders are away. With fewer buyers and sellers, smaller orders can move prices more. Experienced investors therefore give moves on these days less weight. A 1.6% gain on light volume tells you less than a 1.6% gain on heavy volume. The thing to check is what happens on the next normal-volume days. If the gains hold, the signal was real. If they fade, it was mostly noise.
