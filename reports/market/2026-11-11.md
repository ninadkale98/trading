# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-11

> Chip stocks led a narrow rally (SOXX +6.0%, SMH +4.8%, QQQ +2.3%, SPY +1.3%) after CoreWeave beat its quarterly forecasts, raised its outlook and said it would not borrow unsecured money at today's rates, which eased worries about AI spending (CRWV +24.7%, Micron reportedly +13%). The average stock did much less (RSP +0.8%, 1.5 advancers per decliner). The past month's winners, including healthcare, consumer discretionary, retail and homebuilders, were flat. Bonds barely moved (likely because of the Veterans Day bond-market holiday), gold fell 1.1% and the dollar rose 0.7%. A December rate hike is still expected and there was a flood of corporate-distress headlines, so this looks like another bounce inside the month-long rotation out of tech, not proof the rotation is over. Cisco and Applied Materials (Thursday) and Nvidia (Nov 17) are the next tests.

## 1. What happened

**Fact:** Today the beaten-down AI trade led the market. The S&P 500 (SPY) rose **1.3%**, the Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) **2.3%**, and chip ETFs did much better: SOXX **+6.0%**, SMH **+4.8%**, tech sector XLK **+3.2%**. Chip-heavy foreign markets moved the same way, with South Korea (EWY) **+6.2%** and Taiwan (EWT) **+5.8%**. The momentum factor ETF (MTUM, which holds recent winners) jumped **+3.8%**, and the VIXY fear gauge fell **6.8%**.

**The cause looks fairly clear.** CoreWeave (CRWV), a company that rents out AI computing power, reported Tuesday night. Revenue was about $3.66B, up 168% from a year ago and above the top of its own forecast. It raised its 2026 outlook, and its backlog (signed work not yet delivered) grew to about $132.5B. The CEO said it will not use the unsecured debt market (borrowing not backed by assets) "at current yields, nor do we need to." Investors had worried that AI builders could run out of cheap financing, and that statement eased the worry. CRWV closed **+24.7%**. One headline in our pack says Micron jumped 13% "as CoreWeave outlook eases AI spending fears." I couldn't confirm the exact Micron figure elsewhere.

**Not everything in tech rose.** Microsoft slid after Anthropic launched productivity tools that compete with it. Palantir fell on a contract warning. Nvidia is still dealing with the Baird downgrade.

## 2. Where money moved

**Interpretation: today was a narrow, catch-up rally, not a broad one.** Several numbers point that way:
- The equal-weight S&P (RSP, the average stock) rose only **0.8%**, against 1.3% for SPY and 2.3% for QQQ.
- Breadth was **2,223 up / 1,504 down**, about 1.5 to 1. Monday's rally was about 5 to 1.
- The groups that led last month were flat or down: healthcare XLV **0.0%**, consumer discretionary XLY **−0.1%**, retail XRT **−0.1%**, homebuilders XHB **−0.1%**, regional banks KRE **−0.2%**, airlines JETS **0.0%**.

In other words, money went back into the past month's losers for a day. It did not leave the winners in a big way.

**Oil gave a puzzling signal.** Headlines said Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed to have "closed" the Strait of Hormuz and that two more tankers hit mines. Yet crude oil (USO) finished **flat** and is still down **7.9%** for the month. Oil stocks rose (XOP **+2.6%**, XLE **+1.9%**). My reading is that traders have become numb to Hormuz headlines because WTI crude is already near $100. That is a guess.

**Bonds hardly moved** (TLT +0.1%, IEF −0.1%). Today is Veterans Day, and the US bond market is normally closed, so this tells us little. **Gold fell 1.1%** and the **dollar rose 0.7%** (UUP is up 1.6% this week). Both fit yesterday's hot CPI report. Higher expected rates make the dollar more attractive and make gold, which pays no interest, less attractive.

## 3. The story so far

The big picture from our last few summaries still holds:

- **Rotation (money shifting from one group to another):** Over a month, XLK is **−8.4%** and SMH **−10.2%**. Healthcare is **+8.6%**, consumer discretionary **+8.5%**, financials **+7.5%**, and RSP has beaten SPY by **6.2 points**. Growth vs value over 20 days is **−9.9%**. One good day for chips doesn't change that.
- **Chip bounces keep fading.** SMH rose 3.6% on Nov 6, rallied hard on Nov 9 and fell back on Nov 10. Now it is up 4.8% again, but only **+0.5% for the week**. These are sharp rallies inside a downtrend. They count as a turn only if they hold through Nvidia's report.
- **Rates are the headwind.** CPI inflation is **4.08%** a year (core 2.87%), core PCE is 3.12%, and markets are pricing a December Fed *hike*. Yardeni even argues for a 50-basis-point hike (0.50 percentage points). The yield curve (10-year minus 2-year yield) has flattened to **0.25%** from 0.44% a month ago. That usually means markets expect the Fed to slow the economy.
- **Credit is uneasy but not panicking.** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield risky companies pay over Treasuries) is **3.34%**. That is up from 3.11% a month ago but below the 3.48% we saw last week. Today's news, though, had a long list of distress signs: possible bankruptcies at Telesat and Cable One, a prepackaged Chapter 11 (a bankruptcy agreed with lenders in advance) at Mercer, going-concern warnings (the auditor or company doubting it can survive another year) at GoPro and Caesarstone, JetBlue's lenders organizing into groups, and a restatement at Endava. On the consumer side, the New York Fed reported record credit-card debt of **$1.29 trillion**, and consumer sentiment is weak at **48.1**. These are mostly small, weak companies, but they show what high rates are doing to borrowers.
- **The system's regime reading:** "Mixed." **50%** of stocks are above their 200-day average and the exposure dial is at **50%**.

**Both sides.** Bulls can point to CoreWeave's huge backlog as evidence that AI demand is real, and to tech having already corrected about 8–10%, which makes valuations less stretched. Bears can point to a likely Fed hike, sticky inflation, rising distress among weaker borrowers, and well-known investors (Howard Marks, Peter Thiel's partner) calling AI spending a bubble or an "unknowable" gamble.

## 5. Lesson of the day: the advance-decline ratio

The **advance-decline ratio** is the number of stocks that rose divided by the number that fell. It shows whether a rally is *broad* (most stocks joined in) or *narrow* (a few big ones did the lifting).

- **Today:** SPY rose 1.3%, but only about 1.5 stocks rose for each one that fell, and the average stock (RSP) gained 0.8%. A few giant chip and AI names did most of the work.
- **Monday:** about 5 stocks rose for each one that fell. That rally was much broader.

Index headlines can hide this. A cap-weighted index like SPY (weighted by company size) can rise sharply while most stocks barely move. Comparing SPY with RSP, or looking at the up/down count, shows whether "the market" or just its biggest members went up.
