## 1. What happened

Monday's huge rally was led by a few giant tech stocks. Tuesday was the opposite: the index fell while the average stock rose.

- **The index:** SPY (S&P 500) fell 0.5% and QQQ (Nasdaq 100) fell 2.0%.
- **The average stock:** RSP (the equal-weight S&P 500, where every company counts the same) rose 0.5%, the Dow ETF DIA rose 0.8% and small caps (IWM) rose 0.5%.
- **Breadth (how many stocks join a move):** 2,222 stocks rose and 1,553 fell. Nine of 11 sectors finished up.

**Chips were the problem (fact).** The chip ETFs gave back about half of Monday's gain: SMH −7.3%, SOXX −8.3%.
- **Nvidia** fell about 5.9% to roughly $155.38 after Monday's 31.5% jump on Jensen Huang's return as CEO. That leaves it almost exactly where it was on Nov 17 ($155.46).
- **Analog Devices (ADI)** fell 17.4% even though its press release said "record" results. Reports say investors disliked its outlook.
- **Others:** news reports have Micron down about 11%, AMD about 9% and Intel about 8%. AMD was reportedly also hurt by a filing that registers about 337 million shares, from its Taalas acquisition, for possible resale. I could not confirm the filing date, so treat that link as unconfirmed.
- **HPE** fell 16.3% despite "record quarterly revenue." I could not find a clear cause, so I am not guessing one.

**Why chips fell (interpretation).** Commentators pointed to profit-taking after a historic one-day rally and to renewed worry about a December rate hike. My reading: Monday's rally was about Nvidia's leadership news, not about demand, and ADI's outlook revived the demand question. Taiwan (EWT −4.2%) and South Korea (EWY −3.7%), both heavy chip exporters, fell with them.

## 5. Lesson of the day: "beat the numbers, stock falls"

ADI and HPE both used the word "record" in their press releases, and both fell more than 16%. Here is why that can happen:
- **The price already reflected expectations.** A share price already includes what investors expect. Results must beat those expectations, and often the company's guidance (its own forecast for the coming quarters) matters more than the quarter just finished.
- **A "record" can still disappoint.** If the stock had already priced in an even better record, or the outlook implies slowing growth, the shares fall.
- **Our news labels don't measure the reaction.** The +1/+2 tags grade the tone of the press release, not how investors will react to it. That is a good reason never to trade on a headline alone.
