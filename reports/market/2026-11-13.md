# Daily market analysis: 2026-11-13

> Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned unexpectedly on Thursday, and chip stocks absorbed the news on Friday (SOXX −3.7%, SMH −3.0%, XLK −2.0%). The rest of the market stayed calm: SPY fell only 0.4%, the equal-weight S&P rose 0.2%, and staples, financials, energy and healthcare all gained. Two Fed officials (Daly and Collins) openly talked about raising rates, which kept short-term yields high. The week as a whole was still strong (SPY +2.6%, SOXX +9.3%), but Friday showed the month-long pattern again: money leaving big tech and AI for defensive and value stocks. Next week is very busy: Nvidia reports earnings Nov 17 under an interim CEO, and the big retailers (WMT, HD, TGT, LOW, TJX) report too.

## 1. What happened

**Fact:** Late Thursday (Nov 12), Nvidia said Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO, president and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO, and the board will search for a permanent replacement (SEC 8-K). Press reports link the exit to alleged personal misconduct and possible security concerns. Those are allegations, not findings. Our news feed rated the story importance 5 with a negative effect. Other headlines piled onto Nvidia: Treasury Secretary Bessent criticized Nvidia chips "leaking" to China, and there were reports of Dell servers with restricted Nvidia chips turning up in China.

Friday was a **"rotation day"** (money shifting from one group of stocks to another), not a crash:
- Chips fell hard: **SOXX −3.7%, SMH −3.0%**. The tech sector (XLK) fell 2.0%, the Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) fell 1.2%, and the momentum ETF (MTUM, recent winners) fell 2.0%.
- AI "neocloud" stocks (companies that rent out AI computing power) took collateral damage: **Nebius fell about 17%** on a weak outlook plus "Nvidia turmoil."
- The broad market barely moved: **SPY −0.4%** and **RSP (equal-weight S&P, the average stock) +0.2%**. Advancers and decliners were almost even (1,835 vs 1,891), and the median stock was flat.
- Defensive and value sectors rose: **staples +1.4%, communication services +1.1%, energy +1.0%, financials +0.8%, healthcare and utilities +0.5%**.

The Fed added pressure. **Mary Daly** (San Francisco Fed) said "in all likelihood we will need to raise rates," and **Susan Collins** (Boston Fed) said inflation is "too high and appears to be broadening." The 2-year Treasury yield, the bond yield most tied to Fed expectations, sits at **4.86%**, up from 4.79% a month ago. Long bonds slipped (TLT −0.4%). Bitcoin (IBIT −3.5%) and silver (−2.2%) also fell, which fits a mood of selling speculative assets.

**Interpretation:** I think Nvidia was the main cause of the chip selloff, with the hawkish Fed talk adding to it. The Communication Services gain (+1.1%) has no clear cause in our news, so I can't explain it.

## 3. The story so far

For about a month, money has moved out of big tech and chips and into healthcare, financials, consumer stocks and the average stock. Over the month, RSP is up 3.3% while SPY is down 1.2%. This week brought a strong bounce: SPY rose 2.6%, SOXX rose 9.3% after CoreWeave's results, and VIXY (a fear gauge) fell 14.7%. Friday showed how fragile that bounce is. One bad headline and chips gave back a chunk of it, while defensive stocks rose.

**The rate backdrop is the bigger worry.** Headline CPI (consumer prices) is up 4.08% over the year, and core CPI is up 2.87% and rising. Futures already price in a December rate hike, and Fed speakers are hinting it may not be the last. The 10-year yield is 5.10%. The **real yield** (the return after expected inflation) is 2.86%, which makes richly valued growth stocks harder to justify. **The yield curve** (the 10-year minus the 2-year yield) has narrowed to 0.27% from 0.37% a month ago. That tends to happen when markets expect the Fed to tighten.

**Credit is calm, not panicking.** The junk-bond spread (the extra yield investors demand from risky companies) is 3.20%, well below the 3.48% we noted on Nov 6. Junk bonds (HYG) dipped only 0.1%.

**About our "exposure dial":** our rule-based dial jumped to **95%** from 50% last week, because SPY's trend rule and calmer fear gauges switched back on. Keep this in context: fewer than half of stocks (47%) are above their 200-day average (the average price of the last ~200 trading days, a common trend line). The S&P 500 is still down over one and three months. The dial is a mechanical rule, not a forecast. Readings that flip within a week are common at turning points and in choppy markets.

## 4. What to watch

- **Retailers:** Home Depot and Disney (Tue), Target, Lowe's and TJX (Wed), Walmart (Thu). Daly said the consumer "may be running out of gas," and consumer sentiment is weak at 48.1. These reports test whether the consumer stocks that led this month deserve it.

- **Whether RSP keeps beating SPY** on bounce days, not just on down days.
- **Smaller items:** the US–China trade talks and the planned Trump–Xi–Putin meeting (chip export policy), and the nationwide FAA ground stop (airlines, JETS −0.7%).

## 5. Lesson of the day: key-person risk

Key-person risk is the danger that a company's value depends heavily on one person. If that person leaves suddenly, investors have to re-price the uncertainty. Huang co-founded Nvidia and ran it for 33 years, so his exit raises questions about strategy, customer relationships and government relations, even though the products and order book are unchanged overnight. Spillover is also normal. Nebius fell about 17% partly because of the "Nvidia turmoil," because its business is built on Nvidia chips. The lesson for a multi-week hold: before buying, ask how much of the story rests on one person, and size the position with that in mind.
