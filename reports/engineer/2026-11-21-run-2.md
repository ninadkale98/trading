**What I built and why**

The lab keeps asking for earnings and market-condition inputs that can be tested on history. Our estimate-based surprise data only covers a few percent of past stock-days, so those tests run short of trades. Everything here comes from prices and earnings dates we already store, so it all reaches back to 2016.

1. **What happened on and after the last earnings reaction day** (`core/features/earnings.py`). This covers three requests.
   - `earnings_reaction_prev`: the reaction to the report before the latest one.
   - `reaction_streak`: reports in a row with a positive (+) or negative (−) reaction. It is capped at ±4 because the nightly job only looks back about 15 months; the cap keeps live and backtest values identical.
   - `ret_since_reaction`: return since the reaction day's close.
   - `reaction_day_low_dist`: how far the close is above or below the reaction day's low.
   - `reaction_rvol`: volume on the reaction day compared with normal.
   - `reaction_close_vs_open`: whether the opening gap kept going or faded during the day.
2. **Market regime** (new file `core/features/regime.py`). This covers two requests and the trade review's "narrow market" lesson.
   - `spy_dist_sma200`, `spy_ret_120`, `spy_vol20`: SPY's trend, 6-month return and recent volatility.
   - `breadth_200`: the share of stocks above their 200-day average.
   - `breadth_200_chg10`: how much that share changed over 10 days.
   - Each is one value per day, copied onto every stock's row, so any strategy can add a market gate.
3. A new migration, `019`, adds the database columns.
4. Docs are updated: a new beginner page `learning/18`, plus `learning/11`, the glossary, architecture §7, the build log, and five requests ticked in `lab/requests.md`.
