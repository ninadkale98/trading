# Weekly trade review: week of Nov 9, 2026 (dry run, simulated money)

## The short version
- **5 trades closed and all 5 lost money.** Total loss: **−$1,459**, about 1.5% of a ~$100k practice book. These are also the system's first five closed trades ever, so the "all-time" table below is just this week.
- **The market didn't cause this.** SPY (the S&P 500 fund) was flat to slightly up during every losing trade (between −0.4% and +1.0%). Each loss came from something about that particular stock.
- **None of the losers ever really worked.** The best each one got before failing was +0.05% to +4%. These weren't "stopped out at the bottom and then it took off" cases, at least as far as we can see. We don't record prices after an exit, so we can't fully check that.
- **Two problems are in the process, not the market:**
  - **SLN:** the system bought it the day before its earnings report and then had to sell the next morning.
  - **SLN is also being bought by another company,** so its price is pinned near the agreed deal price and has little room to rise.
- **Don't judge the strategies on these 5 trades.** A trailing-stop system closes its losers first. Winners keep running for weeks. 18 positions are still open, and several are up 9–15% at their best point.

### How seriously to take 0 wins out of 5
- **If each trade had a 40–45% chance of winning,** five losses in a row would happen about 5–8% of the time. That's unlucky, but not shocking.
- **Early closed trades are biased toward losers.** Stops get hit within days, while a trailing stop lets winners run for weeks. So the first trades to close are mostly losers. We'll only see the real win rate once the open book (the trades still running) starts closing.
- **Verdict: mostly noise plus a couple of fixable process mistakes.** It's not evidence that any strategy works or doesn't.

## Strategy by strategy

### accumulation (2 closed: HGTY, SLN; open: JAKK, SPNT, TW)
**The idea:** buy stocks where volume on up days has clearly beaten volume on down days, meaning big buyers seem to be "accumulating" shares, while the price is near its high.

**HGTY (Hagerty)**
- **At entry:** the company had raised guidance three days earlier. The stock had jumped 15% in 5 days, was 23% above its 50-day average, and had an RSI of 74. RSI is a 0–100 momentum gauge; above 70 is usually called "overbought."
- **What happened:** two days later it **gapped down**, meaning it opened well below the previous close. The stop was at $16.35, but the fill came at $15.79. That turned a planned loss of about $554 into $726.
- **A clue:** volume that day was **23 times normal** (rvol 23), and the accumulation reading collapsed from 0.51 to 0.06. A spike like that after a big run often means a large holder sold, or there was some other stock-specific event.
- **A data gap:** our news feed captured no clear headline for it (news score −4 at exit). We should find out what happened and why we missed it.
- **Skill or luck?** Mostly bad luck from a one-off event. Buying an overbought stock right after earnings does raise the odds of a sharp pullback, though.

**SLN (Silence Therapeutics): a process miss, not a market loss**
1. **Earnings check failed.** At entry, `days_to_earnings_est` was **−1**: the estimated report date had already passed but the report hadn't come out yet. The "no earnings soon" check didn't treat this as "earnings any day now." The exit rule caught it the next morning and sold. The cost was only $23, but the trade should never have happened.
2. **It's a takeover target.** Merck KGaA is acquiring the company. The stock had jumped **+123% in 20 days** on the deal news. Since then it has barely moved: 0.05% in 5 days, very low daily movement, and volume at 0.14× normal.
   - The accumulation score was high only because of the huge buying volume on the deal-announcement day.
   - The "not up more than 40% in 3 months" filter missed it: the 60-day return was only +38%, because the stock had fallen before the deal.
   - A stock that is about to be bought out at a fixed price has almost no upside left. It's the wrong kind of stock for this strategy.

**Open accumulation trades:** JAKK (best +5.9%), SPNT (+4.1%), TW (+1.5%). Too early to say anything.

### earnings_gap_up (1 closed: ZIP; open: FROG, FLGT, DCTH, TLS, TTEK)
**The idea:** after a strong earnings day on heavy volume, buy the next morning and ride the follow-through.

**ZIP (ZipRecruiter)**
- **At entry:** the stock was up **57% in 5 days** and 61% above its 50-day average, at a brand-new 52-week high. The UBS price target was raised, but only to **$5**, below the $6.59 price, with a Neutral rating.
- **What happened:** it peaked at +4% and then fell 13% the next day, through the 12% stop.
- **The stop was tight for this stock.** ZIP's normal daily swing is about 7%, so a 12% stop was only about 1.7 days of normal movement. One ordinary bad day after a 57% run could hit it.

**The open trades look different.** FROG (best +12.5%), FLGT (+14.8%), DCTH (+11.0%) and TLS (+10.9%) have all had good runs, and TTEK just started.
- TLS was nearly as stretched as ZIP (56% above its 50-day average, up 38% in 5 days) and is doing fine.
- So "too stretched" doesn't cleanly explain ZIP's loss.
- **Verdict:** one loss against four promising open trades. Noise so far.

**Housekeeping:** DCTH and TLS showed `days_to_earnings_est = 0` on their signal day because the next report date hadn't been updated. That's the same stale field that caused the SLN problem. Check that it's now set to the next quarter's date so it can't trigger a false "sell before earnings" exit.

### relative_strength (1 closed: MRNA; open: RCEL)
**The idea:** buy the strongest stocks of the past year while they're in a clean uptrend.

**MRNA (Moderna)**
- **At entry:** the stock was up **325% in 60 days**, **190% above its 200-day average**, and moving about 8% a day. It was bought one day after a very strong earnings report.
- **What happened:** within 3 days it gapped below its stop. The fill was $224.79 against a $228.65 stop.
- **Why the stop was weak:** a 15% trailing stop on a stock that moves 8% a day is less than 2 days of normal movement.

**RCEL (open)** is in a similar spot:
- At entry it was up 118% in 60 days and 148% above its 200-day average.
- Its stop is about 12% below entry, roughly 1.8 ATR.
- It has already been down 10.2% at its worst, close to that stop.

**Verdict:** one trade is not a result. But both trades this strategy picked were extremely stretched, high-volatility names right after earnings. That's worth testing.

### return_to_average (1 closed: CLMT; open: UFCS, TEAM)
**The idea:** buy a healthy stock that has suddenly dropped, and sell when it bounces back to its 20-day average.

**CLMT (Calumet)**
- **The drop was the earnings reaction itself.** The company beat estimates by a lot, yet the stock fell 9% on earnings day.
- The rule only blocks *upcoming* earnings, not a report that came out yesterday.
- When a stock falls on its earnings news, it often keeps drifting the same way for weeks rather than bouncing back. This is called **post-earnings drift**.
- It was also only **1.3% above its 200-day average**, which barely counts as a healthy stock. By exit it was 13% *below* that average.
- **The position was only 2 shares (−$9)** because the exposure dial and cash limited it. The % loss is what matters, and it was a full −1R.

**The two open trades have the same setup** (they fell right after earnings): UFCS (best +8.9%) is working, and TEAM (best +1.8%, worst −5.2%) is flat-ish.

**Verdict:** one loss and one working trade out of three post-earnings dips. No conclusion yet, but it is a clean thing to test.

## Patterns across all trades

1. **Market conditions didn't separate winners from losers.**
   - Losers were entered on both "Mixed/risk-off" and "Healthy" days.
   - Many open trades with good runs (FROG, FLGT, UFCS, ANF) were entered on the same risk-off signal day (Nov 6) as HGTY and CLMT.
   - "Breadth" means the share of stocks above their 200-day average. It sat at 45–50% all week: neither good nor bad.

2. **Most stopped-out trades had stops smaller than about 2 days of normal movement.**
   - ZIP, CLMT and MRNA all had stops of 1.6–1.8 ATR. RCEL (open, 1.8 ATR) is close to its stop.
   - HGTY's stop was wider (2.7 ATR), but a gap went straight through it.
   - A fixed % stop treats a calm stock and a wild one the same. This suggests sizing stops by volatility, while buying fewer shares so the dollar risk stays the same.
   - **Caveat:** none of these losers had a big run first (all reached +4% or less). We can't claim the stops were "too tight" until we track prices after exit.

3. **Gaps make losses bigger than planned.**
   - 2 of 5 exits gapped through the stop (HGTY −1.31R, MRNA −1.10R).
   - Both came within days of earnings, after big runs.
   - A stop limits losses only while the stock trades smoothly. Overnight news skips right past it.

4. **Many losers were very stretched at entry, but the open book disagrees.**
   - HGTY, SLN, ZIP and MRNA were all 23–61% above their 50-day averages, and 4 of 5 losers were bought within 3 days of an earnings report.
   - But TLS, DCTH and JAKK were just as stretched and are fine, and earnings_gap_up trades are post-earnings by design.
   - **Treat this as a hypothesis to backtest, not a rule.**

5. **The market-condition dial flips every day.** The exposure dial went 0.5 → 0.95 → 0.5 → 0.5 → 0.95 between Nov 6 and Nov 12, as breadth moved between 45% and 50%. That's a threshold being crossed back and forth, not a real change in the market. It changes position sizes randomly (CLMT got 2 shares).

## What this review is *not* saying
- It's not saying any strategy is broken. Five trades can't show that.
- It's not saying to widen every stop. That only makes sense if a backtest shows higher returns per trade at the same dollar risk.
- None of this is advice to buy or sell any of these stocks.

## Lessons
- **The earnings-date check must treat a stale estimate (days_to_earnings_est < 0 while days_since_earnings is ~60+) as 'report imminent or unknown' and block entry.**. Evidence: SLN entered Nov 11 with days_to_earnings_est = -1 and days_since_earnings = 65. The report came the next day, forcing a 1-day 'sell before earnings' exit. DCTH and TLS also showed days_to_earnings_est = 0 after they had already reported, so the field is not always rolled forward.. Test: Over the full signal history, count signals with days_to_earnings_est <= 0 and days_since_earnings > 50, and how many had an actual report within 5 trading days. Then rerun all 'no earnings due' strategies with the corrected check and compare how many trades exit early for earnings and their average return.
- **Exclude pending-acquisition targets (prices pinned near a deal price) from accumulation and momentum strategies, since their upside is capped and the deal-day volume fakes the accumulation score.**. Evidence: SLN: Merck KGaA acquisition, +123% in 20 days, then +0.05% over 5 days, ATR 2.4%, volume 0.14x normal. The accumulation score of 0.60 came from the deal-day volume. The 'up <40% in 3 months' filter missed it because the 60-day return was only +38%.. Test: Flag past accumulation and relative_strength signals where the stock had a single-day gain over 25% in the last 30 days followed by a 10-day ATR below 1/3 of its prior level (or a merger_acquisition news tag naming it as target). Compare their forward 20/60-day returns and max gains with unflagged signals.
- **return_to_average should probably not buy dips that are the earnings reaction itself (post-earnings drift tends to continue), and should require a real margin above the 200-day average.**. Evidence: CLMT dropped 9% on earnings day (days_since_earnings 0) and was only 1.3% above its 200-day average. It was stopped at -1R in 4 days and ended 13% below that average. Open trades UFCS (+8.9% best) and TEAM (flat) have the same post-earnings setup, so the evidence is mixed.. Test: Backtest return_to_average split by days_since_earnings <= 5 vs > 5, and by dist_sma200 < 5% vs >= 5%. Compare win rate, average return per trade, and stop-out rate in each bucket.
- **Fixed-% stops are below 2 ATR on volatile stocks, so ordinary noise can hit them. Test volatility-scaled stops with position size adjusted to keep dollar risk constant.**. Evidence: ZIP (12% stop vs 7.2% ATR = 1.7 ATR), CLMT (1.6 ATR) and MRNA (1.8 ATR) were all stopped within 1-4 days. Open RCEL's stop is 1.8 ATR and its worst point (-10.2%) is close to it. All losers had small best points (<= 4%), so this is a hypothesis, not proof.. Test: For earnings_gap_up, relative_strength and return_to_average, backtest initial stops of max(fixed %, k x ATR) for k = 2, 2.5, 3, scaling shares so planned dollar risk is unchanged. Compare average return per trade (in R), stop-out rate in the first 5 days, and max drawdown against the current fixed-% stops.
- **Record what price does after every exit, so we can tell 'stop was too tight' apart from 'trade was wrong'.**. Evidence: This week we could not tell whether ZIP, CLMT, MRNA or HGTY recovered after being stopped out. The data only has best and worst points while the trade was open, so the stop-tightness question can't be answered.. Test: Add post-exit tracking (+5, +10, +20 trading days: close, highest high, lowest low). In a backtest, measure the share of stopped trades that later traded above their entry price within 20 days, by strategy and by stop distance in ATRs.
- **Stocks bought after a big run or right after earnings may gap through stops more often, making losses bigger than planned. Measure this and, if real, size with a gap allowance.**. Evidence: HGTY gapped from a $16.35 stop to a $15.79 fill (-1.31R) on 23x normal volume. MRNA gapped from a $228.65 stop to $224.79 (-1.10R). Both were 1-3 days after earnings and up 15-22% in the prior 5 days.. Test: Across all backtested stop exits, compute how often the fill gapped through the stop and the average extra loss in R, bucketed by ret_5d (< 5%, 5-20%, > 20%) and days_since_earnings (<= 3 vs > 3). If the high buckets are clearly worse, test cutting size by the average gap amount for those buckets.
- **relative_strength may need a cap on how stretched a stock can be at entry (distance above its 200-day average, or 60-day return).**. Evidence: MRNA: +325% in 60 days, 190% above its 200-day average, ATR 8%, stopped in 3 days at -1.1R. Open RCEL: +118% in 60 days, 148% above its 200-day average, worst point -10%. These are the only two relative_strength trades, and both are hyper-extended.. Test: Backtest relative_strength with dist_sma200 buckets (< 50%, 50-100%, > 100%) and ret_60d buckets (< 60%, 60-150%, > 150%). Compare 20/60/120-day forward returns and stop-out rate, and test whether a cap (e.g. dist_sma200 < 100%) improves average return per trade.
- **The exposure dial flips between 0.5 and 0.95 almost daily when breadth sits near its threshold. Add a buffer zone (hysteresis) or a confirmation period.**. Evidence: Exposure was 0.5 (Nov 6), 0.95 (Nov 9), 0.5 (Nov 10-11), 0.95 (Nov 12-13) while breadth (share of stocks above their 200-day average) only moved between 45% and 50%. CLMT was sized to 2 shares partly because of the dial.. Test: Backtest the regime dial with (a) the current single threshold, (b) a +/- 3-point band around the threshold, and (c) a 3-day confirmation rule. Compare the number of dial changes per year, portfolio return, and max drawdown.
- **Judge strategies on open and closed trades together (marked to market), not on closed trades alone. Early closed trades in a trailing-stop system are biased toward losers.**. Evidence: All 5 all-time closed trades are losses held 1-4 days, while 18 open trades include FROG +12.5%, FLGT +14.8%, CRWV +15%, DCTH +11% at their best points.. Test: In existing backtests, compare the win rate of trades that closed within the first 10 trading days against the full-sample win rate for each strategy, to measure how strong this early-close bias is. Use the result to set a minimum trade count before any strategy is judged.
- **Check how well the news feed covers sudden large moves in stocks we hold.**. Evidence: HGTY gapped about 14% on 23x normal volume, with no clear headline captured (news score -4 at exit).. Test: Over the last 6 months, find all days where a held or candidate stock had rvol > 10 and |daily return| > 8%. Measure the share that had at least one news item in the database within 24 hours, and list the misses to see what kinds of events the feed lacks.
