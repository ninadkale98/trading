# Weekly trade review: week of 2026-11-16 (dry run)

## Summary

- **13 trades closed. All 13 lost, and every one exited at its stop.** Realized loss is about **−$3,709**. The average trade lost **−8.2%**, which is about **−3.4% worse than the S&P 500 (SPY)** over the same holding periods.
- **The market drove most of this week.** SPY fell about **9%** from its Nov 11 high (759.8) to Nov 20 (689.8). Nov 20 alone was −3.6%. **Breadth** (the share of stocks above their 200-day average, a rough "how many stocks are healthy" gauge) dropped from **~50% to 34%**. The regime label went from Healthy to Mixed to Defensive. A long-only book with mostly above-average-volatility stocks was always going to lose money in that.
- **All-time record: 0 wins in 18 closed trades.** That sounds damning. But nearly all of these trades were opened Nov 9–13 and closed in the same market drop, so they are not 18 independent tests. Statistically it is closer to one or two bets on "the market keeps rising in mid-November." **This proves nothing about any single strategy's edge, good or bad.** It does show some fixable weak spots, listed below.
- Three things were in our control and worth testing: (1) **six trades were up 7–20% at some point and still ended as losses**; (2) **about 10 positions were opened on a single day (Nov 10)**, right after the regime dial flipped to "Healthy"; (3) **several data problems** showed up (a 16% gap between the signal price and the fill, an earnings-reaction number that contradicts the price history, and news from a different company attached to a ticker).

### Terms used below
- **MFE / MAE**: the best and worst points a trade reached while open, as % from entry. "MFE +11%" means it was up 11% at its best.
- **R**: the amount you planned to risk (entry minus stop, times shares). −1R means you lost exactly what you planned to lose.
- **ATR**: average true range, roughly the typical daily price swing as % of price. A stop only 1.2 ATR away can be hit by an ordinary day's noise.
- **Trailing stop**: a stop that moves up as the price rises (here it seems to follow the highest close) but never moves down.
- **Beta**: how much a stock tends to move when the market moves. Beta 2 means about 2× the market's moves.
- **RSI**: a 0–100 momentum gauge. Above 70 is often called "overbought" or stretched.

## Strategy by strategy

### earnings_gap_up: 5 closed (0 wins), all-time 0/6, average −6.1%, −3.0% vs SPY; 7 still open
- **What happened:** SBC was stopped out the same day it was bought. TTEK hit its stop in 3 days without ever really going up. TLS, DCTH and FROG each rose 11–20% first, then gave it all back as the market fell.
- **Entry context:** The three small caps (SBC, TLS, DCTH, all around $0.5–0.75B market value) were bought after enormous runs: up 38–46% in 5 days, RSI 75–79, 30–56% above their 50-day average. SBC opened 3% below the signal close, reversed, and closed 13% below entry. FROG was the least stretched entry (RSI 56, +7% in 5 days, 8% above its 50-day). It did best: up nearly 20% at its peak, and it still beat SPY by 7 points after the giveback. TTEK barely passed the filters (earnings reaction +5.2% vs a 5% minimum, volume 2.5× vs a 2× minimum, revenue shrinking 7.6%, an analyst target of $40 with the stock at $39.4).
- **Skill, luck or noise?** Noise-sized sample in a falling market. "Post-earnings drift" (stocks with good surprises tend to keep rising for weeks) is a real, studied effect. It is also known to be weaker after huge one-day moves in small caps. **"Stretched entries fade"** is a hypothesis worth a backtest. It is not a finding.
- The stop on gap-ups is clearly not the 12% trail at the start: SBC's was 7.7% below entry and TTEK's 4.6%. It looks like it may be set at the reaction-day low. That is worth confirming, because 1.25–1.5 ATR is inside normal daily noise.

### quality_pullback: 3 closed (0/3), average −4.6%, **+0.4% vs SPY** all-time
- MRX and IRDM rose 9% and 8%, then their 10% trailing stops gave everything back. ANF was bought on a "Mixed / risk-off" day with earnings only 19 days away, so it could never have been the multi-week hold the strategy intends.
- **Verdict:** It moved about in line with the market, which is what you would expect from profitable large stocks in a 9% index drop. No evidence of edge or of failure.

## Patterns across trades

1. **The market explains most of it.** Every entry, in every strategy and regime label, lost. The trades that held up best relative to SPY (FROG +7, IRDM +4, MRX 0) were the least-stretched entries. The worst (CRWV −14, RCEL −11) were the most volatile or most extended. Beta alone doesn't explain everything: low-beta TEAM still lost 6 points to SPY.
2. **Gains came first, then were given back.** Six trades reached MFE of +7.8% to +19.8% and still ended as losses (TLS, MRX, DCTH, CRWV, FROG, IRDM; combined −$1,238). This is the opposite of the "stop too tight" pattern. The trades worked, and then the exit rules let a winner turn into a loser. A breakeven or profit-lock rule might have helped here. It would also cut real winners short in normal markets, so it needs a backtest across regimes.
3. **Intraday stop hits that closed above the stop:** 6 of 13 (RCEL, TLS, TTEK, DCTH, FROG, TEAM) closed above their stop on the exit day, mostly by only 0.3–2.4%. A stop based on the closing price would have kept them at least one more day. In a falling market that could easily have meant bigger losses, so this is a test, not a recommendation.
4. **Entries bunched in time.** About 10 positions (6 of this week's losers plus UPBD, MTDR, SPNT, JAKK) opened on **Nov 10**, the day after one "Healthy / 0.95" dial reading. The dial was 0.5 the trading day before and 0.5 again the day after. Last week's review flagged this flip-flopping. This week shows the cost: one noisy reading loaded the book right at the market top.
5. **"Healthy" with under 50% breadth.** Every "Healthy" day this month had only 47–49% of stocks above their 200-day average. That is an S&P uptrend carried by a minority of stocks (a "narrow market"), and breadth was already falling. Calling that Healthy may be too generous.
6. **New entries kept coming in the selloff.** Eight positions opened Nov 17–20 while the dial read 0.5, then 0.25 (FUN, TSN, XHR, ENR, ATEC, IIIV, HAWK, VVV). Most were limited by the per-trade risk budget, not by the dial. If the dry-run book is about $100k, the cost of open positions looks well above 25% invested on Nov 20. **Please verify that the dial really caps total exposure and not just individual new trades.**

## Data and system issues found (fix before drawing conclusions)
- **CRWV:** the next-open fill was 16% below the signal close. There is no sanity check between signal and fill.
- **PUBM:** the earnings_reaction value conflicts with its 5-day return and its distance from the 52-week high.
- **ENR (Energizer):** the entry news is about **Siemens Energy** (whose German ticker is also ENR). That inflates news_score (16) and news_major (4). The sector is also listed as "Technology" (Energizer is a consumer-staples company), which affects the 25%-per-sector cap.
- **HAWK:** missing rs_rank, trend_score and 200-day data (likely a recent listing), yet it shows 8 insider buyers. Purchases at the IPO may be counted as open-market insider buying.
- **The `stop` field is the *final* (trailed) stop, not the initial one.** For trades that moved up first, we can't compute R or check stop distance in ATR. The initial stop and initial dollar risk need to be logged.
- Fills are modelled at about 0.05–0.15% below the stop. For $500M small caps on a crash day, that is probably optimistic.

## Bottom line
This week was a market event, not a verdict on the strategies. The sample is too small and too correlated to say anything is skill or broken. What *can* be fixed regardless of edge: entry bunching on noisy regime readings, no rule for protecting open gains, no guard between signal and fill, and several data-quality bugs in the earnings and news fields that the strategies depend on.


## Lessons
- **Test a profit-protection rule: once a trade's best point reaches a threshold, raise the stop to about breakeven, or take a partial profit, instead of relying only on a 10–12% trailing stop.**. Evidence: 6 of 13 losers were up first: FROG +19.8%, CRWV +15.0%, DCTH +11.0%, TLS +10.9%, MRX +9.2%, IRDM +7.8%. All ended as losses (combined −$1,238) when the market fell 9%.. Test: For every strategy with a trailing stop, backtest: (a) current rules; (b) stop moved to entry once MFE ≥ +1R; (c) stop moved to entry once MFE ≥ 2 ATR; (d) sell half at +1R. Compare average return per trade (in R), win rate, and the share of trades reaching +3R, split by market regime (Healthy vs Mixed/Defensive), to see whether it only helps in down markets.
- **Test stops that trigger on the daily close (with a wider emergency intraday stop) against the current intraday stops.**. Evidence: 6 of 13 exits (RCEL, TLS, TTEK, DCTH, FROG, TEAM) closed above their stop on the exit day, by 0.3–2.4%. TEAM gapped below its stop at the open, then closed 2.3% above it.. Test: Rerun all strategies with: exit at the next open if the close is below the stop, plus a hard intraday stop at 1.5× the normal stop distance. Compare average R per trade, worst trade in R, max drawdown, and the share of stopped trades that later traded above entry within 10 days.
- **Limit how many new positions can open on a single day (or spread entries over several days), especially right after the regime dial changes.**. Evidence: About 10 positions opened on Nov 10, after one 'Healthy / 0.95' reading between two 0.5 readings. Six of them were stopped out this week as SPY fell ~9% from its Nov 11 high.. Test: Backtest the full multi-strategy book with a cap of N new entries per day (N = 2, 3, 5, unlimited), with leftover signals queued by rank for the next days. Compare portfolio max drawdown, worst 10-day loss, and annual return.
- **Treat a 'narrow market' (S&P 500 in an uptrend but under 50% of stocks above their 200-day average, and falling) as Mixed rather than Healthy.**. Evidence: Every 'Healthy' day this month had only 47–49% breadth. Breadth then slid from 49.6% (Nov 11) to 33.7% (Nov 20) while SPY fell ~9%. All entries made on Healthy days lost.. Test: Backtest the regime dial with an extra rule: if breadth < 50% and its 10-day change is negative, cap exposure at 0.5. Compare the portfolio's return, max drawdown, and per-trade returns of entries made on reclassified days vs remaining Healthy days.
- **earnings_gap_up may need a stretch filter: avoid gap-ups where the stock is already far above its 50-day average or up 30%+ in 5 days with RSI > 75.**. Evidence: SBC (+46% in 5 days, RSI 78, 43% above its 50-day) was stopped the same day. TLS (+38%, RSI 75, +56%) and DCTH (+42%, RSI 79, +31%) gave back 11% gains. FROG, the least stretched (+7%, RSI 56, +8%), did best: −1.1% and +7 points vs SPY.. Test: Backtest earnings_gap_up bucketed by dist_sma50 (<15%, 15–30%, >30%), ret_5d (<15%, 15–30%, >30%) and market cap (<$1B vs ≥$1B). Compare 10/20/40-day forward returns, same-week stop-out rate, and average R.
- **Add an entry sanity check: if the next open is far from the signal-day close, skip the trade or re-evaluate it.**. Evidence: CRWV signalled at a $60.77 close but filled at $50.93 the next open, a 16% gap (about 1.6 ATR). It lost 22.5%. SBC opened 3% below its signal close and was stopped the same day.. Test: Over all historical signals, bucket by (next open − signal close) / ATR: < −1, −1 to +1, > +1. Compare forward 20-day returns and stop-out rates per bucket. Also confirm CRWV's Nov 11–12 bars against a second data source to rule out a data error.
- **Check the earnings and news fields that strategies depend on: earnings_reaction, news-to-ticker matching, and sector labels.**. Evidence: PUBM shows an earnings reaction of −10.5% yet +15.7% over 5 days and 1% below its 52-week high. ENR (Energizer) entry news is about Siemens Energy (German ticker ENR), inflating its news score to 16, and its sector is listed as Technology. Last week found stale days_to_earnings_est values too.. Test: Recompute earnings_reaction from daily bars and EDGAR filing dates for every past signal and count mismatches > 3 points. Check news by matching company name to ticker on a sample of 500 headlines and measure the wrong-company rate. Cross-check sector labels against SIC codes. Then rerun earnings_overreaction and earnings_gap_up on the corrected data and compare.
- **Log the initial stop and initial dollar risk for every trade. The current 'stop' field is the final trailed level, so R and stop distance in ATR can't be computed after a trade moves.**. Evidence: For TLS, MRX, DCTH, FROG, IRDM, RCEL and ANF the recorded stop had already trailed up, so their true R multiples are unknown. The earnings_gap_up initial stops (SBC 7.7%, TTEK 4.6%) were clearly not the stated 12% trail.. Test: Add initial_stop, initial_risk_$ and stop_atr_multiple to the trade log. Then rebuild all 18 closed trades from order history and report R per trade and the stop-distance-in-ATR distribution per strategy. Confirm whether earnings_gap_up uses the reaction-day low.
- **Verify that the exposure dial caps total invested capital, and test blocking new entries entirely in Defensive regimes.**. Evidence: 8 positions opened Nov 17–20 while the dial read 0.5, then 0.25. HAWK and VVV were bought Nov 20 at 0.25 with about 15–20 other positions open; their sizing note says 'risk budget', not 'exposure dial'.. Test: Log gross exposure (cost of open positions / equity) before each entry and assert it stays ≤ the dial. Then backtest three policies in Defensive regimes: current, no new entries, and new entries at half the risk budget. Compare drawdown, return, and the forward 20-day return of Defensive-day entries.
- **insider_cluster should exclude insider purchases made at or around an IPO, and should be tested with a trend filter.**. Evidence: HAWK has no rs_rank, trend_score or 200-day data (likely a recent listing) yet shows 8 insider buyers. FUN (trend score 1, 30% below its 200-day average) and ATEC (trend score 0, −35% in 20 days) were bought in downtrends. All are open, so this is untested.. Test: Flag insider buys within 180 days of a listing date, and buys by funds/10% holders vs executives. Split historical insider_cluster signals by these flags and by trend_score (0–1 vs ≥3). Compare 60/120-day forward returns and drawdowns per group.
- **Strategies meant to hold for weeks should require more time before the next earnings report than the current 2-week minimum.**. Evidence: ANF (quality_pullback) was bought with earnings 19 days away, so the planned multi-week hold was capped at about 2 weeks before a forced earnings exit. It lost 8.2% in 8 days.. Test: Backtest quality_pullback with minimum days_to_earnings of 14, 30 and 45. Compare the number of trades, average holding period, average return per trade, and the share of trades ended early by the earnings rule.
