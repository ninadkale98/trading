# Feature requests from the idea lab

Written by Opus each round; the weekly engineer builds them (core/lab/engineer.py) and marks them done.

## Round 1 (2026-11-08)
- [ ] **Backtest results split by exit reason**: relative_strength and earnings_gap_up hold far less time than allowed, but the summary cannot show whether the stop, trailing stop, trend exit or max hold is responsible. This would make exit variants targeted instead of guesswork. *How:* Have the backtester log an exit_reason on every trade, then report count, average hold, average excess and t by reason. Also report what each stopped trade would have earned by max hold. Source: existing backtest engine.
- [ ] **Opportunistic insider flag and insider role**: The notes cite about 0.8%/month for non-routine buys, while our insider count treats routine and non-routine buyers alike. Executive buys may also carry more information than director buys. This could sharpen our only signal with a positive 60-day reading. *How:* From the Form 4 trades already loaded: mark an insider as routine if they traded in the same calendar month in each of the prior 3 years, otherwise opportunistic (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski). Add insider_opp_buyers_90d and insider_ceo_cfo_buy_90d, dated by filing date rather than trade date.
- [ ] **Gross profit to assets, plus asset growth**: gp_yield_trend uses a market-value proxy, while the published measure is gross profit / total assets. Having both would separate the quality effect from the cheapness effect. Asset growth would allow a separate test of aggressive expansion. *How:* Pull total assets from SEC XBRL company facts, using filing dates for point-in-time accuracy. Compute gp_to_assets = trailing 12-month gross profit / total assets and asset_growth_yoy.
- [ ] **Word-list news tone with long history**: The notes point to a week of news tone predicting about 13 weeks of returns, but AI-scored news_score_5d may lack enough history to backtest and could carry look-ahead issues. A fixed word list gives a testable 2019-2025 version. *How:* Score stored headlines with the Loughran-McDonald finance dictionary: (positive - negative) / total words, summed by stock over 5 and 20 trading days and timestamped by publication. Report coverage per year. Source: our news archive or a historical news API such as Alpaca News.
- [ ] **Short interest and days to cover**: Heavy short interest is a known negative signal, and it would show who is on the other side of momentum and insider trades. It could work as a filter on all long strategies. *How:* Use FINRA bi-monthly short interest, available only from its publication date to avoid look-ahead. Compute short_pct_float = shares short / float, and days_to_cover = shares short / 20-day average share volume.
- [ ] **Market regime features**: Momentum strategies can suffer sharp losses around market reversals. The research supports using macro signals to set risk rather than pick stocks, so a regime measure could scale exposure and let us compare results by market condition. *How:* Compute daily from existing data: spy_dist_sma200, breadth_200 = share of eligible stocks with dist_sma200 > 0, and SPY 20-day realized volatility. Add a credit-stress series such as FRED's high-yield spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2), lagged one day.
- [ ] **Analyst estimate revision history**: The notes rate revisions among the stronger signals, and they would be a natural confirmation for surprise_near_high. Our own snapshots only began in Nov 2026, so they cannot support a backtest yet. *How:* From the daily snapshots compute eps_rev_30d = percent change in consensus next-12-month EPS over 30 days, and net_upgrades_30d. For history, evaluate a point-in-time vendor dataset such as Zacks or LSEG I/B/E/S, since revised-after-the-fact data would add look-ahead.

## Round 2 (2026-11-11)
- [ ] **Earnings surprise with full history, needing no analyst estimates (SUE from SEC filings)**: Earnings drift ideas are the priority, but eps_surprise and eps_surprise_px depend on sparse Alpha Vantage estimate history, so every earnings-surprise backtest is starved of trades (surprise_near_high: 50). A standardized surprise built from reported EPS alone covers every filer from 2016 on and is the classic academic measure. *How:* From SEC XBRL company facts (EarningsPerShareDiluted, quarterly), dated by filing date. sue_xbrl = (EPS_q − EPS_{q−4}) ÷ the standard deviation of the last 8 such year-over-year changes; also sue_px = (EPS_q − EPS_{q−4}) ÷ the close before the filing. Also add eps_beat_streak = the number of consecutive quarters with a positive year-over-year EPS change. Source: SEC XBRL frames/companyfacts API (free).
- [ ] **Rule-based history of guidance changes (2016+) from 8-K press releases**: guidance_change and release_tone are probably the strongest earnings signals we have, but they exist only going forward, so no guidance idea can pass a backtest. A keyword-based version is free of the 'AI remembers the future' problem and could be backfilled. *How:* For each 8-K with Item 2.02 and Exhibit 99.1 on EDGAR from 2016 on, take the sentences that contain guidance words (outlook, guidance, expects, full-year) and match 'raise/raising/increase … guidance|outlook' versus 'lower/reduce/withdraw … guidance|outlook'. Output guidance_change_kw (+1/−1/0), dated by the 8-K acceptance time. Check it against the Haiku labels on recent overlapping quarters and report the agreement rate.
- [ ] **Earnings quality: accruals ratio**: Earnings beats driven by accounting accruals rather than cash tend to reverse (Sloan). An accrual filter would let surprise and earnings-momentum strategies skip low-quality beats, a cheap way to sharpen sue_drift_loose and rs_earnings_confirmed. *How:* From SEC XBRL, using filing dates: accruals_ttm = (12-month net income − 12-month operating cash flow) ÷ average total assets. Lower = better quality. Total assets are already needed for the pending gp_to_assets request, so build them together.
- [ ] **Insider buy timing relative to earnings and buy price versus current price**: insider_buys_the_drop needs to know whether a purchase came after the last report and at what price. Insiders buying at prices above today's close (now underwater) may be a stronger 'they still believe it' signal, and buys right after reports are the least routine. *How:* From the Form 4 trades already loaded (filing date for point-in-time): insider_buy_post_earn_30d = the number of distinct insiders who bought within 30 trading days after the last earnings reaction day; insider_avg_buy_px_gap = (dollar-weighted average purchase price over 90 days ÷ close) − 1.
