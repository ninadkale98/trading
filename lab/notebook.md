## User idea (2026-11-09): buy the biggest companies after a news-driven one-day drop
- Idea: when a mega-cap leader (e.g. NVDA on 2026-11-09, −4.3% gap on CEO-scandal news) falls ≥ 4% on company news while
  the market is calm, buy and hold for weeks; "a company this important always comes back".
- Quick event study (Claude, 2026-11-09; daily bars 2016+, top 10 common stocks by 60-day dollar volume point-in-time,
  day ≤ −4% while SPY > −1%; 620 events, 28 stocks): 60 days later avg +3.2% vs SPY but median −0.4% and only 49% beat
  SPY (a few big winners carry the average); 63% fell a further 10%+ within 60 days. Top 50 instead: 60d avg −1.1% vs
  SPY, 40% beat. NVDA alone: +7.1%, 60% beat, but NVDA was the decade's best stock (hindsight).
- To test properly: honest t (overlap), leave-best-year-out, split by news vs no-news drops once 2019+ Haiku labels
  exist (Chan 2003: news-driven moves tend to drift, no-news moves reverse), split by news type (management/legal vs
  earnings/guidance), exit when the thesis breaks (e.g. CEO leaves) vs a fixed stop.
- Follow-up (user, 2026-11-09): "big companies always bounce back after a crash, especially after heavy-volume
  selling" (example QCOM 147 → 170, Jul → Aug 2026). Crash study (one event per stock per 60 days, daily bars 2016+):
  - Any top-50 stock by dollar volume: about a coin flip vs SPY (44–51% beat at 120 days); hype names (COIN, UPST,
    AMC, SE) dominate the losers.
  - **Steadily big only** (top 100 by dollar volume in ≥ 95% of the last 500 days): better. Top 20, ≥ 25% below the
    60-day high with a heavy-volume down day (≥ 2× 60-day average volume) in the last 5 days: 77 events, 120d
    +9.3% vs SPY (median +6.2%, 58% beat). ≥ 35% below: 31 events, 60d +8.8% (65% beat). Without the volume rule:
    weaker (median +0.7%). 2023–2026 events were positive too, but 2021 was −21%.
  - Caveats: 12 variants tried (multiple testing), events cluster in 2020 and 2022 (not independent), ~half fell
    another 15% first, and only ~30% regained the prior high within 6 months.
  - **Test this round as a pre-registered candidate** (`big_crash_capitulation`): steady top 20, ≥ 25% below the
    60-day high, heavy-volume down day within 5 days, hold up to 120 days, wide stop (≥ 25%) or none; honest t,
    leave-best-year-out, ≥ 4 years, out-of-sample 2023+. Variants count against the bar.
    **Features for it (added 2026-11-10):** `dist_60d_high` (≤ −0.25), `heavy_sell_age` (≤ 5 = a heavy-volume down
    day within 5 trading days), `adv_rank` (1 = most traded) and `big_steady_250` (share of the last 250 days in the
    top 100; ≥ 0.95 = steadily big). The study used a 500-day window for "steady"; the feature uses 250 days, so
    "steady top 20" ≈ `big_steady_250 ≥ 0.95` and `adv_rank ≤ 20`. Also new: `eps_surprise_px` (EPS surprise ÷ price,
    stable when the estimate is near zero), a candidate replacement for `eps_surprise` in surprise_near_high.
- Entry timing (user asked: watch the fall closely, buy when it turns): on the same events, waiting was WORSE on daily
  bars. Steady top 20, ≥ 25%, heavy volume, all exits at signal + 120 days: buy at the signal close +9.3% vs SPY
  (median +6.2%, 58% beat); wait for a close above the prior 5 days' high +5.4% (median 0.0%, 51%; worse than buying
  at once in 74% of pairs); wait for +8% off the low +8.4%; turn + volume calming +6.6% but the fewest "fell 15%+
  after entry" (41% vs 47%; 34% vs 43% in the ≥ 15% version). So the turn signal gives up the bounce's fast start
  and barely avoids the losers; volume calming mainly lowers the pain. Lab: test entry A as the main rule, D as a
  lower-drawdown variant. An intraday (15-min) version needs Alpaca minute history around events; only worth it if
  the daily version passes.

## Lab round 2 (2026-11-11)
- big_crash_capitulation: admit: sign of life: 20d: +1.40% vs pool (t_adj 0.97)
- sue_drift_loose: admit: sign of life: 20d: +1.63% vs pool (t_adj 1.59); 60d: +5.96% vs pool (t_adj 3.39); backtest +3.56%/trade vs SPY (t 2.44, +2.25% without its best year)

- insider_buys_the_drop: admit: sign of life: 20d: +0.92% vs pool (t_adj 2.18); 60d: +1.06% vs pool (t_adj 1.69); backtest +1.87%/trade vs SPY (t 1.43, +0.56% without its best year)
- Lesson: Pre-registered kill rules must be enforced at admission. A 20-day signal-study t_adj near 1 should not override a written 'PROVEN WRONG' rule that has been met. *(evidence: big_crash_capitulation met its kill rules (a), (b) and (c): twin excess -1.15% (t -0.30), SPY excess -3.59%, only 3 positive years. rs_earnings_confirmed met its rule: twin excess +0.37% vs the parent's +1.14%, t 0.64. Both were admitted on 'sign of life' (20d t_adj 0.97 and 0.66).; test: Add a gate in lab.cycle: admission requires none of the machine-checkable pre-registered kill rules to be met (twin t, leave-best-year-out sign, minimum trades). Re-run round 2 admission under it; expected result is big_crash_capitulation and rs_earnings_confirmed rejected.)*
- Lesson: Crash-buying big steady stocks did not reproduce the earlier event study. The raw gains were market beta, not an edge. *(evidence: The pre-test event study showed +9.3% vs SPY at 120d over 12 variants. The backtest showed -3.59%/trade vs SPY on 57 trades. 2020 avg raw return was +29% but excess only +2.6%. 2022 had 20 trades at -9.2% excess. The 60d signal study was -3.17% vs pool.; test: Run the signal study at 120d (the actual hold) and on 2023+ data. If excess vs SPY is <= 0 in either, retire big_crash_capitulation and record that the 12-variant event study was overfit.)*
- Lesson: Loosening the earnings-drift filters kept the edge, so surprise_near_high's result is less likely to be filter-fitting. It still lacks a t >= 2 win over its random twin. *(evidence: sue_drift_loose: 88 trades, +3.56%/trade vs SPY (t 2.44, above the 2.33 multiple-testing bar), +2.25% without its best year, 60d signal +5.96% vs pool (t_adj 3.39), positive in 5/5 qualifying years, max drawdown -6.2%. But vs twin: +2.99%, t 1.62.; test: Report the pre-registered score top-third vs bottom-third, run 2023+ out-of-sample, and measure what share of its trades overlap with surprise_near_high. Keep it only if 2023+ excess vs SPY > 0 and the top third beats the bottom third.)*
- Lesson: For relative_strength-style entries, the tight 2.5×ATR stop decides the outcome. Most trades are stopped out long before the 60-day horizon where the signal shows up. *(evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed: 515 of 786 exits were stops (+49 stop_gap), win rate 35%, avg hold 28 days of 120 allowed, max drawdown -40%. Its 60d signal study (no stops) showed +1.11% vs pool.; test: Re-run the same entries with only a 20% closing stop (no ATR stop or trend-break exit) and a 60-day time exit. Compare twin excess and t with the current exits; if the looser exit is better, test the same change on the parent relative_strength.)*
- Lesson: Raw t-stats are badly inflated for signals that fire on many stocks the same day. Only the overlap-adjusted t should be quoted. *(evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed 60d vs pool: raw t 4.04 but t_adj 0.91 (28,728 signals on only 1,437 days). The 20d raw t was 2.09 vs t_adj 0.66.; test: In every future signal study, flag any idea where signals/signal_days > 5 and require t_adj (not raw t) >= 2 for any claim. Check that round-3 reports never cite raw t for such ideas.)*
- Lesson: Beating the pool is not beating SPY. In 2016–2022 the average universe stock lagged the large-cap index, so a 'vs pool' edge can disappear against SPY. *(evidence: rs_earnings_confirmed 60d: +1.11% vs pool but only +0.13% vs SPY. big_crash_capitulation 20d: +1.40% vs pool but -0.10% vs SPY.; test: Make admission's 'sign of life' require positive excess vs both pool and SPY at the same horizon. Count how many round 1–2 admissions would have been blocked.)*
- Lesson: Insider buying after a bad earnings reaction shows a small positive effect, but it rests mostly on rebound years and may just be generic dip-buying. *(evidence: insider_buys_the_drop: 421 trades, +1.87%/trade vs SPY (t 1.43), +1.38% vs twin (t 0.98), +0.56% without best year, positive in 4/7 years with best years 2016 and 2020 (rebounds). 20d signal t_adj 2.18. Max drawdown -35%.; test: Report the pre-registered split by reaction size (<= -10% vs -5% to -10%), and run a control with the same earnings-drop trigger but no insider condition. Keep the insider condition only if it beats the control vs twin with t >= 2.)*
- Lesson: The pipeline does not compute idea-specific pre-registered diagnostics, so ideas are written with kill rules that never get checked. *(evidence: None of these were reported: the 2023+ out-of-sample check (big_crash rule d), score terciles (sue_drift_loose), the excluded-trades comparison (rs_earnings_confirmed), the reaction-size split (insider_buys_the_drop). validate was null for all four ideas.; test: Either extend the backtest output with a standard set (2023+ out-of-sample, score terciles, leave-best-year-out vs twin), or have the idea writer only use kill rules built from fields the pipeline reports. Round 3 check: every kill rule has a reported value.)*
- Lesson: Running a parent and its variant live together doubles exposure to the same trades and blurs the live comparison. *(evidence: Live now: surprise_near_high + sue_drift_loose, relative_strength + rs_earnings_confirmed, insider_cluster + insider_buys_the_drop. 12 live strategies, 0 retired this round.; test: Measure the share of same-stock, same-week entries between each parent/variant pair in the dry-run book after 4 weeks. If overlap > 30%, keep only the pair member with the higher twin t.)*

## Week of 2026-11-09: trade review
- The earnings-date check must treat a stale estimate (days_to_earnings_est < 0 while days_since_earnings is ~60+) as 'report imminent or unknown' and block entry. *(evidence: SLN entered Nov 11 with days_to_earnings_est = -1 and days_since_earnings = 65. The report came the next day, forcing a 1-day 'sell before earnings' exit. DCTH and TLS also showed days_to_earnings_est = 0 after they had already reported, so the field is not always rolled forward.; test: Over the full signal history, count signals with days_to_earnings_est <= 0 and days_since_earnings > 50, and how many had an actual report within 5 trading days. Then rerun all 'no earnings due' strategies with the corrected check and compare how many trades exit early for earnings and their average return.)*
- Exclude pending-acquisition targets (prices pinned near a deal price) from accumulation and momentum strategies, since their upside is capped and the deal-day volume fakes the accumulation score. *(evidence: SLN: Merck KGaA acquisition, +123% in 20 days, then +0.05% over 5 days, ATR 2.4%, volume 0.14x normal. The accumulation score of 0.60 came from the deal-day volume. The 'up <40% in 3 months' filter missed it because the 60-day return was only +38%.; test: Flag past accumulation and relative_strength signals where the stock had a single-day gain over 25% in the last 30 days followed by a 10-day ATR below 1/3 of its prior level (or a merger_acquisition news tag naming it as target). Compare their forward 20/60-day returns and max gains with unflagged signals.)*
- return_to_average should probably not buy dips that are the earnings reaction itself (post-earnings drift tends to continue), and should require a real margin above the 200-day average. *(evidence: CLMT dropped 9% on earnings day (days_since_earnings 0) and was only 1.3% above its 200-day average. It was stopped at -1R in 4 days and ended 13% below that average. Open trades UFCS (+8.9% best) and TEAM (flat) have the same post-earnings setup, so the evidence is mixed.; test: Backtest return_to_average split by days_since_earnings <= 5 vs > 5, and by dist_sma200 < 5% vs >= 5%. Compare win rate, average return per trade, and stop-out rate in each bucket.)*
- Fixed-% stops are below 2 ATR on volatile stocks, so ordinary noise can hit them. Test volatility-scaled stops with position size adjusted to keep dollar risk constant. *(evidence: ZIP (12% stop vs 7.2% ATR = 1.7 ATR), CLMT (1.6 ATR) and MRNA (1.8 ATR) were all stopped within 1-4 days. Open RCEL's stop is 1.8 ATR and its worst point (-10.2%) is close to it. All losers had small best points (<= 4%), so this is a hypothesis, not proof.; test: For earnings_gap_up, relative_strength and return_to_average, backtest initial stops of max(fixed %, k x ATR) for k = 2, 2.5, 3, scaling shares so planned dollar risk is unchanged. Compare average return per trade (in R), stop-out rate in the first 5 days, and max drawdown against the current fixed-% stops.)*
- Record what price does after every exit, so we can tell 'stop was too tight' apart from 'trade was wrong'. *(evidence: This week we could not tell whether ZIP, CLMT, MRNA or HGTY recovered after being stopped out. The data only has best and worst points while the trade was open, so the stop-tightness question can't be answered.; test: Add post-exit tracking (+5, +10, +20 trading days: close, highest high, lowest low). In a backtest, measure the share of stopped trades that later traded above their entry price within 20 days, by strategy and by stop distance in ATRs.)*
- Stocks bought after a big run or right after earnings may gap through stops more often, making losses bigger than planned. Measure this and, if real, size with a gap allowance. *(evidence: HGTY gapped from a $16.35 stop to a $15.79 fill (-1.31R) on 23x normal volume. MRNA gapped from a $228.65 stop to $224.79 (-1.10R). - relative_strength may need a cap on how stretched a stock can be at entry (distance above its 200-day average, or 60-day return). *(evidence: MRNA: +325% in 60 days, 190% above its 200-day average, ATR 8%, stopped in 3 days at -1.1R.
